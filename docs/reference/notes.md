## What a note is

A note is a **thread** of short plain-text messages on one record: a page, a post, a block or a library image (`Notes.subject_types`: the types of the record kinds, `RecordKinds`, and `ContentBlock`). Five tables, all under `Notes::` (`app/models/notes/`, each with an explicit `table_name`): `note_threads` (`Notes::Thread`: `subject`, `container`, `state` open or resolved with `resolved_at`/`resolved_by_id`, `pinned` with `pinned_at`/`pinned_by_id`, an optional `quote` of at most 500 characters, `origin_view` and `origin_locale`, `created_by_id`, `last_message_at`), `note_messages` (`Notes::Message`: `thread_id` cascading, `author_id` nullified when the user is deleted, `body` of 1 to 4,000 characters, `edited_at`, `mentioned_user_ids` as `bigint[]`, `has_many_attached :files`), `note_deliveries` (`Notes::Delivery`, the inbox: one row per person and message, `reason` one of `mention`, `reply`, `resolved`, `reopened`, `read_at`), `note_follows` (`Notes::Follow`: one row per person and thread, `following` true or false) and `note_reactions` (`Notes::Reaction`: one row per person, message and emoji). Deliveries, follows and reactions cascade from their message, their thread and their user; a deleted user's messages stay and read "a deleted user" (`Admin::Ui::UserNameComponent`). **How a note names a person** is one method, `Notes.name_of`: the name, and for someone without one the part of their email address before the at sign (handoff decision 36). Every place a note draws a person asks it: the components through `Admin::NotesScreen::PersonNameComponent` (a message's author and avatar, a mention's chip, a row's author, who resolved a thread, a reaction's hover title, "Open threads"), `Notes::Mentions.display` (the edit form and the inbox excerpt), the inbox sentence and the toast, the mind graph card's rows, and the mention picker, whose second line is still the whole address. `User#display_name`, which the rest of CS Studio uses, is unchanged (`test/integration/admin/note_names_test.rb`). The first message of a thread is the note that started it, and a thread without a message does not exist: `Notes::DeleteMessage` deletes the thread with its last one.

**Subject and container.** `subject` is what the thread is about. `container` is the record whose screen lists it and whose permissions it follows: for a block, the block's page; for anything else the subject itself (`Notes.container_for`). The container is derived by the model before every validation and never taken from a caller: a thread filed under a page its block does not belong to would be readable by the wrong people. One query on `container` therefore reads everything a page's drawer shows, and a block that changes container inside its page (into, out of or across a section) keeps its threads with nothing to update. Both references are polymorphic, so there is no foreign key and no `has_many` on the content side: `Notes::Remove` is what deletes a record's threads (below), and a trashed record keeps them, hidden with it by the read scope. `Notes.key(record)` is a record's name in a DOM id, a `data-` attribute and the counts payload: the class name without colons, a dash, the id (`Page-3`, `ContentBlock-12`, `MediaAsset-5`).

**Text and mentions.** A message is plain text (`Notes::Text.parts`: text, a mention, a bare `http(s)` URL that `RichText::BuiltIns.safe_href?` accepts; nothing else is recognised, no Markdown and no HTML, and `Admin::NotesScreen::BodyComponent` escapes the rest). A mention is stored as the token `@[<user id>]` (`Notes::Mentions::TOKEN`, at most 18 digits so that every match fits a bigint: an unbounded number passed validation and raised a `RangeError` on save), so renaming a user changes how old mentions read. `mentioned_user_ids` is read from the body before every validation, so the column can't say what the text does not. A textarea can't show a name for a token, so the composer shows "@Dana Smith" and sends beside the text a `mention_names` map of the names its picker inserted to their ids; `Admin::NoteContainer#note_body` turns names into tokens (`Mentions.tokenize`, longest name first) before the service sees the body, and `Mentions.display` is the way back for the edit form. The map comes from the browser and is trusted for that substitution only: who may be mentioned is the service's check on the ids that result. A message mentions at most 20 people (`Mentions::MAX`), refused from the text alone before anyone is looked up.

## Who may do what

Notes follow the content (decision D3); there is no permission of their own. The registry `RecordKinds` (`app/models/record_kinds.rb`: page, post and media, each with its model, its read right, its moderate right and whether its rights are per record, `scoped`) names the container's keys, through `Notes.read_key` and `Notes.moderate_key`: reading, starting, replying, reacting, resolving, reopening and following need `pages.read` / `posts.read` on the page or post, or `media.upload` for an image (what opens the library; there is no per-image read right); deleting someone else's message, pinning and unpinning need `pages.edit` / `posts.edit`, or `media.manage`, or for an image's uploader `media.upload` alone (`Media::Ownership`, as for the image itself). Moderation needs `edit`, not `edit` plus `publish`: a note changes nothing a visitor sees. `Notes.readable?` and `Notes.moderator?` are those rules for a component deciding what to draw; the services ask through `authorize!`. Editing a message is its author's alone, while they may still read the record: a moderator may delete a note, never put words in its author's mouth (`Notes::EditMessage` records the refusal as `access.denied` against the read key with its own sentence, like `Users::UpdateProfile`'s "only yourself" rule). So a view-only reader of a page writes notes on it, replies and resolves, and can change nothing else (`test/system/admin/notes_flow_test.rb`, `notes_view_only_test.rb`).

**Lookups.** `Admin::NoteContainer` (`app/controllers/concerns/admin/`) is what every notes controller includes: it finds the container first, through the read scope (`RecordKinds::Kind#find`: `AccessControl.scope` for a page or post, every kept image for a holder of `media.upload`; the controller adds the one 403, for an unscoped kind whose right the viewer lacks), and everything else is looked up among `container_threads` only. **A thread is never found by its id alone**, nor a message, a reaction's message or a file. A page or post outside the viewer's scope, a trashed one and an id that names nothing are the same 404; an image for someone without `media.upload` is the Media module's 403, as on `Admin::MediaController` (`test/integration/admin/notes_test.rb`, "outside the read scope every part is not found, never forbidden"). A thread that is not among the container's (deleted since the link was made, its block deleted, a thread of another record) is the one thing forgiven: a GET of it answers a 303 to the list with "This note is gone…" (`gone!`), because that GET is the `src` of a lazy frame, where a 404 is "Content missing" and an error in the console (`test/integration/admin/note_counts_test.rb`, "only the thread's absence is forgiven…").

**The mention picker** (`Admin::NoteMentionsController`) lists the users who may read the record, by the name a note gives them (`Notes.name_of`) with the whole email address as the hint, always. It is the second place CS Studio lists users to someone without `users.manage` and, like `Admin::UserSuggestionsController`, deliberately not a directory: six rows at most, only for a record the asker may read. The picker opens on "@" alone, so an empty query answers the first six readers. The read right has no SQL form across users, so the 40 best matches are judged one by one (`CANDIDATES`). A mention the service refuses is refused with **one sentence that names nobody**, the same for one id or several and for an id that belongs to no user (`Authorization#mention_errors`): the token can be typed by hand, so a sentence with a name in it would let anyone who may read one page list the names of users who can't, and learn which ids exist, by sending `@[1] @[2] …`.

## The services

Twelve `ApplicationService`s in `app/services/notes/`, each in `EXPECTED_SERVICES` with a factory:

- `Notes::Start` creates a thread with its first message on a subject (optional quote, files, origin view and language).
- `Notes::Reply` adds a message; a reply to a resolved thread reopens it (the log gets `note.replied` and `note.reopened`, the followers the `reply` row only).
- `Notes::EditMessage` changes the author's own text, sets `edited_at`, and tells only the people the edit newly mentions.
- `Notes::DeleteMessage` deletes a message with its deliveries, reactions and files; the thread goes with its last message.
- `Notes::Resolve` / `Notes::Reopen` change the state. Resolving an already resolved thread succeeds with no event and no second round of deliveries. Resolving unpins: a pinned thread is always open.
- `Notes::Pin` / `Notes::Unpin`: moderation. A resolved thread can't be pinned.
- `Notes::SetFollowing` is "Notify me" / "Stop notifying me". The spec calls it `Notes::Follow`; that constant is the record, so the service has this name.
- `Notes::React` gives or takes back the actor's reaction. It takes the intended state (`on:`), so a POST sent twice, or a press in a tab that had not seen the reaction given in another, ends where the person meant. Only an emoji of `Notes::EMOJI`. A resolved thread takes no reaction and gives none back.
- `Notes::MarkRead` marks the actor's own deliveries read: one thread's, one record's, or all ("Mark all read"). It writes `read_at` only.
- `Notes::Remove` deletes every thread of a record that is being deleted for good ("Removal" below). Not called by a person.

There is no `Users::UpdateNotifications`: the two note settings are written by `Users::UpdateProfile` with the name (`docs/reference/users-groups-tokens.md`).

**A note never writes the record it is on.** No `touch`, no `updated_at`, no `PublicSite.bump_version!`. An autosave base is therefore never moved by a note, and a note neither waits for nor trips a stale-save check (`test/services/notes/start_test.rb`, "a note writes nothing of the record it is on"; in a browser, `test/system/admin/notes_flow_test.rb`, "a note sent while an autosave is on the wire goes after it, once, and moves no base").

**The lock order.** `Notes::Authorization#lock_container` is the first statement of every notes transaction: the container's row lock, read through `kept`, so a record trashed or purged meanwhile reads as gone. It is the same first lock every write to a page's blocks takes (`ContentBlocks::Structure#lock_owner`; `docs/reference/content-blocks-and-types.md`, "The lock order"), so a note and a block's delete run one after the other, never interleaved. `Notes::Start` then looks a block up among that page's; every service that acts on an existing thread re-reads and locks the thread under that lock (`with_locked_thread`; `Reply`, `EditMessage`, `DeleteMessage` and `MarkRead` with a record do the same by hand). A trashed or purged record, a thread removed a moment ago and a message deleted in another tab are all the ordinary failure "This note is gone: it was deleted, or what it was on was." (`Notes::Start` names its subject instead: "This block was deleted."), with nothing written and nothing signalled. A request can also carry a thread loaded a moment before a purge committed, whose `container` then reads nil: `read_refusal` and `moderation_refusal` raise `ContainerGone`, and `AnswersGone`, prepended to every service that includes the module, turns it into the same failure; it was a `NoMethodError` out of the permission lookup, a 500, before that. One consequence was ruled on purpose: you can't delete your own note on a trashed record until it is restored, because that removed the one notes write that could run unlocked beside `Trash::Purge`. "Mark all read" names no record and takes no lock: it is one UPDATE of the actor's own rows. The tests are statement order only (`capture_sql`), no two-connection test: `test/services/notes/start_test.rb` ("the page's row is locked before the block is looked up and the thread written"), `thread_state_test.rb` ("each of them locks the page's row before it reads or writes a notes row"), `delete_message_test.rb`, `react_test.rb`, `mark_read_test.rb`, and `stale_thread_test.rb` for the nil container.

**Following and deliveries** (`Notes::Deliveries`, called inside the action's transaction). A message's author and everyone it mentions follow the thread: a missing `note_follows` row is created; an author's explicit unfollow (`following: false`) is left alone, because writing again is not asking to be told again; a mentioned person's unfollow is undone, because the mention is addressed to them. A new message tells everyone it mentions (`mention`) and every follower (`reply`), a mention winning over a reply; an edit tells only the newly mentioned; a resolve or a reopen tells every follower (`resolved`, `reopened`) on the thread's **first** message. Never the person who did it, and at most one row per person and message (the unique index). Those two rules meet: someone mentioned in the first message already has a row for it, and so does every follower after the first resolve. **The row is reused**: it takes the new reason and is unread again (`Deliveries.write`, `find_or_initialize_by`), since the newest thing to tell that person about that message is what just happened to the thread. That is why the inbox orders and dates by a delivery's `updated_at` (`Delivery.inbox_order`), never `created_at`, under which the news of a resolve would be buried beneath everything written since the mention; and why `Notes::MarkRead` leaves `updated_at` alone, since reading is not news. A follower who lost the right to read the record still gets the row; the inbox does not show it (`Notes.readable_threads`). A delivery is read when its owner opens the thread: `Admin::NoteThreadsController#show` calls `Notes::MarkRead` for that thread, and only when one indexed EXISTS says there is a row to mark, so a thread opened with nothing unread takes no lock on its page. Reading the list marks nothing: a row says "new" until its thread is opened.

**Audit.** The recorded actions are `note.started`, `note.replied`, `note.edited`, `note.deleted`, `note.resolved`, `note.reopened`, `note.pinned`, `note.unpinned` and `notes.removed`. An event's subject is the **content record** the thread is on (page, post, block, image), so it shows in that record's Activity and is read-scoped like it; `snapshot: nil`; metadata holds `thread_id` and `message_id` only (`Authorization#note_metadata`), `thread_ids` for `notes.removed`. **A note's text, its quote and its files' names are in no event** (decision D14): the log is append-only, so a deleted note's text would otherwise stay readable for ever. They are in no log line either: `config/initializers/filter_parameter_logging.rb` masks `body`, `quote` and an uploaded file's name **for a request to a notes controller** and only there (a bare `:body` would also have dropped a post's and a block's body from their audit snapshots, which `Audit::Snapshot.scrub` filters by the same list), `mention_names` everywhere, and the passage a new note quotes travels in a URL as `note_quote`, a bare filter entry, never as `quote`: the first line of a request's log filters the query string with nothing to say which controller it is for (`test/integration/admin/note_writes_test.rb`, "a posted note's text, quote and picked names are filtered…", "the quote a GET carries is note_quote…"; `test/integration/admin/note_files_test.rb`, "no notes request logs a file's name…"). A refusal (`access.denied`) names the container, the record whose right was missing. `Notes::React`, `Notes::MarkRead` and `Notes::SetFollowing` say `# audit: none (…)`: a reaction, a read mark and a follow are not changes to content. `test/services/service_behaviour_contract_test.rb` requires every allowed call to record an event, so their factories carry `control_unaudited: "<reason>"`, under which the control run must record **nothing**. `Audit::Sentence::NOTE_VERBS` words the nine actions ("Dana started a note on Pricing"; `test/models/audit/sentence_test.rb` fails in both directions). `Dashboard::Feed::NOT_A_CHANGE` is `access.denied`, the three take-over actions of live editing (`page.taken_over`, `post.taken_over`, `block.taken_over`) and those keys: "Mine" on the dashboard and "Changed by" in the editors (`Audit::ChangeSince`) ignore them, because a note is not a change to the record. `Dashboard::Activity` lists `note.started` and `note.resolved` only, so replies don't flood the feed.

## Removal

A trashed record keeps its notes and gets them back on restore. Whoever deletes a page, a post, an image or a block **for good** calls `Notes::Remove.call(record, with: [...])` inside its transaction, because a polymorphic subject has no foreign key to do it:

- `ContentBlocks::Destroy`, for the block and, for a section deleted with its blocks, each of them (`with:`), **after** the block is gone and under the page's lock: a block's right is its page's, and the page is still there (`test/services/content_blocks/destroy_test.rb`, "a block's notes are removed after the block is deleted, under the page's lock"). "Remove section, keep blocks" removes nothing: a lifted block's container is still its page.
- `Trash::Purge` of a page, once for the page with its whole branch and all their blocks, **before** anything is destroyed. `Notes::Remove` asks the purge's own right again, and a `pages.delete` scoped to a branch is matched against the page's closure rows, which the destroy deletes. The object in hand still answers afterwards, but only because Active Record keeps the ancestor ids it read for the first check; a page read afresh is refused. Asked first, the answer depends on neither (`test/services/trash/purge_test.rb`, "the notes are removed before the first page of the branch is deleted", "someone whose delete right reaches one branch only purges a page of it, notes and all").
- `Trash::Purge` of a post or an image, **after** the destroy and with no lock taken for it. The destroy's DELETE holds the record's row to the commit, and that row is the first lock every notes service takes, so a note started beside the purge either committed before and is found, or waits on the row and then finds none. A row lock taken *before* the destroy does the same and was built first; it turned the purge's order round to the post's row and then its join rows, while `Posts::Save` writes `post_tags` first and the post after, so a save that changes tags beside a purge of the same post could deadlock, and nothing rescues `ActiveRecord::Deadlocked` (`test/services/trash/purge_test.rb`, "a post and an image are deleted before their notes are touched, and the purge locks neither row itself"). One deadlock of that family is still open and is older than notes: `Posts::Save` and `Tags::Merge` take a post's row and its join rows in opposite orders, so the purge of a trashed post that carries a tag cannot agree with both (a roadmap row).

The lock is the caller's: `Notes::Remove` takes none before it reads, because its callers hold the row of every container whose threads go (it says so in its class body, `# lock: none (…)`, which is what `test/lib/notes_guards_test.rb` asks of a notes service without the call). It costs the same statements whatever the number of threads and files: one read of the thread ids, one of the people told, one audit insert (`notes.removed`, on the record, only when something was removed), the files (`Notes::Files.remove`), one DELETE of `note_threads` whose cascade takes the rest (`test/services/notes/remove_test.rb`, "it costs the same statements for one thread as for many"). It authorizes what its callers already did (the item's delete right or `trash.empty`; a block: its page's edit right), so nothing else can call it to wipe notes it may not moderate.

Those two call sites are the only references to `Notes::` from the content side. `test/lib/notes_boundary_test.rb` reads the content side (its `CONTENT_SOURCES`: the page, post, block and media models and the model concerns, the block and content type definitions, and the services under `pages/`, `posts/`, `content_blocks/`, `media/`, `trash/`, `tags/`, `translations/` and `starter_contents/`) and fails on any other reference to a constant or a method of `Notes`, with an allow-list that names the two files, lets them name nothing but `Remove`, and fails on an entry that matches nothing.

## The frame

The whole notes UI is one `<turbo-frame id="notes">` with two views under that one id: the **list** (`admin/notes/index`, `Admin::NotesScreen::ListComponent`) and one **thread** (`admin/notes/thread`, `ThreadComponent`), which replaces it. The editors embed it in the drawer, the Media panel embeds it inline, and `/notes/<page|post|media>/<id>` shows it as a page. Every part has a stable id and its own GET, which is what live delivery fetches again:

| Part | DOM id | Fetched again by |
|---|---|---|
| The notes frame | `turbo-frame#notes` | `GET admin_record_notes_path(type, id)` |
| The list view | `#notes-list[data-notes-view=list]` | the same |
| One group of threads | `#notes-group-<key>` | the same |
| One thread's row | `a#note-row-<thread id>` | the list |
| The thread view | `#note-thread-<id>[data-notes-view=thread]`, its parts `#note-thread-<id>-messages` and `#note-thread-<id>-reply` (`[data-note-part]`) | `GET admin_record_note_thread_path(type, id, thread)` |
| One message | `#note-message-<message id>` | its thread |
| The resolved threads | `turbo-frame#notes-resolved` | `GET admin_resolved_record_notes_path(type, id)` |
| The counts of one record | `#notes-counts` | `GET admin_record_note_counts_path(type, id)` (JSON) |
| A count marker | `#notes-marker-<key>` | filled from the counts |
| The header's button and its count | `#notes-open`, `#notes-open-count` | filled from the counts |
| The drawer | `aside#notes-drawer` | not fetched: it holds the frame |
| The sidebar's unread count | `#sidebar-badge-dashboard` | `GET admin_unread_notes_path` (JSON) |
| "Notes for you" | `turbo-frame#notes-for-you` | `GET admin_notes_card_path` |
| One delivery, as data | (the toast) | `GET admin_note_delivery_path(id)` (JSON) |
| "Open threads" | `turbo-frame#notes-open-threads` | `GET admin_open_note_threads_path` |

`type` is `page`, `post` or `media`. Further routes under the record: `mentions` (JSON), `threads` as JSON for the mind graph's card (`admin_record_note_threads_data_path`), `POST threads`, `POST threads/:id/:change` (resolve, reopen, pin, unpin, follow, unfollow: six segments, each with its literal, so nothing a request sends is handed to a service as a flag), the messages' create, update and destroy, a message's `reactions`, and a message's `files/:id`.

**The list** (`Notes::Listing`): pinned threads first, then open threads grouped by what they are on, the record itself first and then one group per block in the order the page shows its blocks (`BlockOutline#reading_order`), then "Resolved (N)", collapsed and loaded when opened. 50 open threads are listed and "Show more" asks for 50 more; the counts are exact whatever the limit. A group's heading (`Notes::Heading`, worded by `SubjectComponent`) names a block by its type, its column inside a section and something that tells it from the others, since a block has no name in this CMS: the first six words of its text in the language being read, an image's file name, or "Block 3", its place on the page. A row is the first message's author, time and first lines, the reply count and "new" while it holds an unread delivery for the viewer. The new-note field is drawn for `?compose=<key>` (`#notes-new`), not as a third view, so a failed submit and a live refresh stay simple.

**The thread**: row 1 is "← All notes N", "Notify me" / "Stop notifying me" and Close; row 2 what the thread is on, with Pin for someone who may pin; a line saying "Pinned" or "Resolved by …"; the quote (`QuoteComponent`, the one drawing of it, the new-note field's too); the messages (more than three: the first and the last two with "Show N more"); and, at the bottom, the reply's composer with Resolve (Reopen on a resolved thread) beside Send. **In the drawer and the sheet the two rows stay and only the messages scroll** (handoff decision 35): the rows are `[data-note-rows]`, what follows them is `[data-note-scroll]`, both still inside the `messages` part, and the stylesheet makes the second a scroller of its own there (`.admin-notes-drawer`), while the drawer's body, which the list scrolls in, does not scroll under a thread. In the media panel and on a record's notes page the same thread is in the flow of what scrolls there. Whoever needs "what scrolls this view" asks `scrollerOf` of `notes/merge`, which reads the computed `overflow-y`; the messages' scroller is replaced with its part by every refresh, so its position is read before and put back after, the "1 new message ↓" button listens for scrolls on the document in the capture phase, and a redraw by Turbo itself (Edit, Delete, Cancel) has the place put back inside the render (`keepPlace`). A refusal is said where its button is: a reply's, and Resolve's or Reopen's, in the composer; Pin's and Notify's under the two rows and above what scrolls, in the warn alert (`ThreadComponent::REFUSAL_CLASS`, with `border border-warn` as every warn alert has, decision 44; 13px, 8px in from the drawer's edges) with a × (handoff decision 40; `admin--notes--refusal`). The person's next action in the thread redraws that part and so clears it; a refresh for someone else's change carries the alert over. When the sentence is "This note is gone…" the frame goes back to the list four seconds after the alert first appeared; the timer goes with the element, so it never fires into what the person opened meanwhile (`test/system/admin/note_thread_answers_test.rb`, "a refused Pin is said in the warn alert…", "a Pin refused because the note is gone takes the drawer back to the list after four seconds", "the timer of a gone refusal is cleared…"). Resolve is a button inside the composer that names an empty form of its own beside it (`form=`), because forms can't nest. A message's Edit and Delete are in its "⋯" menu and are links back to the thread with `?edit=<id>` or `?delete=<id>`, which draws that one message's edit form or its "Delete this note?" in place.

**Answers.** Every write answers the frame: a 303 to the view the person should now see, or the same view again with its status (403, 422), the reason and what was typed (`failed_form`; nothing there reads `result.record`, a notes failure has none). A thread that went while the service ran is the list with the sentence, never a 404 inside the drawer (`answer_thread_failure`). The file names a refused form was sent with are named back ("Choose the files again: …"), since a browser never gives a file field its files back. **A reply to a thread that is gone keeps its text**: the 303 to the list lost it (the list was drawn over the field and the composer took the redirect for a note that went through), so that one answer is the list itself, at 422, with "This note is gone…" and what was typed in the field for a new note on the record (`Admin::NoteContainer#reply_gone!`); a reply with neither text nor files is still the 303.

**A GET that writes.** Opening a thread is what reads it, so that GET changes the viewer's own read marks. It must not be fetched by anything but a person opening the thread: **every link into the notes frame carries `data-turbo-prefetch="false"`** (a thread's row, "All notes", the "⋯" menu's Edit and Delete, "New note", "Show more"), or Turbo's hover prefetch would mark a row read that nobody opened; a new link to that action needs the same attribute. Where it matters: both editors and the Media screen switch prefetch off for the whole page (`<meta name="turbo-prefetch" content="false">` in their views), so in the drawer and the media panel the attribute is a second line; on a record's notes page (`/notes/<type>/<id>`), which has no such meta, it is the only one (`test/lib/notes_guards_test.rb`, "every link into the notes frame switches prefetch off", a scan of the notes components and views; `test/system/admin/note_thread_answers_test.rb`, "no link in the thread view is prefetched").

**Counts.** `Notes::Counts.for_container` answers one record's screen: `{ total, subjects: { "<key>": n }, unread: [ "<key>" ] }`, the open threads per subject from one grouped query and, beside it, the keys whose open threads hold a delivery the viewer has not read. A resolved thread is not counted; a pinned one is. "Unread for you" is the one meaning accent has in a marker (a dot, a pill, a badge, the NEW divider): a count turns accent when the viewer has an unread delivery on a thread it counts. In a note's *text*, accent is the system's link colour as everywhere, and a mention is a small chip: ink on the sunken surface for anyone else, accent on the accent tint when it is the viewer who is mentioned. Lists read `Counts.by_container` and `Counts.unread_among`, one query each per surface, over ids the caller has already narrowed through the read scope: `Notes::Counts` hides nothing itself. The statement counts are pinned by comparing two runs: `test/integration/admin/notes_test.rb` (the list, a thread, the resolved rows, the number of blocks with notes), `test/integration/admin/note_counts_test.rb` (the posts list, the tree, the media grid), `test/queries/notes/listing_test.rb`.

## The drawer and the markers

**The drawer** (`Admin::NotesScreen::DrawerComponent`, `aside#notes-drawer`, a `complementary` landmark labelled "Notes") is rendered once per editor, outside every autosave form, and holds the frame, whose `src` is set when it first opens. From 1024px it is a 400px **column to the right of the content and below the editor's sticky header**: it takes its place in the page and the editor stays usable beside it; it has a line on its left and no shadow. The first build, following the spec, laid it over the content with the menu shadow and padded the header; a top-level card's "⋯" menu then opened under the drawer, and Claude Design moved it into the flow (handoff decision 1; the spec's §12). The shell's grid has no fourth track: the room is taken out of the content column (`<main>` pads its right side, the header's negative margin grows by the same amount) and the drawer is fixed in it, from the header's bottom edge, which `admin--notes--drawer` measures and keeps current (`--admin-editor-header-height`). `data-notes-open` on `<html>` is what the stylesheet reads; the layout's head script (`admin/shared/_notes_head`) sets it before the first paint from what the browser remembers, so the editor does not change width when the script connects. While the column is there the page tree **stays beside the editor where it fits and folds where it does not** (Claude Design, 2026-10-13; until then it folded at every width from 1024px). It fits while the editor column would still be 640px wide: the modules sidebar (240px, where it is a column) + the tree at the width the person chose for it + the 400px of notes + 640px are no more than the window, which at the default tree width is a window of 1552px (the figures are pixels at a 16px root font and laid out in rem, the notes' 400px excepted). The sidebar is a column from 1280px only, so the sum steps there: a tree of 220 to 239px stays beside the notes at 1260 to 1279px, where the sidebar is a drawer and the editor really has its 640px, and folds from 1280px until the window is 1500px wide. That is the sidebar's breakpoint and not a fault of the rule, which is the editor's true width (`test/system/admin/tree_column_test.rb`: "either side of the sidebar's breakpoint: a 220px tree stays beside the notes at 1270px and folds at 1280px"). A media query cannot read the chosen width, so the sum is done in script and said on `<html>` as `data-tree-fits` (the head script `admin/shared/_tree_head` before the first paint, `admin--shell--tree-width` from then on), and the fold's rules ask for its absence, so without the script the tree folds as before. Studio never narrows the tree to make it fit: it folds at the width it has and is a column of that width again when the notes close; a reset of the width beside the notes is the default, and folds the tree where the default does not fit. Folded, it is the tree drawer the shell already has below 1024px, at the chosen width; what opens it is "Pages ▾", the top bar's up to 1279px and, from 1280px where there is no top bar, a button at the start of the page editor's breadcrumb row (`docs/reference/cs-studio-shell-and-dashboard.md`, "The tree column"; `test/system/admin/tree_column_test.rb`, `test/system/admin/notes_column_test.rb`). Below 1024px the notes are a full-screen sheet under the top bar with "← Back to <title>". While a thread is open the drawer's own header is hidden (the sheet's too) and the thread's row 1 holds Close, or in the sheet the text button "Back to page".

**When to use which.** The shell's left drawers (`admin--shell--drawer`: the sidebar, the page tree) are navigation, modal while open below their breakpoint, with a scrim. The Media panel is a column that *is* the selected image's form. The notes drawer is the one place where something is read and written **beside** an editor that goes on working: not a modal, no scrim, nothing made inert, and Escape closes it only while the focus is inside it. A shell drawer opening closes it and the other way round (`admin--notes--drawer:opened`, `admin--shell--drawer:opened`), except the page tree folded beside the notes column, which is opened from it. ⌘K and dialogs work while it is open.

**`admin--notes--drawer`** lives on `<body>`, like `admin--ui--dialog`: the header's button, the markers on the block cards and the drawer are far apart in the document, and cards come and go by Turbo Stream. It opens and closes the drawer, remembers "open" per browser in `localStorage["cs-notes-open"]` (a convenience: the next editor opens with its notes showing; below 1024px only an explicit ask opens the sheet), keeps the list's scroll position for the way back from a thread, and manages the focus: into the drawer when an opener asked, back to what opened it on close, and never out to the page when the frame's answer removed what held it. A drawer that opens by itself from memory takes no focus. Neither does one whose opener asked, when the person has moved the focus elsewhere before the frame has loaded (`focusStayed`): the view is on screen a few frames before `turbo:frame-load`, and CI's slower browser met a test that clicked into the editor in that gap, had the focus taken into the drawer, and closed it with the Escape meant for the editor (`test/system/admin/notes_drawer_test.rb`, "the focus is not taken into the drawer from someone who moved into the editor before the notes had finished loading"). **The address follows the drawer** (handoff decision 39; `syncAddress`): while it is open the editor's address carries `notes=open`, and `thread=<id>` while a thread is its view; closed, neither. The server opens the drawer for those two parameters, so a reload keeps what was on screen. The entry is replaced, never pushed, so Back still leaves the page; and it is Turbo's entry, so the replacing is done by Turbo's own history (`Turbo.session.history.replace` with the restoration identifier it already has), which keeps `history.state.turbo` and Turbo's record of where the document is. It is written only while the address is this drawer's editor (`data-path` on the drawer): an editor reached by a link into `#content` is drawn, and its remembered drawer opened, a moment before the promoted visit makes the address its own, and `turbo:load` is when that has happened (`test/system/admin/notes_drawer_test.rb`, "the address follows the drawer…", "a remembered drawer puts notes=open into the address of the editor it opened on, not of the page before it").

**The counts are filled by the script.** The server writes one record's counts into the page (`#notes-counts`) and into every answer of the frame (`CountsComponent`); whichever copy arrived last is the truth, and `admin--notes--drawer#refreshCounts` fills the header's number, every marker and the warning in a block's delete dialog from it. **The server never renders a count into a card**: a card is drawn by many streams (an add, a move, a duplicate, a section's lanes), none of which should cost a count query or go stale; a card drawn again connects its marker and is filled like the rest.

**The openers.** The header's "Notes" button with the count (`#notes-open`; `Admin::NotesScreen::HeaderButtonComponent`, which the editors' header draws through the slot `editor.header.actions` and does not name; the tree's and the posts list's pills are `RowPillComponent`, in `tree.row.markers` and `posts.row.markers`, and the Media panel's section `MediaSectionComponent`, in `media.panel.sections`: `docs/reference/presence.md`, "The seams"). A marker in each block card's header and on each tile of a section (`MarkerComponent`, `#notes-marker-<key>`), which the card and the tile no longer draw themselves: it is registered in the slot `editor.block.markers`, after the avatar of whoever is changing the block (`docs/reference/live-editing.md`, "The slot on a block"), and a slot builds it as `record:, label:` where its other callers say `subject:, name:`: with open threads a pill with the count, pressed to open the drawer at that block's group; without, nothing at rest and an add-note control on hover or keyboard focus of the card; and "Add note" is always first in the block's "⋯" menu, so neither touch nor a test depends on hover. A view-only reader's "⋯" menu has "Add note" as its only item. **A section takes no notes of its own on screen**: no marker on its card, no "Add note" in its menu (handoff decision 10: it has no content of its own to discuss); the services were not changed for this. With text selected in a rich-text field, that block's marker reads "Note on selection" and the selected text, cut to 500 characters, becomes the new thread's quote: a copy, shown with the thread, not highlighted in the editor and not tracked when the text changes (decision D4). The selection is noted at `mousedown` on the marker and is good for the click of that same press only. While a thread is the drawer's view, the card or tile of its block carries `data-notes-active` (an accent border).

**The delete warning.** A block's delete dialog says "This block has 2 open notes. They will be deleted with it." when it has any; a section deleted with its blocks counts the notes on the blocks inside. "Remove section, keep blocks" stays instant and says nothing about notes: the blocks keep theirs.

**The composer** (`ComposerComponent`, `admin--notes--composer`): a textarea that grows to 200px, with attach, emoji (the fixed set `Notes::EMOJI`, which the reactions share) and the at-sign. Enter adds a line and Ctrl/⌘+Enter sends, once: a held key and a second press while the first submit is out are ignored. The shortcut is said by the Send button's tooltip and `aria-keyshortcuts`. What is typed is kept per person and per form in `sessionStorage` (`cs-note-draft:<user id>:<key>`; the person is in the key because the storage belongs to the tab, and the next one to sign in there would otherwise get this one's unsent text) and put back when the form is drawn again; it is dropped when the form's own submit succeeds. The key keeps the next person's forms clean, not the storage: the text and the names of chosen files would stay readable in the tab, so **every draft is removed at sign-out** (as the document goes, `pagehide`, so that a "Leave site?" the person answers with Stay costs nothing) and a page loaded for someone else removes what is not theirs (`forgetDrafts`; a session can end without the button). **While a note is on its way its field is read-only** (from the form's `submit`, which comes before the write queue's hold, to `turbo:submit-end`): Turbo took the form's data at the press and the answer empties the field, so anything typed in between was lost. **A send that is still out after 300ms says so** (handoff decision 41): the field's text is dim, the submit reads "Sending…" with `aria-busy`, and the paperclip, the emoji set and the at-sign are disabled (the emoji set is a `<details>`, so it is `inert` and its `<summary>` says `aria-disabled` with the same look as the other two, decision 45); a send answered sooner shows nothing. One method takes all of it back (`settled`): on `turbo:submit-end`, which follows an answer of any status, a network error and a stopped submission alike, and when a form connects that is not sending, because Turbo's snapshot of a record's notes page, taken mid-send, would otherwise hand a read-only "Sending…" form back on Back. **A composer's baseline is the text the server drew** (the field's `defaultValue`), not what the field holds when the controller connects: an open edit is moved into the new messages part by every refresh, the controller connects again, and a baseline read from the field there made the edit look unchanged, so its draft was removed and "Unsaved edit" no longer protected it. An edit that was refused is drawn with the refused text in its field, so the server hands the saved text beside it (`saved`) and the baseline is that. The mind graph card's field keeps a draft too, per person and per post (`graph-Post-<id>`), and is read-only, and after 300ms "Sending…", while its note is sent. **Unsent note text never raises the leave guard**, which is about content (`test/system/admin/notes_flow_test.rb`, "leaving an editor with a half-typed note asks nothing, and an unsaved edit of the page still asks"). One edit form is open at a time; an edit that is left is kept as a draft and the message says "Unsaved edit" in its meta line until the edit is saved or cancelled (`admin--notes--unsaved`).

**Reactions** (`ReactionsComponent`, `admin--notes--reactions`): chips under a message, the viewer's own tinted; a press toggles the viewer's. For a message nobody reacted to, the add control stands in the meta line and shows on hover or focus of the message; "React" is also always in the message's "⋯" menu. A resolved thread shows its chips read-only.

**The mind graph** badges a post that has open threads with a small mono count at the node's top-left and shows them in its card (`mind_graph/card_notes.js`; `docs/reference/cs-studio-shell-and-dashboard.md`, "Mind graph").

## The inbox

`Dashboard::NotesForYou` is one person's inbox: their deliveries on threads they may read **now** (`Notes.readable_threads`, which also hides the trash), unread first and then by when a row last had something to say. A row is who did what, the first 140 characters of the message with mentions as names, what the thread is on, and where it opens as a route *name* with its arguments (`Admin::NotesScreen::InboxRowComponent` words it; the name is resolved by `Admin::NotePathsHelper#note_route_path`, the one place a notes screen does that, against the fixed list `NOTE_ROUTES`: a route name from a query is data, and `public_send` on unchecked data is a method call chosen by data, so a name off the list raises): the record's editor with `?notes=open&thread=<id>`, or for an image the Media screen with that image chosen and the thread in its panel. A reopen's row names nobody ("A note was reopened"): the thread records who resolved it, not who reopened it. A delivery on a record the person has lost the right to, or that is in the trash, is neither listed nor counted, while its row stays and is back the moment the right or the record is (`test/queries/dashboard/notes_for_you_test.rb`; `test/integration/trash_visibility_test.rb`, "a trashed record's notes leave the inbox, the full list and the unread count, and are back when it is restored"). `Dashboard::OpenThreads` is the records the viewer may read that have open threads, one row per record with its count and who wrote last, most recently active first. Both are constant in statements.

`Admin::NoteInboxController` is open to every signed-in user, like the dashboard: the full list at `<admin>/notes` (50 a page), "Mark all read" (`Notes::MarkRead` with no record, which also marks the rows hidden at that moment: a row that comes back into view read is a smaller surprise than a button that says "all" and leaves a count behind), the two dashboard cards as their own frames, the sidebar's number and one delivery as JSON. Nothing there marks one row read: a row is read by opening its thread. The sidebar's Dashboard entry shows the viewer's unread count (`Admin::BaseController#notes_badge_count`, `#sidebar-badge-dashboard`): the one **filled** badge of the sidebar, hidden at zero, and exactly one statement per page load whatever the number of notes (`test/integration/admin/note_inbox_test.rb`).

**A new list or count of notes** narrows through `Notes.readable_threads`, or counts over ids the read scope already narrowed, and belongs in `test/integration/trash_visibility_test.rb` beside "note counts in the posts list, the page tree and the media grid are for kept records only…" and "the inbox, Open threads and the sidebar's count show notes on kept records and never on trashed ones".

## Live delivery

Notes are the first use of Action Cable in the app. **A pushed signal carries a kind and ids, never text, a title or a name** (decision D16): the browser then fetches the named part again through the same scoped GET it uses on load, so a push can never show a note to someone who may not read it, whatever changed since they subscribed.

**What is sent** (`Notes::Signals`, which sends through `Live::Signals`, the one place in the app that broadcasts): to a **person's** stream `{ kind: "delivery", delivery_id, reason }` when a delivery row is written or reused for them, and the payload-free `{ kind: "inbox" }` when their inbox got smaller (rows of theirs were read in another tab, or went with a deleted note); to a **record's** stream `{ kind: "threads", thread_id }` when a thread of that container was started, replied to, edited, deleted, resolved, reopened, pinned, unpinned, reacted to or removed (`Notes::Remove` signals the *page* of a deleted block, whose drawer may be open). `origin`, on a record's signal and on `inbox`, is the opaque id of the browser tab whose request made the change (`X-Notes-Tab`, read into `Current.notes_tab` and echoed only in the shape `Signals::TAB` allows), so that tab does not fetch again what its own answer already showed it. Each is sent once every open transaction has committed (`ActiveRecord.after_all_transactions_commit`): a tab told before the commit would fetch the old rows and nothing would tell it again; a rollback at any level sends nothing. A push is best effort: a broadcast that fails is logged by its kind and the error's *class* (a driver's message can quote what it was sending) and swallowed, because the note is already committed and an exception there would turn a saved note into an error page and stop the callbacks queued behind it in the same commit. `test/models/notes/signals_test.rb` asserts on every broadcast of every notes service that it holds only a kind, ids and a reason, that it is sent after the outermost commit and not at all when rolled back, refused or gone; it fails for a service that has no case there, and for any other broadcast in `app/`.

**Subscribing.** `ApplicationCable::Connection` identifies the user by the very lookup a request uses (`Session.from_cookies`); no session, no connection. `Notes::PersonChannel` takes no parameter and streams for the connection's user. `Notes::RecordChannel` (a `Live::RecordChannel`, which says only `stream_name_for`) takes a type and an id, looks the container up **through the read scope at subscribe time** (`RecordKinds.find`), as `Admin::NoteContainer` does, and rejects alike a record out of scope, in the trash or not there; the stream's name is built from the record that was found, never from what was sent. Every subscription re-reads the user through the session (`ApplicationCable::Channel#subscriber`): the connection's `current_user` is a snapshot from connect, and `AccessControl` reads the owner flag off the object it is handed, so an owner demoted since is judged by their grants and a user deleted since is rejected. A session that ends disconnects that user's sockets (`Session`'s `after_destroy_commit`). A right taken away *without* the session ending is not re-judged for a stream already open: accepted, since the signal says nothing and the fetch it leads to is scoped (a roadmap row).

**The browser**, four files. `app/javascript/live/connection.js` is the connection; `app/javascript/controllers/admin/notes/inbox_controller.js` (`admin--notes--inbox`, on `<body>`) is the person's side: the person's subscription, the sidebar's count, the dashboard's cards, the toasts and their bursts, and what was heard just before a Turbo visit; `app/javascript/controllers/admin/notes/live_controller.js` (`admin--notes--live`, on `<body>`) is the record's side: one subscription per `record` target, the refresh of a list or a thread and the frame merge; `app/javascript/notes/merge.js` is the rule both of those redraws go through. The inbox controller announces each delivery as `admin--notes--inbox:delivery` on `document` and asks the live controller whether the person is watching a thread with a synchronous event (`admin--notes--inbox:watching`, answered in the event's `detail`): neither holds the other. `data-notes-live="connected"` on `<body>` says every subscription of the page has been answered; each controller says how its own stand and `app/javascript/live/state.js` puts the two together, a half that has said nothing counting as not answered, and presence's parts (an element of the page that holds a subscription of its own and is not always there) saying it on their own element with `data-live-part`, which the server renders as `waiting` (`docs/reference/presence.md`, "The seams"). The attribute and `await_notes_live` keep the name they had when only notes spoke. The cable consumer is the one bundled in turbo-rails (`cable.subscribeTo`); no import-map pin was added for it, and only `app/javascript/live/connection.js` asks for it (the connection as a module of its own: the consumer, the pooled subscriptions, the tab's id; the two controllers listen through its `listen`), so a public page opens no socket (`test/integration/public_javascript_test.rb`, `test/integration/admin/note_signals_test.rb`). One person subscription per tab (the inbox controller's) and one record subscription per `record` target (the live controller's): an editor's drawer, the Media panel's notes, the mind graph's card. Subscriptions are pooled per document and released a task late, because unsubscribing and subscribing again to the same identifier in one task is an order Action Cable can't promise across a Turbo visit.

**What is read is replaced; what is typed is never touched.** The rule has one owner, `app/javascript/notes/merge.js` (`mergeList`, `mergeThread`, `mergeGone`; its own tests are `test/system/admin/notes_merge_test.rb`), which the controller calls for a live refresh and for the frame merge below alike. In the list everything is replaced *around* a new-note field that is on screen. In a thread the `messages` part is replaced, while a message with an open edit form or an open "Delete this note?" stays the element it is; the `reply` part is never replaced, only its Resolve / Reopen button and the empty form that button sends are swapped when the state changed. A composer that holds chosen files and no text counts as typed: a file field's files cannot be put back by a script. A message's files stay the very elements they were, so a picture is not fetched again and a playing GIF goes on playing. Whatever had the focus has it afterwards, and the scroll position stays unless the reader was at the bottom. Messages that arrived are set off by a "NEW" divider; a reader who is not at the bottom gets "1 new message ↓" and nothing moves. A thread that is gone while its reply holds text keeps the text, with "This thread was deleted" above the field and everything that would send it switched off. Signals in a burst are one fetch per part per 500 ms, one request per part out at a time. After the socket was down, each part is fetched once. A refresh is the controller's own `fetch`, no Turbo request: it is never held by the write queue and never enters it (`test/system/admin/notes_live_test.rb`).

**The frame merge.** The answer to one of this tab's own buttons (a reaction, Pin, Notify, Resolve or Reopen, a reply) is the whole thread view, and Turbo would draw it over the frame: an edit form open on another message closed and lost its text. So when a form of the thread was sent and the answer is the same thread's view, it is merged exactly as a live refresh is (`turbo:before-frame-render` on the notes frame). The first build of the merge could still lose the text in one case: a prefetch of a link in the frame (a pointer resting on "All notes" for about 100 ms) cleared the note of what had been sent, and the answer was drawn in full. It was found in review and reproduced by dispatching a prefetch's own event; in the drawer and the media panel a person could not have caused it, since those screens switch prefetch off for the page, but on a record's notes page they could. A prefetch is ignored by the merge now, and that is the second reason no link into the notes frame is prefetched (`test/system/admin/note_thread_answers_test.rb`, "a link prefetched while Pin's answer is on its way does not cost the open edit its text"). **An older live answer is never drawn over the tab's own newer one**: a live GET that left before the person's reply was saved and comes back after the reply's answer was drawn holds the older thread, and the tab ignores its own signal, so nothing would correct it; the controller counts Turbo's draws of the frame and drops a live answer when one happened, or is on its way, since its fetch began, then asks again (`notes_live_test.rb`, "an older live answer is not drawn over this tab's own newer one"). A refresh asks for the view without `edit`, `delete`, `shown` and `notice`, which belong to the answer before it ("a live refresh asks for the view without the notice and the shown message…").

**Watching.** One predicate decides the refresh, the toast, the chime and the desktop notification: the person is *watching* a thread when it is the view of a notes frame of this tab **and the document is not hidden**. The window's focus is not asked for, so two windows side by side both stay live. When true, the thread is refreshed (its GET reads the delivery) and nothing is said. When false, the toast is raised and the delivery stays unread. The first build fetched a thread in a hidden tab like any other, and that GET marked the note read on the person's behalf while the toast was still showing; so **a thread in a hidden document is not fetched**: it gets its counts, is remembered as stale and is fetched when the document is visible again. A closed drawer gets its counts only, for the same reason. A list is fetched hidden or not: reading a list reads nothing (`notes_live_test.rb`, "a thread open in a hidden document is not fetched: the note stays unread and is told, and is read when the page is seen again").

**The toast.** A `delivery` signal makes the tab fetch that one delivery as data (`GET admin_note_delivery_path`, through `Dashboard::NotesForYou#find`: the viewer's own row on a record they may read now, or a 404 that says nothing about which), and raise `Admin::Ui::ToastComponent` cloned from a layout `<template>` with an accent dot, the sentence ("Dana mentioned you on Pricing") and "Open", for 8 seconds. Deliveries signalled just before a Turbo visit are handed to the next page's controller. A burst is deliveries within 10 seconds of the one before: the first two get their own toasts; from the third they are replaced by one, "You have 3 new notes", whose Open leads to the inbox and whose 8 seconds start over with each further one.

**The chime and the desktop notification** (`admin--notes--alert`, on `<body>`; the two settings arrive as values on `<body>` with the page) act on a `mention` only. If any tab of the person is watching the thread, nobody chimes and nobody notifies. Otherwise exactly one tab chimes, if the sound setting is on (two short tones from the Web Audio API, no file; a tab that has not had a click plays nothing and queues nothing), and exactly one raises the desktop notification, if that setting is on, this browser granted the permission and **no** tab of theirs is visible. The tabs agree over a `BroadcastChannel` named after the person: each claims with the delivery's id and whether it is visible and watching, a visible tab answers a claim at once even while its own copy of the signal is still on its way, and 150 ms after its claim each decides from the claims it holds; the winner says "done", which every other tab honours however late its copy comes. One chime and one notification per burst. Known limits, on the roadmap: a visible tab busy for longer than 150 ms cannot answer, so a hidden one may chime with CS Studio in view; two tabs can each chime once when two mentions arrive in the same instant; and real audio, the real permission prompt and Safari are tested against fakes only (`test/system/admin/notes_alert_test.rb` stubs `AudioContext` and `Notification`). The settings and the permission line are on the account screen (`docs/reference/users-groups-tokens.md`). **A saved setting reaches open tabs at once.** `<body>` lives as long as the tab, since CS Studio moves between screens inside `#content`, so values written on it with the page stayed what they were until a full reload: a desktop notification switched on was not raised, and a tab with the old value could win the tabs' agreement. The server's word for the two settings is an element in the layout (`admin/shared/_notes_alert_settings`, the controller's `settings` target); the account screen's save replaces it in its stream, the controller takes the values as it connects, and passes them to the person's other tabs over the `BroadcastChannel` they already share. That is this browser's tabs, which is where the chime and the notification happen; a tab in another browser has the new values with its next full page load.

**No page refresh, no morphing** (decision D20): no `turbo_refreshes_with`, no `broadcast_refresh`, no `<meta name="turbo-refresh-method">`. The write queue assumes it (`docs/reference/save-rule-and-write-queue.md`), and nothing in live delivery touches an editor form or anything outside the notes parts. `test/lib/notes_guards_test.rb` ("nothing in the app turns on Turbo's page refresh or morphing, or broadcasts one") refuses all three anywhere under `app/`, `config/` and `lib/`, and `test/models/notes/signals_test.rb` any broadcast outside `Live::Signals`.

**In tests** `config/cable.yml` gives the `test` adapter, which is a subclass of the in-process `async` one with a record of what it sent, and a system test's server runs in the test process: a service called from the test thread reaches the browsers, and no test switches adapters. `await_notes_live` (`test/application_system_test_case.rb`) waits until every subscription of the page has been answered, presence's included; a signal caused a moment earlier would be lost with nothing to wait for. A notes system test clears `localStorage["cs-notes-open"]` in its teardown, because the browser is shared with the tests that follow.

**Deployment.** Production uses Solid Cable (`config/cable.yml`; its database is the `cable` entry of `config/database.yml`, `content_spiel_production_cable`, and its schema is `db/cable_schema.rb`: there is no `db/cable_migrate` directory, although `database.yml` names one as the migrations path), unused until notes. Before the first deploy: that database exists and has the schema loaded; the proxy passes WebSocket upgrades on `/cable`; and a same-origin handshake has been tried through the real proxy: `force_ssl` and `assume_ssl` now follow `PUBLIC_HOST` and `ASSUME_SSL` (`HostSettings.apply`, `docs/reference/hosting.md`), and Action Cable's same-origin check compares the browser's `Origin` with the request's own host and the scheme the proxy (or `ASSUME_SSL`) reported, while the proxy's `ssl` and `host` in `config/deploy.yml` are still commented out and that file sets no `PUBLIC_HOST`. Without the cable CS Studio works as without push: counts and lists are fresh on load.

## Files

A message may carry up to five files of at most ten megabytes each (`Notes::Files`), uploaded with it as one multipart post (no direct upload) and **fixed when the message is written**: an edit changes the text only. They are working material, not content, and are not library images: no `Media::Asset`, no usage row, no variant.

**The type is what the bytes are.** `Marcel::MimeType.for(io)` is asked with neither the file's name nor the type the browser declared. Allowed: PNG, JPEG, GIF, WEBP, PDF, plain text, CSV, and the Office and OpenDocument text, sheet and slide formats. SVG and HTML are refused. Text and CSV have no signature, so such a file is taken as text only when its bytes *are* text: valid UTF-8 with no control character but tab, newline and return; a UTF-8 byte order mark is taken off and what follows is asked again, since Marcel calls anything behind a mark `text/plain` and markup would pass. Legacy Office formats (.doc, .xls, .ppt) share one container signature with files that are none of them, so they are refused. **And the name must agree with the type** (`EXTENSIONS`): the first build stored and served by the sniffed type alone, and the review found that what a person *downloads* opens by its name's ending on their machine, so markup with no signature named `.html`, a script named `.hta`, `.ps1` or `.cmd`, and a macro-enabled `.docm` (whose bytes start like a `.docx`) all got through as "text" or "DOCX" under the uploader's name. A file is now refused when its name does not end in one of the endings of the type its bytes are; the refusal says which endings the type may have. Nothing parses a file: no variant, no analyzer (`attach` stores the blob as already analyzed). One refused file refuses the whole note: nothing is saved, nothing stored, nobody told (`test/models/notes/files_test.rb`, `test/services/notes/files_test.rb`).

**Their own storage service.** Active Storage's read routes are public to whoever holds a signed id and do not expire (`config/initializers/active_storage_access.rb`), because public pages embed them; a note's file may be confidential. So no Active Storage URL of a note's file is ever rendered, and, so that a URL built by mistake one day answers 404 instead of handing the file to the internet, note files live on a storage service of their own, `notes` (`config/storage.yml`, `Notes::Files::SERVICE`), which the public blob, representation and disk controllers refuse by its name (`ActiveStorageNoteFilesRefusal`: the blob controllers through `blob_scope`, because the representation controllers process a variant in a `before_action` of their base class, before a later callback could refuse). `bin/rails media:orphans` skips that service. Tests: `test/integration/admin/note_files_test.rb`, "no rendered view of the notes holds an Active Storage URL…" and "Active Storage's public routes refuse a note's blob, by signed id and by a disk key, and still serve a library image".

**The one action that reads a file** is `Admin::NoteFilesController#show`, at `…/threads/:thread_id/messages/:message_id/files/:id`. The lookup is a chain, each step asked only of what the step before found: the container through the read scope, the thread among the container's, the message among the thread's, the attachment among the message's files. The id is an *attachment's*, never a blob's key or a signed id, so it can't be turned into a way to read any other upload. The type sent is the one stored; a stored type that is not on the allowed list is `application/octet-stream`. Only the four image types are answered inline (the thread shows them in an `<img>` at their natural size up to the message's width, a picture smaller than 120×90 centred on a tile of that size, handoff decision 37, and a click opens the same address in a new tab; there is no lightbox); anything else is a download. Every answer carries `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `Content-Security-Policy: sandbox` and `Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy: same-origin`. **The file's name is in no log line**: `send_data` given a `filename:` makes Rails log "Sent data <name>" at INFO on every view and download, which the review of the first build found, so the `Content-Disposition` header is built here with Rails' own formatter and `send_data` is told neither a name nor a disposition. A document is `private, no-store`. A picture is `no-cache, private` with an ETag, so the reader's own browser keeps it and must ask before every use (with `no-store` every picture was downloaded again each time a thread was opened); the 304 is answered at the end of the same lookup, so someone who lost the right gets the 404 of a full request, never "not modified".

**Removal of files.** Whoever deletes messages removes their files first, inside its transaction (`Notes::Files.remove`: `Notes::DeleteMessage`, `Notes::Remove`): the attachment and blob rows go there, in three statements whatever the number of files, and **the stored bytes go only once every open transaction has committed**, because a file erased from storage cannot be brought back by a rollback and a purge that rolls back must find its notes' files where they were. There is no `purge_later` job on that path: `Notes::Remove` deletes threads with one DELETE and no callback, and no blob row is left for a job to find. Erasing is best effort; a file whose erase failed is left without a row, and nothing sweeps `storage/notes` for it (a roadmap row). Nothing limits an upload's size in front of the app: a 200 MB body is received in full before the service refuses it (pre-launch, on the roadmap).

**GIFs and reduced motion** (`admin--notes--gif`). Without reduced motion a GIF plays. With it, the picture is in the page but invisible, its first frame is painted on a canvas over it, and a Play button stands in the middle; playing, the button reads Pause and sits bottom-right. No server-made still: a note's file has no variants.

## What is not built

Each is a row of `docs/roadmap.md` ("Collaboration on content", "Before the first deploy" and the gaps): following a whole page or post; notifications with no tab open; a sound or a desktop notification for more than a mention; mail for a mention; GIF search; an anchored highlight in the text; notes on tags, users or settings and on several records; notes on one language version; assigning a thread and due dates; a permission of their own; MCP tools for notes (the services take any actor); legacy Office files; live counts in the posts list, the page tree and the media grid (they are fresh on load); note counts in the media picker's tiles.
