# Publishing: drafts of live content

> Describes: `app/models/publishing.rb`, `app/models/publishing/` (`version.rb`, `snapshot.rb`, `changes.rb`, `address_taken.rb`), `app/values/publishing/state.rb`, `app/services/publishing/`, `app/queries/publishing/`, `app/models/concerns/publishable.rb`, `app/controllers/admin/publishing_controller.rb`, `app/controllers/concerns/admin/publishing_state.rb`, `app/components/admin/editor_screen/` (`publishing_state_component`, `publish_changes_dialog_component`, `changes_overview_component`, `slug_dialog_component`, `page_settings_form_component`), `app/javascript/controllers/admin/editor/publish_dialog_controller.js`, `app/models/public_site/paths.rb`, `app/mcp/mcp/tools/update_post.rb`, `db/migrate/20261011120000_create_published_versions.rb`. The design is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-11-drafts-of-live-content-design.md`, whose "As built" says where the code departed from it. How an address follows a copy is `docs/reference/page-tree-and-routing.md`.

## What it is, and is not

Autosave writes the rows of a page or post, always. The public site draws a **published copy** of a record that is live or scheduled, never its rows, so nothing typed into such a record reaches a visitor by itself: going public is "Publish changes…", now or at a time, which takes a new copy; "Discard changes…" writes the copy in force back over the rows. A draft (no `published_at`) has no copy in use and behaves as it always did: its rows are what Preview shows and what Publish makes live (by taking its first copy).

It is not: a history screen or a restore (every publish keeps its copy, so the data is there), a release across several records, publishing per language, or a split of the right to edit from the right to publish (D5: whoever may change a published record may publish it, which is why editing one needs `publish` as well as `edit`: `AccessControl::OwnerRule`). Preview, CS Studio, the tree and the lists of the admin keep reading the rows.

## The states, and what each one shows

`Publishing::State` (`app/values/publishing/state.rb`) is the one place that says which of five states a record is in and which actions make sense in it; the header, the page tree, the posts list, the dashboard and `Publishable#status` ask it and compare nothing themselves. Nothing is stored: it is derived from three facts, the time (`published_at`: none, later than now, or not), whether a copy is pending (`Publishing.pending`, one statement, asked only for a record that has a time) and the mark (`unpublished_changes_at`). The actions are what makes sense BEFORE rights; the caller then asks `can?`.

| State | What a visitor sees | What the editor shows | Actions offered |
|---|---|---|---|
| `draft` (no time; a record unpublished again is one too, whatever copies it kept) | Nothing | "Draft"; autosave writes the rows, "SAVED 10:42" | publish, schedule |
| `scheduled` (a time in the future) | Nothing yet; at its time, the copy taken when it was scheduled (later edits are unpublished changes) | "Scheduled" and the time; with edits since, also "Unpublished changes"; with a change published for a time, also "Changes scheduled for …" | publish ("Publish now"), schedule ("Edit schedule"), unpublish; and the ways out of a change, as for a live record: while the mark is set and no copy is pending, publish_changes and discard_changes; while a copy is pending (a change published with a time of its own), publish_changes, cancel_scheduled_change and discard_changes (only while a copy is in force: the first copy of a scheduled record is, a record written around the services has none) |
| `live` (a time that has passed, no copy pending, no mark) | The copy in force | "Live" and the hint that changes are published with "Publish changes…" | unpublish |
| `live_with_changes` (live, the mark, no copy pending) | The copy in force, not the rows | "Unpublished changes"; the save line reads "SAVED · NOT PUBLISHED" | publish_changes, discard_changes, unpublish |
| `live_change_scheduled` (live, a copy pending) | The copy in force until the pending copy's `live_at`, then that one, with no write and no job | "Changes scheduled for 12 Oct 2026, 09:00" (and "Unpublished changes" for edits since) | publish_changes, cancel_scheduled_change, discard_changes (only while a copy is in force: a record whose only copy is the pending one is shown from its rows, so there is nothing to return to), unpublish |

A scheduled record with a change scheduled stays `scheduled`: the pending copy is a fact beside the state (`pending_at`), as the mark is, and the actions follow the facts. Where a service would refuse an action the state does not offer it (`discard_changes` with no copy in force). A combination that should not exist (a mark on a draft) is answered as the nearest true state and never raises, because this runs on a page view. `test/values/publishing/state_test.rb` holds the table: "every combination of the time, a pending copy and the mark is exactly one state, with its actions", "a copy pending on a live record offers every way out of it, with or without edits since", "a copy pending on a scheduled record offers the same ways out as on a live one, with or without edits since", "a scheduled record whose only copy is the pending one is not offered a discard, and the service has nothing to discard there", "a live record whose only copy is the pending one is not offered a discard: nothing is in force to return to, and cancelling is its action", "Publishable#status and the state's status are one answer, for every combination, and the booleans follow".

## The table of copies

`published_versions` (`Publishing::Version`): one immutable copy per publish. A copy has three content columns, not one: `snapshot` (the record's own fields and a post's tags), `blocks` (a page's blocks, in reading order) and `lists_posts` (whether any block of the copy lists posts). They are separate because a menu, a list of subpages and a post list read titles and excerpts only, and loading every block's body of every page in the menu for that is waste on each public request (`Publishing.published_all` reads a copy without `blocks` unless asked); `lists_posts` lets `PublicSite::Paths` load only the copies that matter. `tag_ids` (an integer array, GIN-indexed) is a post's tags as published, for list queries.

**The order of copies.** `live_at` is never earlier than the moment a copy is taken, and taking one removes the pending copy, so a record has at most one copy in the future and ids, numbers and `live_at` rise together. `Publishing.current` relies on that: the newest id among the copies in force is the one with the greatest `live_at` not after now (`test/models/publishing/version_test.rb`: "a copy taken while one is pending replaces it, and what was replaced never becomes current", "a live_at in the past is the moment the copy is taken"). The first copy of a scheduled record is in force at once and simply not shown until `published_at` passes: `published_at` stays the one clock of whether a record is visible (the resolver, the `published` scope), and a scheduled CHANGE is the only copy with a future `live_at`. **Publish now** on a scheduled record therefore changes its time only, and edits made since it was scheduled stay unpublished changes; the toast says so when there are any ("Published as it was scheduled. The changes made since are not published yet: “Publish changes…” publishes them."; `test/integration/public_drafts_test.rb`: "Publish now on a scheduled record with edits since puts what was scheduled on the site, and the answer says the changes are not published yet"). Whether it should instead offer to publish the later changes with it is the owner's to decide (roadmap).

**Who writes a copy.** `Version.take!` alone, called only by `Publishing::PublishChanges`, `Pages::Save` and `Posts::Save` (the first publish and a schedule), and by `PublicSite::Paths.foresee` inside a savepoint it rolls back (`test/lib/publishing_guards_test.rb`: "a copy is taken only where the spec says: the publish service, the two save services and the rolled-back trial"). A row is never updated or removed afterwards, with two sanctioned exceptions to "never changed" and one to "never removed": `settled_at` (`Version.settle!`, below), a merged tag (`repoint_tag!`, below: its id wherever a copy refers to it, and the id and name a post's copy shows of it, the one piece of a copy's content ever rewritten) and a pending copy, which `take!`, `CancelScheduledChange` and `DiscardChanges` withdraw because it never was public (`withdraw_pending!`) ("no row of published_versions is written or removed but by take! (and the destroyed record's own dependent delete)", "inside Publishing::Version only take!, repoint_tag!, settle! and withdraw_pending! write a row; only Tags::Merge repoints, only PublicSite::Paths settles and only the cancel and the discard withdraw"; `test/models/publishing/version_test.rb`: "a copy is never updated"). `take!` is one transaction of its own whose first statement locks the record's row (the page's row first, the lock order of every block write), and it reads the record from that row, never from the object the caller loaded: a colleague's autosave committed since is in the copy ("a copy is taken from the row as it is, not from the object the caller loaded earlier", "the first statement of a copy locks the record's row, then nothing is read before it"). `Snapshot.of` reads fresh and takes no lock, because the overview calls it on a request that must not lock a page.

**The migration** `20261011120000_create_published_versions` writes the first copy of every live or scheduled record from its rows by logic frozen in its own file, so the site is unchanged on the day it ships. **It writes no `media_usages` rows of role `published`** (it calls no application class), so after migrating an existing database **run `bin/rails media:reindex` once** (the migration prints a line saying so): until then an image only a first copy shows can be trashed or deleted, and a later "Discard changes…" of that page can fail on it. `media:reindex` writes those rows for every live and scheduled record with a copy (`test/models/media/usage_index_test.rb`: "media:reindex leaves exactly the rows the incremental path leaves, in every state"); `test/models/publishing/first_copies_migration_test.rb` compares its copies with `Snapshot.of`'s, and a change of the copy's shape on purpose needs a successor migration.

## What is in a copy, and what is immediate

`Publishing::CONTENT` lists the columns of `Page`, `Post` and `ContentBlock` a copy holds; `ROWS` lists those left on the rows on purpose, each with its reason; `NEVER_STORED` the identity and bookkeeping. **Every column of the three tables has a place in exactly one of them** and `test/models/publishing_test.rb` reads the schema and fails for a column in none or in two ("every column of #{model.table_name} is in exactly one of the copy, the rows and never stored", one test per table), so adding a column means deciding whether the public sees it from the copy. **Immediate, as before**: a slug, the place in the tree, `published_at` (publishing, scheduling, unpublishing), the trash, a post's content type and author. A slug is immediate because it moves an address, which is not content a copy could hold, so a live page's slug has its own confirmed form (below). Translation bookkeeping (`translation_sources`, `translation_original_changed_at`) is in the copy as a record of the marks at the time, but nothing reads it back and a discard does not copy it back.

## What a rebuilt record is

`Snapshot.rebuild(version, record)` returns a read-only `Page` or `Post`: the row as it is now (id, slug, tree, `published_at`) with the copy's content columns written over it; a page's `content_blocks` association and a post's `tags` are set from the copy, so the existing templates and components draw it unchanged. It raises `ActiveRecord::ReadOnlyRecord` for anything that writes, deletes, touches, locks or reloads it, and for the writers of its loaded collections. Two things it cannot do, which is why the public readers are guarded (below): a **scope** on a rebuilt page's blocks (`page.content_blocks.listing`, `.where`) still queries the rows, and `page.children` or `self_and_ancestors` read the live tree, by design. A block's copy is its whole row (`Snapshot.blocks_of`), plus `listed_tag_id` for a list block (below). A library image is looked up live by the id the copy holds; one that is gone renders nothing. A block whose type left the registry after the copy was taken is skipped and the rest drawn ("a block whose type left the registry after the copy was taken is skipped, and the rest of the copy is drawn").

**A published record with no copy** is served from its rows, as before this work (`Publishing.published` returns the record itself). The services and the migration make every copy; a row written around them (a console, and the test suite, which creates live records directly) has none. Cost, chosen on purpose: a page published from a console follows typing until someone presses "Publish changes…" once. The alternative, a copy taken by a model callback, would break "written only by the services" and rewrite the setup of most public tests. `test/lib/publishing_guards_test.rb` holds only that `published_at` is assigned in the two save services ("published_at is assigned only in the two save services"); **nothing keeps a record from being published around them**.

## Every public reader

**Nothing public reads a content column of a published record's rows where it has a copy.** The page, a post, the menu, a page's list of subpages, post lists, the sitemap's dates, the render cache and the ETag all ask `Publishing` (`published`, `published_all`, `Publishing::Listing`, and `Publishing::Version.current_for` under them); what stays on the rows is addresses (`public_paths`), the tree itself, whether a record is visible at all (`Resolver.visible?`) and internal links (they read only the target's address and visibility). Two guards, because no source grep can see a record handed in from elsewhere:

- `test/lib/public_readers_test.rb` is the source guard. What it scans is **derived** (every controller descending from `PublicController` and the concerns it includes, their views, the layouts, `app/views/shared`, the public helpers, the public components and every component those name, `config/sitemap.rb`, and the models and queries in its `EXTRA`), so a new file is scanned the day it appears, and a controller or helper that is not classified fails the test until someone decides. A line that reaches rows is exempt only by `# public: rows (why)` on it, the why at least ten characters ("every public reader reaches a published record through its copy, or says on the line why the rows are right", "it fails for a line that reads rows, however it starts, and only a mark with a reason lets it pass", "it can see a new reader: a controller, a view and a component the guard was not told about"). Going through `Publishing.` on the line is not an exemption: a relation started from rows and handed to `published_all` still says which rows are listed.
- `test/integration/public_canary_test.rb` is the behavioural guard, and the one that really holds: every content column of every live row is overwritten with a canary without publishing, every public URL in every language is requested, and no canary may appear anywhere in the WHOLE response, the body (the menu, the footer and the head included) and every header, as well as in the title, the description and `main`, which are compared with what they were ("rows overwritten without publishing change nothing a visitor sees, in any language, and publishing brings them"; "every route of a public controller is requested here"). A new public page with a route belongs in it.

Behaviour, reader by reader: `test/integration/public_drafts_test.rb` ("a live page's heading, subtitle, description and blocks stay as published while its rows change, and follow a publish", "the menu and a page's list of subpages name a live page as published, and follow a publish", "a post list shows a live post's title, excerpt and tag as published, and follows a publish").

**Lists** (`Publishing::Listing`): which posts a list holds is the post's type and `published_at` as before and its tags AS PUBLISHED (`tag_ids` of its current copy), decided in the database with the limit applied there, so a list costs the same statements for 3 posts as for 30 ("a filtered list is decided in the database: the same statements for 3 posts as for 30, and the posts read are bounded by the limit"). A live post with no copy is listed by its rows' tags. `Listing.blocks` is every list block of the site as published: a copied page's from its copy, any other page's from its rows, none of a trashed page.

**The preview** shows the rows: the menu, the lists and a post's link there (`PublicSite::Urls#path_for(post, locale, listed_on: page)`, preview only) all read rows ("the preview of an address shows the rows and the public page the copy").

## Addresses follow the copy

A post's address runs through the page whose list block lists it, and that is content, so it is derived from the list blocks and the tags as published. `PublicSite::Paths.refresh` is the one entry point; the whole account (the recorded host, the settle marker, who calls it, why only a record's own address refuses a save, the yield and the report, `foresee`) is `docs/reference/page-tree-and-routing.md`, "One entry point derives addresses" and "Addresses follow the published copies". What a change to this file must keep: a write path that changes the list blocks or the tags belongs in the parity test (`test/models/public_site/paths_test.rb`: "every write path leaves public_paths equal to a full rebuild", "changing a live page's list block to another type moves no address until the page is published"), and a publish is refused only for the record's OWN address (`Publishing.keep_address!`, `Publishing::AddressTaken`, rescued by `Pages::Save`, `Posts::Save` and `Publishing::PublishChanges`); another record that loses its address yields, is logged once and reported once (`Rails.error.report`), and the publish dialog tells the person beforehand (`Publishing::Changes.addresses`, a POST through the write queue, never a GET, because it tries the publish in a savepoint under the record's row lock). **The same trial names the live records the publish would MOVE to another address** (a post whose list on this page is removed and which another page lists too goes from `/blog/x` to `/x`; a post whose own published tags take it to another list): `foresee` answers `lost` and `moved`, and the dialog's "Addresses" box shows the second as its own group under its own sentence, each record once per pair of addresses, old → new, with the languages. The old address stays as a redirect of the record, which the trial reads from its own rows (`redirects:`) and the sentence says; where another record takes the old address in the same publish there is none, and the line says that instead. A hand test found the dialog silent about a move; nothing is refused for one (`test/integration/public_drafts_test.rb`: "removing a page's list block when another page lists the posts too MOVES them: named beforehand with the old and the new address, and the old one then redirects", "a post whose own published tags take it to another list is foreseen as moving, its own address among the moved"; `test/components/admin/editor_screen/changes_overview_component_test.rb`: "the records that would move are a second group under its own sentence: each once, by title with a link, the old address, the new one and the languages", "both groups stand under one heading, the losses first and as they were"; `test/system/admin/drafts_test.rb`: "the dialog names the post that will MOVE when the page is published, with its old and its new address, and a visitor is sent on from the old one"). A live record with no address shows a "No address" chip (`Publishing::Addressless`). The sitemap's date for a record with a copy is the later of the copy's `live_at` and the record's `published_at`; a record with none keeps its `updated_at` (`config/sitemap.rb`). **A slug or a move does not move a copied record's sitemap date** (listed as interim in the roadmap).

## The content version

`PublicSite.content_version` has two rules and a fourth part (`docs/reference/rich-text.md` has the whole account). For a record with a copy a content write moves nothing and **taking a copy moves it, when the copy is in force at once**; for a record with no copy every write moves it, as before. A scheduled change moves nothing when it is taken and moves the version at its instant by a read-time part (the newest `live_at` in force), with no write and no job; a copy of a record that is only scheduled moves nothing until the record goes live. What stays immediate (slug, tree, `published_at`, the trash, a type, a destroy) moves it as before. The public ETag reads a page's current copy's id only ("a 304 for a page reads its copy's id and not its blocks"). `PublicSite.bump_version!` is the one mover (`test/lib/version_bump_test.rb`); `test/models/public_site/version_test.rb` lists every write path ("creating a live page moves; editing it stays, and publishing the changes moves", "a scheduled change coming into force by the clock changes the version with no write", "a live record with no copy moves on every write, as before: its rows are what the site shows").

## Images in use

An image a record's current or pending copy shows counts as used, so it cannot be moved to the trash or deleted from under what is public: `Media::UsageIndex.refresh_published` writes rows of role `published` on the page or post, in the transaction that takes a copy. They exist only while the record is live or scheduled (an unpublish releases them in the same transaction), a record counts once in "Used on" and in MCP's count, and a pending copy's images are released when the change is cancelled or discarded (`test/models/media/usage_index_test.rb`: "an image only the published copy shows cannot be moved to the trash or deleted, and can once the changes are published"). The rows of a copy that a scheduled change replaced by the clock stay until the next publish of that record (an image that could be deleted cannot yet), and `media:reindex` drops a superseded copy's row that the incremental path keeps until the next copy.

## The mark

`pages.unpublished_changes_at` and `posts.unpublished_changes_at` say THAT the rows of a published or scheduled record have moved on; `Publishing::Changes` says how. A save of a content column sets it (`Publishable#note_unpublished_changes` calls `Publishing.note_change`, a conditional UPDATE by id, so a stale object cannot clear a colleague's mark: "a write whose object was loaded before a colleague's publish still marks, and one loaded before an unpublish does not"); a `touch` sets it around the callbacks (every block write touches its page); a write around the callbacks calls `Publishing.note_change`, or says `# changes: none (reason)` on its line. **The guard is per file**: a file that marks anywhere is taken at its word for all its writes (`test/lib/publishing_guards_test.rb`: "a write around the callbacks in a file that writes pages, posts or blocks marks the record as changed, or says on its line why it is no change to content"). It is cleared only by `Publishing.clear_changes`, called by a service when a copy of the current rows is taken or the rows are found to be the newest copy, and it may be set when the net difference is nothing (typed and typed back). The overview finds that out and says "Nothing to publish" but writes nothing, so **only a press clears it**: Publish changes that finds nothing, Discard changes that finds nothing different, a Cancel scheduled change that leaves the rows the same as the copy in force, or a copy taken. It is no audited field, no change to the settings form's base and moves no content version ("the mark is no audited field and no change to a page's settings base", "the mark moves nothing a stale check reads and does not move the public content version"). A post's related posts are in no copy, so changing them sets no mark.

## Changes

`Publishing::Changes.of(record)` compares the rows with the newest copy, both in the form a copy is stored in, and answers the overview as data: changed fields, the languages in which anything changed, and the blocks, each `changed`, `added`, `removed` or `moved`. Timestamps, translation bookkeeping and a bare `position` are not compared (`Publishable::BOOKKEEPING`). **"Moved" is a rule of thumb**: a change of container, or not part of the longest run of blocks in unchanged order; now and then two blocks that swapped are named as one moved, never a change missed. Two equivalences, both so that a discard can end in "nothing differs": a language holding an empty rich-text document is the same as a language with none (`Changes.languages_with_text`), and a block that came back under a new id is the block it was (`Changes.stand_ins`, content and place the same). A record with no copy has nothing to compare with.

## Publish changes

`Publishing::PublishChanges` needs the right to publish and writes no row of the record (so no editor's base moves and no hold is asked). Under the record's row lock: nothing differs and nothing is scheduled to move, so the mark is cleared and the answer is "Nothing to publish" with no copy; otherwise `Version.take!` with the time given, or **the pending copy's own time when none is** (a kept time), records `page.changes_published` or `post.changes_published` (number, `live_at`, whether scheduled) and clears the mark. A new time for changes already scheduled is something to publish even if nothing else changed. A publish is **not refused while another tab holds a part of the record**: it writes nothing an editor holds, and what is published is what has been saved, which the dialog says by naming the holder ("publish is not refused by a hold: it writes no row an editor holds").

**The server checks what the person was shown.** The publish, discard and cancel dialogs say whether a change is scheduled and for when; a colleague can set, move or cancel one between filling the dialog and pressing. Each form carries the pending copy it was shown (`pending`), and `Publishing::ShownSchedule` makes the service, under the lock, refuse with one sentence and write nothing when that is not the pending copy now; a caller that shows nothing (MCP, a script) leaves it out and is not checked ("scheduled since: 'Now' from a dialog that showed no schedule does not publish over a colleague's scheduled change", "moved since: 'At the scheduled time' does not keep a time a colleague has moved"). A refused publish reopens the dialog with the sentence, the choice and the time typed (`flash[:publish_error]`, as the Schedule dialog does); a refused discard or cancel is a toast on the freshly drawn editor.

## Discard changes

`Publishing::DiscardChanges` returns the rows to **the copy in force** and withdraws a pending copy with it, through `Pages::Save`, `Posts::Save` and the block services, so every rule they hold (validation, media, translation tracking, derived rows, the audit log, live refresh) holds for a discard. One transaction, the record's row lock first; a step that fails rolls everything back ("a discard that fails half way leaves the rows as they were"). The order, and why: (1) what the copy does not have goes first, plain blocks and then sections with `keep_blocks`, which lifts whatever of the copy's blocks someone put inside; (2) sections before the blocks in them, because a section's layout must be the copy's before a block is put back into its third column, and a deleted section must exist before its blocks are created; (3) places last, the top level before the columns, each through `ContentBlocks::Reorder`, the one service that moves a block between containers. Only what differs is handed to a service (a field, a language or a block that is the same is not), so a discard asks for no image it does not put back. Blocks are reconciled by id: one the rows lost is created again as a NEW block (its notes went with it; the delete dialog says so), one the copy does not have is deleted with its notes. What does not come back: the slug, the tree place and the publish state (immediate), translation bookkeeping (whatever the save services leave when the text is written back). **Tags go back by id and none is ever made**: a post's tags come back as the ids its copy holds, each under its present name, a list block's filter as the tag whose id the copy recorded; a tag deleted since stays deleted, a merged tag stays merged (a merge alone leaves nothing different: every copy was rewritten with the rows). Where a rename or a delete leaves the rows different from the copy (the copy still shows the old name), the discard succeeds, says so in the toast (`REMAINS`) and the mark STAYS, so the difference is seen and published or left by a person ("after a merge a discard leaves the post on the KEPT tag: the merged tag is not made again, and publishing afterwards changes no list and no address"). A block of a type that left the registry cannot be recreated and the discard fails with that message, changing nothing.

**Whether another tab holds part of the record is asked by the controller, not the service** (`Admin::EditingClaims#refuse_discard_under_hold`, any hold of the record by another tab; a call with no tab, such as the console or MCP, is not refused): the service knows no reservation, as no service does. Events: `*.changes_discarded` with the number returned to and the withdrawn one. The audit sentences are "published changes to Pricing", "scheduled changes to Pricing for 12 Oct", "discarded the unpublished changes to Pricing", "cancelled the scheduled changes to Pricing" (`Audit::Sentence`), and the three actions are in `Editing::Signals::MAP`; `*.scheduled_change_cancelled` is in `Dashboard::Feed::NOT_A_CHANGE` (it writes no content).

## Cancel a scheduled change

`Publishing::CancelScheduledChange` withdraws the pending copy; the site keeps showing the copy in force and **the rows stay exactly as they are**, so what was scheduled is unpublished changes again wherever the rows differ from the copy in force (the mark says so; where they do not, it is cleared). It needs the right to publish, takes the row lock first and is not refused by a hold.

## The header, and why its forms are never replaced

The header draws three replaceable parts and three dialogs. `Admin::EditorScreen::PublishingStateComponent` draws `#publishing-chip` (the "Unpublished changes" chip, "Changes scheduled for …", the "No address" chip, and the marks `[data-publish-changes-offered]` and `[data-discard-changes-offered]` that decide whether a button shows) and `#publishing-actions` (the "Discard changes…" and "Publish changes…" buttons, which only OPEN dialogs); the changes overview is the third, inside the publish dialog. **Only the chip is ever replaced**: by a save's answer (`Admin::PublishingState#publishing_state_streams`) and by another tab's change (`Admin::EditingController#publishing`, a live part other tabs fetch; 204 for a draft). **The dialogs stand outside all of them and the forms in them are drawn once**, with the page, for every record that is not a draft and a viewer who has the right, whatever the record has to publish at that moment, because the write queue holds a form that was pressed while a save was out (`admin--saving--queue-hold`) and sends it afterwards, and a form replaced by the very answer it waits for is gone when the queue is drained, so the press would be lost (`docs/reference/save-rule-and-write-queue.md`; "Publish changes pressed in its dialog while a block's autosave is still out…" in `test/system/admin/unpublished_changes_test.rb`, and the Discard and Cancel counterparts in `test/system/admin/drafts_test.rb`). **A dialog moved inside one of the replaceable parts would close under the person at their next autosave: no guard.**

**What a dialog says is asked when it opens** (`admin--editor--publish-dialog`): the overview and everything about a schedule, by a POST through the write queue (`PublishingController#changes`, never a GET: it takes the row lock to foresee addresses), so it is true only once every save of the tab has landed. `part=schedule` answers the schedule alone, no overview and no trial, for the discard and cancel dialogs. An open editor is not told that a colleague published or discarded: its chip is refreshed, the overview is read again at the next opening, and what is published is always what is saved at the press. Whether the cancel dialog and its menu item EXIST is from the page view.

## The slug's own form

A live page's slug is not in the autosaving settings form. `Admin::PagesController#update` ignores `page[slug]` for a page with a `published_at`; the settings show the address as text with "Change address…", which opens `Admin::EditorScreen::SlugDialogComponent`, drawn by the view beside the live region (outside the settings form: a form in a form is invalid, and the write queue would read it as part of the autosaving one). Its form is a `_top` form behind `admin--saving--queue-hold`, `PATCH pages/:id/slug`; the action is an instant action like Publish: no hold is taken, it is refused while another tab holds the page's settings (`Admin::EditingClaims#refuse_instant_redirect`), it goes through `Pages::Save` (the address clash keeps the slug validation's message and changes nothing; someone who may not publish is refused there), it sets no unpublished-changes mark, and the old address redirects to the new. A draft's slug stays a field. A post's slug stays in its autosaving form: on a live post a typed slug moves the address at once. (`test/integration/admin/page_editor_test.rb`: "the settings autosave of a live page does not take a slug, and a draft's does", "a live page's address changes through its own form, at once, leaving a redirect, with no unpublished change", "an address that is taken or reserved goes back with the reason and changes nothing", "the address form is refused while another tab holds the page's settings, and by someone who may not publish"; `test/components/admin/editor_screen/page_settings_form_component_test.rb`: "a live page's settings show its address and Change address…, and no slug field; a draft's show the slug field as before"; `test/system/admin/drafts_test.rb`: "Change address… moves a live page at once: the visitor finds it at the new address and is sent on from the old one".) The slug-preview script throws without its slug field, so the wrapper carries the controller only when the field is there. The dialog is drawn once at page load: a draft published in another tab shows its slug field until a reload.

## MCP

`update_post` writes the rows, so a live post it changes has unpublished changes afterwards (the answer's `unpublished_changes` is true); `publish_changes: true` publishes in the same call and is **not atomic with the edit**: when the publication is refused (the post's own address taken), the edit stays saved and the answer says so. `get_post` and `list_posts` return the working version and say `unpublished_changes` (`Publishing::State.of(post).changes?`).

## Tags

A list block's filter is a tag NAME in its settings, matched by name across two copies taken at different times a rename would empty every list, so **matching is by id**: a page's copy records, for a list block, the id its filter named when it was taken (`listed_tag_id`), a post's copy carries `tag_ids`, and the public list matches one against the other. **No tag is ever created automatically**: not by a publish, not by a discard. A copy that recorded no id for a named tag lists nothing until its page is published again. Renaming or deleting a tag changes nothing public while the copies hold its id; the carrying posts are marked as having unpublished changes (a rename of a tag with forty posts is forty marks, and there is no "publish all of these"), and **a discard cannot clear that mark until the name is published**. A **merge is immediate everywhere, the name a visitor reads included**: `Publishing::Version.repoint_tag!` (the one sanctioned write to a copy's content after it is taken, by `Tags::Merge` alone) replaces the merged tag in every copy, in force, pending and older. In `tag_ids` (the tags AS PUBLISHED for every list query, and what a discard puts back) and in a list block's `listed_tag_id` it replaces the id, because a tag's id in a copy is a reference and left alone every list would lose its posts and their addresses. In a post's `snapshot["tags"]`, which is what the public post page and the lists print, the merged tag's entry becomes the kept tag's, id and name, where it stood; a copy that carried the kept tag already keeps that entry as it is (under the name it was published with) and loses the merged one, so the tag is there once and the others keep their order. It used to leave `snapshot["tags"]` alone, and a hand test found the site printing the merged-away name beside the kept one until each post was published again. So **a merge marks no post**: rows and copies are rewritten alike and nothing is left to publish (`Tags::Listings#touch_carrying_posts(mark: false)`; a post marked before the merge keeps its mark and its changes). The name in a list block's copied `settings` is not rewritten (no visitor reads it), so a page whose list named the merged tag IS marked, with "Changed: Post list" to publish and nothing a visitor would see change. The merge bumps the public content version once for all of it. The three tag services say what they touched through `Tags::Listings#pages_listing`. (`test/queries/publishing/listing_test.rb`: "a filter whose tag did not exist when the copy was taken lists nothing, also once a tag has that name, until the page is published again", "a tag renamed after both copies were taken drops no post from the list"; `test/integration/public_drafts_test.rb`: "merging a tag changes no public list and no address, with no publish, and publishing the page and the posts afterwards changes nothing either", "after a merge a visitor reads the kept tag's name once and the merged one nowhere, on the post and in the list, with no publish", "a merge alone leaves a live post with nothing to publish and no mark; one that had unpublished changes before keeps its mark and its changes"; `test/models/publishing/version_test.rb`: "a merge rewrites what a post's copy shows of the merged tag: the kept tag's id and name in its place, the others where they were", "a merge removes the merged tag from a copy that already carried the kept one, which stays once, where and as it was", "a merge rewrites the pending copy and the older ones as it does the one in force".)

## In tests

A record made with `create!` has no copy and is served from its rows. One made through `create_page` or `create_post` (the services) has one, so after changing it **call `publish_changes(record)` before reading the public page** (`test/support/content_helper.rb`; it asserts the result). `follow_rows!` removes every copy taken so far and derives the posts' addresses again, for a test of something that acts on rows at once (a tag service's own sync). A copy's clock is moved by writing its row, not by waiting: `Publishing::Version.of(page).pending.update_all(live_at: 1.minute.ago) # version: none (a test moves a copy's clock)`, and the unit tests hand `now:` to the services. A browser test never waits for a time; it presses with a save held on the wire (`delay_next` in `test/system/admin/drafts_test.rb`, the pattern of `editing_holds_test.rb`), because a press that is lost to a replaced form is exactly what these dialogs exist to prevent. No guard keeps a test from taking a copy around the services.

## What is not built

A history screen and restoring an earlier version. Releases across several records (a release is a set of copies that come into force together: one `live_at` for several records is already possible; a preview of the site with pending copies applied is not). Publishing per language. Separating the right to edit from the right to publish. A slug, a tree position and the publish state as pending changes; a post's slug as a confirmed form. Notes of a block a discard brings back. A refusal of a publish that takes ANOTHER record's address away (it is announced, not refused). An action that publishes the changes of several records at once. The roadmap holds each, with the interim behaviours of this work.
