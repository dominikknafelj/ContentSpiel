# Presence, and the seams notes and presence share

> Describes: `app/models/presence.rb`, `app/models/presence/`, `app/queries/presence/`, `app/channels/presence/`, `app/jobs/presence/`, `app/controllers/admin/presence_controller.rb`, `app/controllers/concerns/admin/record_of_kind.rb`, `app/components/admin/presence_screen/`, `app/components/admin/ui/tooltip_component.rb`, `app/javascript/controllers/admin/presence/`, `app/javascript/controllers/admin/ui/tooltip_controller.js`, `app/javascript/live/`, `app/models/live.rb`, `app/models/live/`, `app/channels/live/`, `app/models/record_kinds.rb`, `app/components/admin/slots.rb`, `app/components/admin/ui/slot_component.rb`, `config/initializers/admin_slots.rb`, `app/values/person_name.rb`, `config/locales/admin/presence_screen.en.yml`.

The spec is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-presence-design.md` (its "As built" section at the end lists where the code left it), the look is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-presence-handoff.md` (items 1 to 31). This file describes the code and the incidents behind its rules. Notes (`docs/reference/notes.md`) is the first user of the three seams below and presence the second; read this page before changing either.

## What presence is, and is not

Presence says who else has a **page, a post or a library image** open: their initials in the editors' header and in the media library's image panel, and a marker in the page tree and the posts list on the records where someone is working. It is live: a person arriving or leaving shows within about a second. It is **not a lock** and holds nothing for anybody: it warns of nothing on save and changes nothing about a save, conflicts stay with the existing save handling (`docs/reference/save-rule-and-write-queue.md`). Reservations on a block ("Dana is editing this block") are step 3 of the roadmap's collaboration item.

Everyone who can open a record sees everyone on it, read-only viewers included; nobody sees themselves; one person in several tabs or browsers counts once. There is no invisible mode and no per-person setting.

## The table, and why it is throwaway

`presence_marks` (`Presence::Mark`, `db/migrate/20261010120000_create_presence_marks.rb`) has one row per person, record and browser tab: `user_id` (cascading), `record_type` and `record_id` (polymorphic, **no foreign key**: a trashed or purged record's rows simply go stale), `tab` (the tab's id, in the shape `Live::Signals::TAB` allows), `token`, `state` (`here` or `away`, a check constraint), and three clocks: `seen_at`, `arrived_at` and `state_at`. It has no timestamps. It is unique on user, record and tab and indexed on record type, record id and `seen_at`.

**It is an ordinary table**, not `UNLOGGED`: the spec (D5) said "unlogged if the schema file keeps that marking", and Rails' schema dumper does not record it, so `db:schema:load`, which is how the test database and a new machine are made, would have built a logged table behind the migration's back. The table is in the main database (not the cable database, which exists only in production, and not the cache, which is the null store in tests): the write load is one tiny update per open tab every 30 seconds.

`Presence::Mark` has four class methods, each **one statement** and none of them going through the model's callbacks: `arrive` (an upsert; `arrived_at` is kept when the same tab arrives again, the token, state, `seen_at` and `state_at` are replaced), `report` (state and `seen_at`; `state_at` moves only when the state differs, said in the statement as a `CASE`), `beat` (`seen_at` only) and `leave` (deletes the row that carries the given token). `report`, `beat` and `leave` answer whether a row was there.

## Here, away, stale, swept

- **Here** while the tab's document is visible and the person did something in it (a key, a press, a pointer move, a scroll) within the last **5 minutes**. **Away** after that, or while the document is hidden, as a tab's script reports it. **Gone** when the tab closes, leaves the screen or loses its connection.
- The tab **beats every 30 seconds**, carrying its state, and reports a change at once.
- A row not refreshed for **`Presence::STALE_AFTER`, 150 seconds**, is ignored by every read, whatever the sweep did or did not delete (`Presence::Mark.fresh`). A tab that crashed or lost its network sends no leave and causes no signal; this is what takes it off the screen.
- `Presence::Sweep` deletes rows older than **`Presence::SWEEP_AFTER`, 5 minutes**, every 5 minutes, in every environment (`config/recurring.yml`). It only keeps the table small and signals nothing: what it deletes nobody could see.
- Both scripts also **read again every 60 seconds** while the document is visible, and when it becomes visible, so that a crashed tab's person ages out of a screen that hears no signal.

**Why 150 seconds and not the spec's 60.** Browsers throttle the timers of a hidden tab; Chrome, after five minutes hidden, runs them about once a minute. A hidden tab is exactly an *away* person, whose 30-second beat then arrives every minute or so; with a 60-second limit the person flickered off and on. 150 is two throttled beats and some slack. The cost is that a crashed tab's person is shown for up to two and a half minutes instead of one. (`test/queries/presence/roster_test.rb`: "a row just past STALE_AFTER is not seen and one just inside it is".)

**"Away N min"** is counted from `state_at`: for a person with several tabs, the *latest* `state_at` among their away rows (they have been away since the last of their tabs went away), and "here" is counted from the earliest `arrived_at` among their here rows. A keep-alive never moves `state_at`. Known: an arrival resets it, so a laptop that reconnects shows "away 0 min" again (roadmap).

## The two streams, and why the list signal names nothing

`Presence::Signals` says *that* presence changed, never who: `{ kind: "presence" }`. It is sent, through `Live::Signals`, after the outermost commit, on two streams:

- `presence:record:<Type>-<id>` (`Presence::Signals.record_stream`; the type is the polymorphic name without its colons, `MediaAsset-5`), to which `Presence::Channel` subscribes after finding the record through the read scope. A tab showing that record fetches its people again.
- `presence:list`, to which **any signed-in user** may subscribe (`Presence::ListChannel`; a session that has ended is rejected). A tab showing a list fetches all its markers again.

**The list signal carries no record.** The spec (§5.2 item 6) had `{ kind: "presence", record: ["post", 812] }` on it, so a list could ignore signals about rows it does not show. The first build did that, and the review found what it meant: the stream is open to every signed-in user, so the signal told someone with no grant on posts that post 812 exists and that somebody is working on it now, which is the one thing every other door answers alike for "missing" and "not yours". Now a list tab hears "somebody did something somewhere", and re-reads **all** its markers on any signal, at most once every 5 seconds (below), through the read scope. A busy site costs a list one request per five seconds, whatever happens. (`test/channels/presence/channel_test.rb`: "a signal on the list names no record, whatever the kind".)

## The channel, and why a row has a token

`Presence::Channel` (a `Live::RecordChannel`) takes `type`, `id` and `tab` (a tab with no id of the right shape is rejected before anything is looked up), writes the tab's row (`Mark.arrive`) and signals. It offers a client exactly two actions, `report(state)` and `beat(state?)`; leaving is the subscription's `unsubscribed`, and there is no "leave" to send.

**A subscription owns its row by a token.** `arrive` makes a fresh random token and `leave` deletes only a row that still carries its subscription's. The incident: a tab keeps its id across a reconnect, so a new connection arrives at the very row the old connection is still subscribed to, and the server notices the old one is dead later, when it runs *that* connection's `unsubscribed`. That late leave deleted the row the new subscription had just written, after which every `beat` and `report` updated zero rows and the person was invisible although they were there. Now the late leave finds another token and deletes nothing (`test/channels/presence/channel_test.rb`: "two connections of one tab: a late unsubscribe of the older leaves the newer subscription's row, and the newer one's deletes it"; `test/models/presence/mark_test.rb`: "leave deletes the row of that token only: a late leave of an older subscription deletes nothing").

**A keep-alive heals.** A `beat` or a `report` that finds no row (swept, deleted by a late leave, any cause) writes it again with the subscription's token and the state last reported, and signals, so a person who is there is back within one keep-alive (`"a beat finds no row, puts it back with the subscription's token and the state it carries, and tells the others"`). The tab reports its state on **every `connected`**, not only the first: an arrival makes the row "here", and a hidden or idle tab that reconnects would otherwise be shown as here until its next change. A `beat` carries the tab's state, so a row put back is what the tab is, and a `beat` whose state differs from the last one reported is a `report`.

**And a subscription that has left heals nothing.** Action Cable hands each of a connection's commands to a pool of threads and takes a subscription out of its list only after `unsubscribed` has returned, so a `beat` sent in the same moment as the leave could be worked on the same object, find no row and put one back, with nobody left to delete it: the person stayed for as long as a row stays fresh. `unsubscribed` therefore sets `@left` *first*, before it deletes, and `report`, `beat` and `heal` return at once once it is set ("a beat and a report that come after the leave write no row and tell nobody"; "a report that comes after the leave does not touch the row a newer subscription of the tab holds"). Two narrower races remain, both corrected by the 150-second filter and both on the roadmap: a `beat` that updated nothing and asks to heal just *after* the leave started, and a heal overwriting a newer subscription's token; and `heal` itself is check-then-act.

**Not throttled.** A client may send `report` and `beat` as fast as it likes. Each is one indexed update, and a `report` that changes the state also two broadcasts. Roadmap.

## Why a channel's hooks are private

Action Cable lets a client call any **public** method a channel class defines itself (`action_methods`; `dispatch_action` hands an arity-1 method the client's data). In `Live::RecordChannel` the hooks are `subscribed`, `arrived(record)` and `stream_name_for(record)`; a public `arrived` in presence *writes a row*, and a public `subscribed` is a command anyone with a socket could send again. The review of the base channel found the hooks public, and a client could have sent `{action: "arrived"}`. So the hooks, `subscribed` and `unsubscribed` are private in the base and in every subclass, `Presence::Channel` offers exactly `report` and `beat`, `Notes::RecordChannel`, `Notes::PersonChannel` and `Presence::ListChannel` offer none, and `test/channels/live/record_channel_test.rb` ("no channel under app/channels that descends from Live::RecordChannel offers an action it does not mean to"; "the test subclasses make the guard able to fail: a public hook would be an action") fails for a descendant that makes one public. The stream hook is `stream_name_for`, **not** Rails' own `stream_for`, which exists on every channel (for a model) and which a subclass that forgot to define the hook would silently get; the base raises `NotImplementedError` for it ("a subclass that forgot stream_name_for fails loudly, not with Rails' stream_for"). `arrived` runs before `stream_from`, so one that raises leaves nothing open and the subscription is never confirmed.

## The roster

`Presence::Roster.new(viewer:)` answers two questions, reading only fresh rows and never the viewer's own (your other tab is you):

- `on(record)`: one `Person` per *other* user, `state` here if any of their fresh rows is, `since` as above; here before away, then by the **earliest `arrived_at` over all their fresh rows**, so a reload (a new row) does not send someone to the end of the line, then by user id. Two statements at most; a user deleted since the rows were read is skipped, not an error.
- `here_people(kind, ids)`: for a list, `{ record id => Here(count, users) }` for the records of the kind among `ids` with somebody *here* (away people do not mark a row), the first five users named in the same order. One statement for the marks and one for the users, whatever the number of ids, and none for the users when nobody is here; `ids` may be a relation, which becomes a subquery.

The roster is told nothing about rights and **trusts its caller to have narrowed**: each door below finds the record or the ids through the read scope first. (`test/queries/presence/roster_test.rb`.)

## The two fetches

`Admin::PresenceController` (a class, never `Admin::Presence`) only reads:

- `GET presence/:kind/:id` (`admin_record_presence_path`) answers a record's people as `turbo-frame#presence-<kind>-<id>`, at the header's size or, with `size=panel`, the media panel's, with the frame alone for the script's fetch. The record is found by `Admin::RecordOfKind#record_of_kind`, which is `RecordKinds` plus the rule below: a 404 for every nil, the 403 notes gives for a library image without `media.upload`.
- `GET presence/here?kind=&ids[]=` (`admin_presence_here_path`) answers `{ "<id>": { count, names, label } }` for the asked ids (at most 500, digits only) that the viewer may read and that have someone here. The ids are narrowed with `kind.readable_ids(...).where(id: ids)` as a subquery; a list that is not readable, an unknown kind and a bad id are the same empty answer. `label` is the marker's one text, for a screen reader and the tooltip alike (handoff 32), worded by `Admin::PresenceScreen::RowMarkerComponent.words`, the one place a marker is worded, so the first drawing and the later answers agree and **the script holds no wording**.

Neither answer is kept by any cache, the browser's included (`Cache-Control: no-store`): a fetch answered from one would show who was here a minute ago.

## The two scripts

Both are Stimulus controllers on `live/connection` and say on their element whether their subscription has been answered (`live/state`, below). Neither touches an editor: no form, no write queue, no address, no autosave base (`test/system/admin/presence_test.rb`: "presence touches nothing: the page's autosave base, its address and its updated_at are what they were").

**`admin--presence--record`** (`app/javascript/controllers/admin/presence/record_controller.js`), on the element `Admin::PresenceScreen::PeopleComponent` renders, subscribes to `Presence::Channel`, reports the tab's state (away while the document is hidden or after 5 minutes without input; the input listeners only note the time, a pointer move is looked at once a second), beats every 30 seconds and redraws the frame when told or on the 60-second timer. Params are built in one order with the id as a string, because the pool's key is their JSON and two spellings would be two subscriptions. A subscription refused, or an answer of 404 or 403, ends the part for good (it stops listening, which is leaving); a redirect to sign-in or a 500 is not that, and it goes on asking.

**`admin--presence--list`** (`list_controller.js`), on the page tree and the posts table, subscribes to `Presence::ListChannel` (one fixed set of params, so every list of a page shares one subscription) and re-reads its markers (the `here` address, in batches of 500 ids, one after the other; the `chunk` value) on any signal, no sooner than **5 seconds** after its last reading, with one more reading afterwards if signals arrived meanwhile; also when its subscription is confirmed, every 60 seconds, and when it comes out of Turbo's cache. Never while the document is hidden: what was owed is read once when it is seen. It changes the markers in place (`hidden`, the count, and the one text for a screen reader and in the tooltip, each only where it differs) and **never redraws a row**. When the tree is replaced by a Turbo Stream, the old controller hands its clock to the new one (`handover`).

**The frame is filled by a plain `fetch`, not a Turbo request.** The first design navigated the frame. A Turbo request in an editor is held while a save is out (`saving/frame_hold.js`) and is heard by everyone who listens to Turbo's frame events; who else has the page open is nobody's business there, and a presence refresh stuck behind a slow save would have shown stale people. Both scripts fetch with `fetch`; the record's asks for the frame by its `Turbo-Frame` header so the answer is the frame alone, and puts the answer where the frame stands, and the list's asks for JSON. The frame element has no `src` and nothing links into it. (`test/system/admin/presence_lists_test.rb`: "a list's readings are plain requests: no Turbo fetch, visit, frame load or stream".)

**Status texts are patched in place.** "Away 12 min" is in the answer three times (the button's name, the list's row, the circle's tooltip) and grows by one every minute, so with an away person on screen nearly every read differed from the one before. Replacing the group each time took an open list from under a reader, hid a tooltip that was showing and told a screen reader the button again. Now an answer that is the one shown touches nothing, an answer that differs only in its words has the words put where they stand (`sameShape`, `patch`: the same circles in the same order with the same state and initials, the same rows with the same state and initials, the same "+N"), and only a real change (someone came, went, turned away or came back, the order or "+N") is drawn anew, with an open list and the focus put back (`test/system/admin/presence_test.rb`: "an away person's growing minutes change the texts in place: the same button keeps the focus, an open list stays read, a showing tooltip stays shown"; "an answer that is the one shown is not drawn again, and the focus stays on the very button it was on"; "a list being read survives a real redraw, and the next press of the button closes it").

**A tab that is going says so.** A Turbo visit away disconnects the controller, which stops listening, which is leaving. A full load (an address typed, a link out, signing out) disconnects nothing, and the browser may keep the page for its Back button with the socket still open, so the server heard of no close and the person stayed until the row went stale. `pagehide` therefore ends the subscription at once (`suspend()` in `live/connection`) and `pageshow` takes it up again (`resume()`); `pagehide` comes before the document is hidden, so the "away" of a page on its way out is never sent (it would reach the server beside the leave, and a report that finds no row puts one back).

**Turbo's snapshot** keeps the page as it was left, people included. As the toasts do, a render that comes out of the cache (a preview, a Back or a Forward) has its presence parts emptied and marked waiting before it reaches the page, and the people are read again on connecting; the `restoring` flag that decides this is reset at `turbo:load` and has no test of its own (roadmap).

**Names are text.** A name is what a person typed. The server draws it escaped; the scripts write it with `textContent` and nothing in presence builds HTML from a name (`test/components/admin/presence_screen/row_marker_component_test.rb`: "a name is text, whatever was typed as one"; `test/integration/admin/presence_test.rb`: "a name with markup in it is answered as the text it is"; `test/system/admin/presence_lists_test.rb`: "hovering a marker shows its words in the shared tooltip, in the tree and in the posts list, whole and on top, and a name is text").

## What the screens show

The look is the handoff's; the tests cite its items. In short: the editor header and the media panel get **one group**, a button (the one Tab stop; Enter, Space or a click opens the list of everyone, Escape returns the focus) around up to three 24px circles (two 20px circles in the panel, at every width) and "+N", here before away, away drawn unfilled, "Dana Weiss · away 12 min" as the circle's tooltip, the button named for everyone with how each is. A tree row and a posts row get a 12px user icon where somebody is **here**, with the count from two; in the posts list it stands on the title's line inside the row's link, so its words join the link's accessible name. Names come from `PersonName.of` (below).

`Admin::PresenceScreen::PeopleComponent` and `RowMarkerComponent` are the two components. The marker element is **always rendered**, `hidden` where nobody is, so the list's script changes it in place.

**The name rule is a value.** A person's short name (their name, or for someone without one the part of the email address before the at sign; the whole address said more than an avatar needs to) was notes' rule (`Notes.name_of`). Presence needed the same rule and may not know notes, and notes may not know presence, so the rule moved to `PersonName` (`app/values/person_name.rb`, with the letters of a circle: `PersonName.initials`), which `Notes.name_of` and presence both call. `User#display_name`, which answers the whole address, is not changed and is not used for either (`test/values/person_name_test.rb`, `test/integration/admin/note_names_test.rb`).

**The shared tooltip** (`Admin::Ui::TooltipComponent`, `admin--ui--tooltip`) is new: CS Studio had none, and the handoff forbids a `title` here because a keyboard does not reach it. It shows after 300ms on hover and at once on keyboard focus, lines up with its host's edge instead of crossing the window's or a clipping box's, goes on Escape and when its host is pressed, and shows nothing over a host whose button has its list open. Existing icon buttons still use `title`; the sweep to move them is later (roadmap), as is a vertical collision check.

## The exception to the service rule

Presence is the one write in CS Studio that is not an `ApplicationService` (the others are sessions, the setup marker and derived rows). A channel writes and deletes its own `presence_marks` rows through `Presence::Mark`, because a presence is not content, nobody asks for it by name, and a row is ignored by every read after 150 seconds. So it has no `authorize!` (the channel's door is the read scope: you may be on what you may read), no `record_event` (it is never audited, and nothing of it reaches the log), no `touch` of the record it is on and no content version: a presence write says `# version: none (presence is not content)` on its line, as `test/lib/version_bump_test.rb` requires of a write that goes around the callbacks. `test/models/presence/mark_test.rb` ("presence writes nothing but its own row: not the record, not the content version, not the audit log") and `test/channels/presence/channel_test.rb` ("presence writes nothing of the record, of the content version or of the audit log") hold it; `test/integration/admin/presence_test.rb` holds that the two reads do not write either. Presence knows nothing of notes.

## The seams

Notes and presence are the first two features that live beside content and on the same screens. The plan (D6, D7) made them share what both need and nothing else: three seams, extracted from notes while presence was being built, each used by both.

### `RecordKinds`: which records a feature can be about

`RecordKinds` (`app/models/record_kinds.rb`) is a registry of the models a feature can attach to: `page`, `post` and `media`, registered where the registry is defined, in that order. A `Kind` has a `name` (as it appears in a URL or a subscription), a `model_name` (a string, resolved on demand so that a development reload never leaves a stale class), the right that **reads** it, the right that **moderates** it, and `scoped`.

- `scoped: true` (pages, posts): the rights are *per record*, so a record outside the user's grants is **not found** (`AccessControl.scope`); every nil is a 404.
- `scoped: false` (the library image): holding the read right (`media.upload`) opens *every* record, and a user without it gets a **403**, as `Admin::MediaController`'s gate does; it takes a `base` relation (`Media::Asset.kept`).

One relation, `Kind#readable(user, channel:)`, describes what a user may read; `find` and `readable_ids` are derived from it so they cannot drift (`test/models/record_kinds_test.rb`: "find answers exactly the records readable holds, whoever asks, trashed ones included"). `RecordKinds.find(name, user, id)` is nil for an unknown kind, a bad id, an out-of-scope record, a trashed one and a missing user alike. Notes reads its kinds from it (`Notes.subject_types` is the kinds' types plus `ContentBlock`, which is no kind: a block is reached through its page), `Admin::NoteContainer`, `Admin::RecordOfKind` and both record channels find records through it, and `Notes.readable_threads` narrows through each kind's `readable`.

**To add a fourth kind** (a new model; a new post type, block type or page type needs nothing) register it once and place the slots on its screen (below). A test adds one with `RecordKinds.with(*kinds) { … }`, which takes it out again in an `ensure`.

### `Live::Signals` and `Live::RecordChannel`: the server side of live updates

`Live::Signals.later(stream, payload)` is the **one sender** of Action Cable signals (`ActionCable.server.broadcast` appears nowhere else in `app/` or `lib/`). A feature has a module of its own that decides when to say what (`Notes::Signals`, `Presence::Signals`) and sends through it. It sends after the outermost commit, a failed broadcast is logged by kind and the error's class and swallowed, and it **refuses at the call** a payload that could carry text: a `kind` is required; every key is `kind`, `reason`, `origin`, `record` or ends in `_id`; `kind` and `reason` are one short lowercase word (`/\A[a-z_]{1,32}\z/`); `origin` is a tab id in the shape `TAB` allows (a nil one is refused, not skipped: the caller leaves the key out); an `_id` is an Integer or nil; `record` is `[a kind `RecordKinds` knows, an Integer]`. The payload is copied, keyed with symbols, checked and frozen once. (`test/models/live/signals_test.rb`: "a payload that could carry text is refused at the call"; `test/models/notes/signals_test.rb`: "nothing in the app broadcasts but Live::Signals, and only a ...::Signals module calls it".)

`Live::RecordChannel` is the base of a channel on one record: it finds the record through `RecordKinds.find` **at subscribe time**, for the user as they are now (`subscriber` re-reads the session, so an owner demoted since the socket connected is judged by their grants and a deleted user is rejected), rejects alike a record out of scope, trashed, missing, of an unknown kind or with a bad id, and builds the stream's name from the record it found, never from what was sent. A subclass says only `stream_name_for(record)` and may say `arrived(record)`; both private (see above). A subscription already open is not re-judged when a right is taken away; the signal says nothing and the fetch it leads to is scoped (roadmap).

In the browser the shared half is `app/javascript/live/connection.js` (one socket per document, the pooled subscriptions released a task late, the tab's id `TAB`, and `perform`, `suspend` and `resume`; see its header) and `live/state.js`, which writes `data-notes-live` on `<body>`: `"connected"` once **every** subscription of the page has been answered, which the system tests wait for (`await_notes_live`, in `test/application_system_test_case.rb`). The two halves on `<body>` (notes' person and records) each say how they stand; a *part* that is an element of the page and not always there (presence's group, a list) says it on its own element with `data-live-part`, which the server renders as `waiting`, so the page is not called live before the script has connected, and a page with no such part is live without one. The attribute keeps the name it had when only notes spoke, and so does the helper; renaming both is on the roadmap.

### `Admin::Slots`: named places on a screen

A slot is a name at a place on a screen where a feature draws something of its own. `Admin::Slots` (`app/components/admin/slots.rb`; in the components layer because it holds component classes, a named exception in `test/lib/source_layout_test.rb`) keeps an ordered list of entries per name, and a screen draws `Admin::Ui::SlotComponent` at the place and does not know what stands there. With nothing registered a slot renders nothing at all, no wrapper element. The names are the screens' to draw and are listed in `Admin::Slots::NAMES`:

| Slot | Where | Registered today |
|---|---|---|
| `editor.header.people` | first in the editors' action row | presence's people, `size: :header` |
| `editor.header.actions` | the editors' action row | notes' button |
| `media.panel.header` | the image panel's file-name row, between the name and the "×" | presence's people, `size: :panel` |
| `media.panel.sections` | the panel's sections | notes' section |
| `tree.row.markers` | a page tree row, after the title | notes' pill, then presence's marker |
| `posts.row.markers` | the posts list's Notes cell | notes' pill |
| `posts.row.title` | the posts list's title line, inside the link | presence's marker, `joined: true` |

**Features register in `config/initializers/admin_slots.rb`**, the one file that names them all, in order, inside `to_prepare` after `Admin::Slots.reset!` (a development reload replaces the components and would otherwise register them twice or keep old classes). A component is built as `component.new(record:, viewer:, data:, **context, **options)`: `record` is the page, post or image (the row's, in a list), `viewer` the user (passed, never read from `Current`), `data` what the entry's loader answered for that record and nil for a single-record slot, `context` whatever the host handed over as `slot_context:` (the editors pass `counts:`, the numbers they already read for the drawer, so the button costs no statement), and `options` the registration's own (`register(name, component, size: :panel)`), merged last so they win. **Every slot component must accept `**`** for the context it does not use. The reserved names `record`, `viewer`, `data` and `component` are refused as options.

**A single-record slot against a list slot.** A single-record slot (a header, a panel) leaves `data` nil and its component asks for what it needs. A **list slot** (the tree's and the posts list's rows) registers a **loader**, `->(records, viewer:) { { record id => data } }`, which `Admin::Slots.load` calls **once per render of the list with all its records**; a row's component only reads its entry from the hash. That is what keeps a list at the same number of statements for 3 rows and for 30 (`test/components/admin/pages_screen/tree_component_test.rb`: "the tree with notes and people on every page costs the same queries for 3 pages as for 30, and marks each row with both"; `test/components/admin/posts_screen/table_component_test.rb`: "the table with notes and people on every post costs the same queries for 3 posts as for 30, and marks each row with both").

**The host components do not name the features in their slots**: no word "note" in the code or comments of the eleven files in `SLOT_HOSTS` of `test/lib/notes_boundary_test.rb` ("the editors' header and the media panel do not name notes: they draw their slots"). The *views* that assemble a screen may (they hand the drawer's counts through), and a list of **named exceptions**, each with the trigger that would make it a slot, is in the same test: the block card's marker and menu item and the section tile's (a slot when a second feature puts something on a block, which is block-level presence, step 3), the dashboard's cards, the sidebar's badge, the mind graph's count and nodes, the media tile's chip and the grid's read, and the posts table's Notes column *header* (a column mechanism for lists is more than two features justify).

### What was not extracted, and the trigger

- **Deletion hooks.** `Notes::Remove` stays a direct call from `Trash::Purge` and `ContentBlocks::Destroy`, listed in `test/lib/notes_boundary_test.rb`. Presence has nothing to clean up (its rows expire). Trigger: edit reservations.
- **A "session ended" event.** A session's end already closes the cable connection, and a closed connection removes the person's rows. One user, so it stays `Session`'s own callback.
- **The places in the named-exceptions list above**, each with its trigger.
- **A column mechanism for lists.** A second feature that wants a *column*, not a marker.

## In tests

Presence's system tests are two Capybara sessions (`using_session`); time is moved by `backdate` and the clock, not by waiting. `await_notes_live` waits for presence's subscriptions as well as notes' (its parts are in `live/state`). The system suite is sensitive to machine load: macOS background services (Software Update, Spotlight, photo analysis) at a load average of 80 to 110 made two whole runs worthless on 2026-10-10, with failures that were the machine's. Check `sysctl -n vm.loadavg` before a run; a run that takes twice its usual time is not a result.

## What is not built

Presence on the dashboard, in the mind graph and in the media grid; the block-level label ("Dana is editing this block", step 3, with reservations); any per-person setting. The gaps the build left are on the roadmap, with their reasons: no rate limit on `report` and `beat`; the two narrow heal races; "away N min" restarting at a reconnect; no vertical collision check in the tooltip; a subscription not re-judged when rights change; checked by hand only on Chrome (Safari, idle detection on a second real machine, reduced motion, a real back/forward-cache restoration and a throttled hidden tab's keep-alives are by hand).
