The spec is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-11-live-editing-design.md` and the look is the Design System's live-editing cards. This file describes the code and the incidents behind its rules. Live editing is the third user of the seams notes and presence share (`docs/reference/presence.md`, "The seams"), and it sits on the write queue (`docs/reference/save-rule-and-write-queue.md`): read both before changing it.

## What it is, and is not

Two things, in one namespace (`Editing`).

**Live refresh:** what someone saves appears in every other open editor of that page or post, without a reload. The server says *that* something changed; the editor fetches the part and merges it around whatever the person has not saved yet.

**Edit reservations:** a block, a page's settings or a post that someone is changing is theirs for `Editing::HOLD` (2 minutes) after their last saved change, read-only for everyone else, who can take it over. A reservation is soft: nobody is locked out by a colleague who went to lunch.

It is **not co-editing**: one person types in a block at a time. It is not presence either (`docs/reference/presence.md` says who has a record open; a reservation says who is changing one thing on it), and neither knows the other. A library image is not held and is not refreshed. A write through the MCP server holds nothing and is not refused by a hold.

What can be held is a **subject**: a `ContentBlock` (a section's own settings form is its block's), a `Page` (which means its settings form, not its blocks) or a `Post` as a whole. `Editing.subject_of(record, block_id)` turns a request into one (the record itself without a block id, a block of that page with one, nil for anything else, which the caller answers 404) and `Editing.key(subject)` is its name in the page and in a request: `block-<id>` or `record` (`test/models/editing_test.rb`: "a request names the record itself without a block id, a block of this page with one, and nothing else", "a subject's name in the page").

The four numbers are constants of `Editing`: `HOLD` (2 minutes), `PASS_OVER` (5 seconds, how long a take-over waits for the holder's tab), `NOTICE` (6 seconds, how long "Updated by …" and "Taken over by …" stay) and `SWEEP_AFTER` (10 minutes) (`test/models/editing_test.rb`: "the timings are ordered: a pass-over comes before a hold ends, and the sweep after"). The scripts hold none of these four: `Admin::EditorScreen::LiveRegionComponent` renders them as values. What the scripts do hold is the timings that are theirs alone and that no server rule depends on: `LOST_FOR` (30 s, how long a hand-over is remembered for the "Taken over by …" text) and `ANNOUNCE_AFTER` (150 ms) in the hold script, the 25 ms of its two waiting loops, `BACKOFF` (1, 3, 9 times the `retry` value) in the live script, and the defaults of the values `timeout` and `retry` (the live script also repeats `NOTICE` as the default of its `notice` value, which the region always sets).

## The signal

`Editing::Signals` (`app/models/editing/signals.rb`) decides what is said and to whom, and sends through `Live::Signals`, the one sender.

**The source is the audit log.** `Audit.record` publishes a notification once the outermost transaction has committed, also for an event coalesced into an earlier one, and `config/initializers/editing.rb` subscribes `Editing::Signals.heard` to it (by the notification's string, so no application constant loads while Rails boots). `heard` maps the event's **action** to a signal, so no content service names this feature, and a write through the MCP server or the console is covered like one from CS Studio. The only thing a content service does for it is `ContentBlocks::Destroy` writing the deleted block's `page_id` into the metadata of `block.deleted`: the row is gone when the notification runs (`test/services/content_blocks/destroy_test.rb`: "the deleted block's event names its page").

**`MAP` and `SILENT`.** `MAP` gives an action its reason: `saved` (a page's or post's update, publish, unpublish, schedule, move, translation confirmation, and the three of publishing: `changes_published`, `changes_discarded` and `scheduled_change_cancelled`; a block's update and translation confirmation), `structure` (a block created, deleted or reordered) or `gone` (a page or post trashed or purged). `SILENT` names every other action with the reason it sends nothing (a created or restored record has no open editor; library images and site settings have no live editor; a user, a group, a token, a note, a session, a language and the other actions of `Audit::Sentence::SPECIAL_ACTIONS` are not a change to a page's or a post's content; an AI translation writes nothing; a take-over sends its own signal; a tag's rename, delete and merge are a known gap, below). Together they hold every action in `Audit::Sentence::ACTIONS`: **a new action must be placed in one of them**, and `test/models/editing/signals_test.rb` fails for one neither knows ("every action the app records is mapped or silent, with a reason, and none is both"). At run time an action no list knows is reported through `Rails.error` and sends nothing ("an action no list knows is reported and sends nothing").

**What a signal carries:** `{ kind: "content", content_block_id:, reason:, origin: }`, never text. `content_block_id` is the block's id for a plain save of a block of a page and nil for everything else: the record itself, any `structure`, any `gone`. Two refinements, both found in review. (1) A save that folded a section's columns redraws the list, so it is `structure`; but a later plain save of the same section is *coalesced into* that audit row, whose metadata still holds `folded_block_ids`, so the fold counts only on a row with `occurrences` of 1 ("a section's fold signals structure", "a plain save of a section after its fold is a save with the block's id, not a structure"). (2) Trashing a branch is one event on its root with `descendant_ids`; an editor open on a child page would hear nothing, so `gone` is sent on each descendant's stream too ("trashing a branch tells the editor of each child page too"). A block of a post has no editor of its own and no signal.

**A change of holder** is the second kind: `Editing::Signals.holder(record, subject, reason)` sends `{ kind: "holder", content_block_id:, reason:, origin: }` with the reason `claimed`, `released`, `requested` or `taken`. A renewal, a lapse and a withdrawal send nothing.

**The stream** is `editing:record:<Type>-<id>` of the page or post (`Editing::Signals.record_stream`). **`origin`** is the tab that caused the write, so that tab skips its own echo. It is read from `Current.editing_tab`, which `Admin::BaseController` sets for every CS Studio request from the header `X-Notes-Tab` in the shape `Live::Signals::TAB` allows; a junk id leaves the key out (`test/models/editing/signals_test.rb`: "the tab that made the change is named, and a junk id is left out"; `test/integration/admin/editing_signals_test.rb`: "a well-formed tab id is the origin of the signal a save causes, and a malformed one gives none"). **It is not `Current.notes_tab`**, which only the notes controllers set: a content request that set that one would put an origin on `Notes::Remove`'s signal, and the tab that deleted a block would skip the refresh of its own open notes (`test/models/notes/signals_test.rb`: "deleting a block signals its page, whose drawer is still open, when the delete commits"). The header keeps the name it had when only notes sent it.

**`heard` never raises.** It runs inside the request that made the write, after the commit: an exception would turn a saved block into an error page. A failure is logged by the action and the error's class and reported ("nothing is sent for a write that is rolled back, and a signal that fails does not fail the write").

**Every editor write carries the tab header.** A write sent without it is nobody's: it claims nothing and its own tab fetches what it has just drawn. Turbo's requests get it from one `turbo:before-fetch-request` listener in `app/javascript/editing/requests.js`; the three hand-built writes (the autosave, the block reorder, the page tree's move) add it themselves from `TAB_HEADER` (`test/lib/editing_tab_header_test.rb`: "each hand-built write of an editor sends the tab header", "Turbo's own requests get the header from one listener"; `test/system/admin/editing_live_test.rb`: "my own save draws no mark and fetches nothing", "my own reorder and my own Turbo form fetch and draw nothing").

## The channel

`Editing::Channel` is a `Live::RecordChannel`: the record is found through the read scope at subscribe time, for the user as they are then, and a record out of scope, a trashed one, an unknown kind and a bad id are rejected alike. It takes `page` and `post` only; a library image is rejected before anything is looked up, because nothing is ever signalled for one. It offers a client no action and writes nothing: both hooks are private (`test/channels/editing/channel_test.rb`: "a reader is subscribed to the record's stream, built from the record that was found", "a record out of scope, a trashed one, an unknown kind and a bad id are rejected alike", "a library image has no stream to subscribe to", "the channel offers a client no action").

## The three fetches

`Admin::EditingController` (a class, never `Admin::Editing`, which would shadow `Editing::`) answers what an open editor fetches. The routes hang under `editing/:kind/:id` with `kind` only `page|post`:

| Request | Answers |
|---|---|
| `GET editing/:kind/:id/blocks` (`admin_editing_blocks_path`) | 200: the children of `#content-blocks-list`, as the page view draws them. A post has no list: 404 |
| `GET editing/:kind/:id/blocks/:block_id` (`admin_editing_block_path`) | 200: the elements that are this block: its card; or, in a section on the default language's tab, its tile and its panel; or, in a section on a translation tab, its card of the section's group. **204** for a block that is drawn as a section (`SectionCardComponent.draws?`): its card holds other blocks' forms, so the caller fetches the list. **410** for a block this page's audit log says was deleted, with the notice as its body (below). 404 for a block of another page and one that never existed. With `mark=1` the card carries "Updated by …" |
| `GET editing/:kind/:id/record` (`admin_editing_record_path`) | 200: the record's own form: a page's settings panel; or a post's form with its notice area (`form_only: true`, without the header above it, which nothing fetches) |

Every part is found through the read scope (`Admin::RecordOfKind`) and drawn for its viewer as they are now: a record out of scope, in the trash or of a kind with no editor is a 404, never a 403. No answer is kept by any cache (`Cache-Control: no-store`), a 404 included. A fragment is drawn by the very components, with the very arguments, that `pages/show.html.erb` and `posts/show.html.erb` draw the editor with, because the browser swaps one element for the other and the ids and marks have to be the page's own (`test/integration/admin/editing_test.rb`: "outside the read scope, in the trash, of another page and of an unknown kind every part is not found", "every answer, a 404 included, is kept by no cache", "a top-level block is its card; a block in a section is its tile and its panel; a section is 204", "every fragment is the element the editor's own page draws", "the same fragment is the element the editor's own page draws when a block, a section's block and the settings are held", "a reader gets the parts, view-only"; `test/integration/trash_visibility_test.rb`: "a trashed page's and a trashed post's editing parts are not served"). The list costs the same statements at any size (`test/integration/admin/editing_test.rb`: "the list costs the same statements for 3 blocks as for 30").

**The 410.** A block that is not this page's and one that never existed are a 404; a block the audit log says was deleted from this page is gone, and the body is the notice the hold script shows under the text of someone who was still typing in it: "Dana Weiss deleted this block. Your changes weren't saved." It is asked of `Audit::Event.deleting_block(block_id, page_id)`: the block's own `block.deleted` event, **or the section's that took it along** (`deleted_block_ids`), on that page, within the last 7 days (an editor open longer is told the plain sentence). A block kept when its section was removed ("Remove section, keep blocks") is not gone ("a block deleted since is gone, not missing, and the answer is the notice naming who deleted it", "a block deleted together with its section is gone too, with the same notice: the section's event lists it", "a block kept when its section was removed is not gone, and a block of another page or never made is a 404"; `test/models/audit/event_test.rb`: "deleting_block finds the event that took a block away: its own, or the section's that took it along, on that page only").

**The "Updated by" mark.** `Editing::LastChange.for(block, viewer:)` reads the block's newest audit event that is a change (`Dashboard::Feed::NOT_A_CHANGE` leaves out a note and a take-over). **The newest change decides**: if it was made by the viewer, or without a user (a token, a job), nobody is named, and an older person's change is never named in its place (`test/queries/editing/last_change_test.rb`: "the newest change decides: one made without a user, or by the viewer, leaves no one to name"; `test/integration/admin/editing_test.rb`: "with mark=1 the card says who changed it, and never for the viewer's own change").

## The marks in the markup

The server's markup is the scripts' only source for addresses, wording and the four timings above: they hold no address and no wording, and no number but their own waits (listed under "What it is, and is not").

- **`#editing`** is the element both scripts stand on (`Admin::EditorScreen::LiveRegionComponent`, `data-controller="admin--editing--live admin--editing--hold"`), rendered with `data-live-part="waiting"`. On a page it wraps the rail and the blocks, not the header. The rail's Notes panel stands inside it but in no unit and no part, so live editing's merges never reach it: what replaces anything in the notes is notes' own merge (`docs/reference/notes.md`, "Live delivery"). On a post it wraps `PostFormComponent`, which draws the header itself, so the post's header is inside the region. It carries the addresses (`blocks-url`, `block-url` with `:block_id` to substitute, `record-url`, `claim-url`, `release-url`, `take-over-url`, `hand-over-url`, `withdraw-url`), the times in milliseconds and `HeldFields.rules` as JSON (`test/integration/admin/editing_test.rb`: "the live region carries the addresses the scripts need, and nothing for blocks on a post").
- **`data-editing-unit`**: what is replaced or kept as one. A block's card in each of its three placements (in the list, as a section's panel, in a translation tab's group), a section's own settings **form**, the page settings panel (`details#page-settings`) and a post's form (`#post-form`).
- **`data-editing-part`**: a section's tile. Not a unit: it holds no form.
- **`data-editing-subject`**: `block-<id>` or `record`, on every unit and on a tile. The subject, not the element's id, says which block an element is: a tile and a top-level card of one block have the same id, and a panel and a card have the same inner ids.
- **`data-editing-version`**: below.
- **`data-editing-held`**: someone else holds this subject, for a reader as for an editor (the strip is shown to both). Whether the form is frozen is a separate fact: `fieldset.admin-held` for one rendered read-only, `data-editing-frozen="true"` for one frozen in place by the script. A script that asks "can this form be typed in" asks those, not `data-editing-held`.
- **`data-editing-gone="true"`** is written by the merge on a kept unit whose block the answer does not have, and **`data-editing-discard`** by the live script on a unit whose refused text was given up.
- **The strip's slot** (`data-editing-strip-slot`): always there, empty unless the subject is held: `#block-<id>-hold` between a card's header and its form (and a section's, between its head and the settings region), `#page-hold` at the top of the settings panel, and for a post `#editor-notice`, the header's notice area it shares with the "Changed elsewhere" bar (`HoldStripComponent.slot_id`). Beside each stands a polite live region, `[data-editing-announcer]`.
- **The strip** is `data-admin--editing--hold-target="strip"` with `data-editing-subject` and `data-editing-kind` (`held`, `lost` or `deleted`); a `held` strip also carries `data-editing-expires-in` (milliseconds until the hold lapses, never below 0), `data-editing-holder` (the holder's user id), `data-editing-taken-text` ("Taken over by Dana Weiss · just now") and `data-editing-announce` ("Dana Weiss took over this block").
- **The mark place** (`data-editing-mark-place`) in a card's header holds `[data-editing-mark]`: the colleague's avatar and "Updated by …" when the answer was asked for with `mark=1`, or "Taken over by you · just now" after this tab's take-over (a template of the live region that `editing/marks.js` clones into the block's own card or panel). While a mark stands the card glows in the people hue, except a held card.

**`Editing.version(subject, hold:, take_over:)`** is what a unit or part says about what it shows: a block's or a post's `updated_at`, or a page's `settings_changed_at`, in microseconds; and, when a reservation makes the unit read-only for this viewer, who holds it, in which tab, since when, and whether the viewer may take it over. A card adds whether it is the first or the last of the list (its Move up and Move down start disabled accordingly) and a panel adds its section and column, because a move along a section is written with `update_columns` and leaves `updated_at` alone. **Every version is written through this method**, which one place calls (`Admin::EditorScreen::HeldUnit#editing_version`; a card adds its place to that), so what else a unit's look depends on is added in one place (`test/lib/editing_guards_test.rb`: "every data-editing-version in the app is written from a method that calls Editing.version"; a source grep, which does not see a version built elsewhere and handed in through a variable). It does **not** cover what a unit shows of other records or of the viewer (their rights, a library image's file, a tag's name, an ancestor's slug).

**Why a catch-up draws only what differs.** The first build replaced everything on every fetch. An editor fetches the list and the record's form once whenever its subscription is confirmed, so every clean card was replaced, every rich-text editor mounted again and any menu the person had just opened closed, after every page load. Now such a fetch is a *catch-up* (`onlyChanged`): a clean unit or part whose version equals the answer's is left as the element it is. The cost: a unit whose look depends on something its version does not cover is missed by a catch-up, though never by a signal's fetch (`test/system/admin/editing_merge_test.rb`: "a catch-up leaves a card of the same version as it is, with its menu open, and replaces one that changed", "after a catch-up a panel's heading names the column the block is in now, although only its place changed", "without the option every clean card is replaced, as before").

## The merge

`app/javascript/editing/merge.js` owns one rule and knows no controller, opens no connection, fetches nothing and raises no toast:

> A form that holds changes not yet saved is never replaced, and neither is anything else of its unit. Everything else is replaced by what was fetched.

"Unsaved" is the caller's question (`unsaved(form)`); the module is handed a predicate, so its tests hand it a plain one. `mergeBlockList(list, fresh, …)` reconciles the block list; `mergeUnits(root, fresh, …)` replaces the elements a one-block or a record answer names; `anchored(root, draw)` holds the scroll. Its tests are `test/system/admin/editing_merge_test.rb`.

**The mechanics are the merge core it shares with notes** (`app/javascript/live/keep_merge.js`, `keepMerge(container, fresh, { kept, within, sync, place })`, which answers `{ orphans }`; its own tests are the "keepMerge" cases of `test/system/admin/editor_modules_test.rb`). The core knows no feature: it draws an answer around a set of elements that stay, `kept`, matched by id level by level, reconciling inside an element with a twin for which `within(element, twin)` is true (attributes through `sync`), and leaving an element the answer lacks that is kept or `within(element, null)` as an orphan after its nearest preceding sibling the answer has (or before `place(orphan, fresh)`). This module's `reconcile` says what is kept: the units that hold unsaved text, and the catch-up's `same`, the caller's held parts and the notices, each of those three only where the answer has it in the same container, so a clean element is never left behind as an orphan; its `within` asks for an element with a twin whether it holds any of those, and for one without a twin whether it holds a unit with unsaved text. The orphans are what it marks gone. Notes call the same core with one kept element, the new-note field (`docs/reference/notes.md`, "Live delivery").

**It decides by ids, subjects, versions and state, never by comparing HTML.** A fetched fragment is not byte-equal to what is on screen even when nothing changed: every form carries a fresh masked CSRF token, and a mounted editor has added its own elements to the one on screen.

**A kept unit is the same element afterwards, and is not moved**, under someone who is typing: the list is reconciled around it by the core above, and an ancestor of a kept unit (a section's card, its header, its body, its panels) stays and is reconciled inside. **That is why every ancestor of a unit up to the list has an id**: the core matches by id, level by level. An ancestor without one fails quietly: the core takes it for something the answer does not have, keeps it, and marks the unit inside `data-editing-gone`, so a person is led into the "deleted block" flow for a block that exists. The markup is held by `test/integration/admin/editing_test.rb`, "every ancestor of every unit and part up to the block list, and of the record's form up to the region, has an id": the editor's page on the default language's tab and on a translation tab, a post's, and the fetched list, with a top-level card, a section's own form, a tile, a panel and a group's card. It reads what the server draws; an id a script took away afterwards it would not see. ("a card that holds unsaved text is the same element with its text after a list merge, and a clean one is replaced", "one kept card while the others are reordered around it is never taken out of the document", "a section's own unsaved setting stays while its lanes take a colleague's new tile".)

**A section's settings region stays open through a merge.** A section's form stands in `#section-<id>-settings` under the card's head (`docs/reference/content-blocks-and-types.md`, "Sections in the editor"). `merge.js` (`withState`) keeps an open region open and its words `aria-expanded="true"`, as it keeps an open panel, or a colleague's catch-up would close the settings under someone typing; a hold's `reveal` opens a closed region that holds the subject's unit through the card's cancelable `admin--editor--section:open-settings` door (`test/system/admin/editing_holds_test.rb`, "a tie on a section's settings closed meanwhile opens the region again, with the notice and the frozen form"). A frozen block card or panel is itself a kept unit (its autosave is paused, so its form always counts as unsaved): a merge never synchronises its attributes, and it keeps the `data-card-edge="held"` `freeze.js` wrote; no test holds that a merge leaves a frozen card's edge alone. A section's frame is not a unit (its settings form is), so a merge does synchronise it, `syncAttributes` giving it its server twin's edge or none: `data-card-edge` is not in `merge.js`'s `RUNTIME` list (`data-notes-active`, `data-chosen` and `data-editing-away` are) and stays out of it. A frozen section's held edge comes from the stylesheet instead, `.admin-section-card:where(:has(> .admin-section-card__settings form[data-editing-frozen="true"]))`, which a frozen form keeps through any merge (`test/lib/admin_design_rules_test.rb`, "a section whose settings form is frozen draws the held edge from the form, under the unavailable edge"; `test/system/admin/editing_holds_test.rb`, "a tie on a section's own settings…"). The "lost" strip says the same in words.

The exceptions the rule grew, each from a review finding:

- **Gone, and its place.** The answer has no element of a kept unit's subject: the block was deleted. The unit stays with its text, marked `data-editing-gone="true"` (taken back if a later answer has the element again), where it was: after the nearest preceding sibling the answer still has, else first, and two gone units keep their order. Only a unit that holds unsaved text can be gone; a clean element the answer lacks is simply removed ("a block deleted while its form holds unsaved text stays, marked gone", "a gone card keeps its place among the cards that are redrawn, and two gone cards keep their order", "a clean element of the same version under a level the answer lacks is removed with it, never marked gone", "a unit that is marked gone loses the mark when the answer has it again; a unit without an id that holds text is kept").
- **A hidden panel is shown** when it is a kept unit whose tile is not on screen, or nothing on screen would lead to the form that holds the person's text. `admin--editor--section` does not count such a panel as "the open one": opening another tile does not close it, and only its own Close button and Escape inside it do ("a hidden panel whose block was deleted is shown, and reported gone", "a panel shown because its tile is gone stays shown when another tile is pressed, and closing the other leaves it").
- **Displaced (`moved`).** The answer has a kept unit's subject somewhere else (a card moved into a section or out of one, a panel moved to another section) or in another shape (card against tile and panel). The first build drew the block in its new place as well: two forms of one block, the same ids twice, and the old one flagged gone. Now **a block is one subject and has one form on screen**: the unit stays where it is, is not marked gone, and *every* element of that subject is removed from the answer before drawing. It is reported in `moved` (a subset of `kept`), and the caller fetches the list again once that unit's form holds nothing unsaved, with no notice. A panel moved to another lane of its own section is home and simply kept. `mergeUnits` sees one block's answer, which names no container, so it reports `moved` only for a change of shape ("a kept card whose block was moved into a section stays where it is, is not gone, and the block has one form", "a kept panel whose block was moved out to the top level stays, is shown, is not gone, and the block has one form", "a kept panel whose block was moved to another section stays, is not gone, and the block has one form and one way in", "a one-block answer displaces nothing, whatever the root it is merged into").
- **`same`.** In a catch-up, a clean unit or part of the same version is the same element afterwards, if every element above it up to the root has an id; if the answer puts it elsewhere among its siblings it is still moved there ("a catch-up puts a card of the same version in its new place among its siblings, and does not replace it").
- **A part the caller holds** (`hold(part)`): a tile whose own menu or delete dialog is open is left as it is and never marked gone ("a part the caller holds is left as it is, and the part is not marked gone").
- **A notice nobody has dismissed.** A part that shows an alert with something in it is not replaced, and in a list merge a strip slot that shows one stays as it is wherever it stands: a section's slot is outside its unit, and the answer's empty or "held" slot must not take away what the person has still to copy or discard ("a part that shows an alert is not replaced"; `test/system/admin/editing_holds_test.rb`: "a tie on a section's own settings: the notice with Copy and Discard survives the winner's signals and the list merge").
- **What a script moved away (`away`).** An element of the list that a script moved out of it (a section's settings region, shown in the rail) carries `data-editing-away` and its own id, and the live script hands every such element of the region to `mergeBlockList` (`away: [...this.element.querySelectorAll("[data-editing-away]")]`). A merge never moves a unit, so it never puts one back: putting the region under its head before a merge and into the rail after it would take its form out of the document twice for every list fetch, at moments a colleague chooses, under someone typing. Instead every element of that id is taken out of the answer before anything is drawn (the first is the away element's twin), so the list gets no second copy, and the away element is merged where it stands, as an ancestor of a kept unit is: its attributes synced but for `hidden` (its owner shows it) and `RUNTIME`, its children reconciled with the same kept, same and held sets. The kept units, a catch-up's `same` and what the person did to the screen (`withState`: the focus, an open region, the chosen section) cover the away elements as they cover the list. An away element whose id the answer lacks (its section is gone) is left in place: a kept unit in it is marked gone, as everywhere, and one with none is returned in `awayGone`, which the live script puts in its `admin--editing--live:merged` event's detail for the script that moved it to remove. The away rule stays in `editing/merge.js`, on top of the core and not inside it, because it is live editing's alone (an element merged where it stands against a twin taken out of the answer before anything is drawn is not a mechanic notes have): each away element is one more call of the same `reconcile`, against its twin. The three steps are named helpers side by side in `editing/merge.js`, kept together: `takeTwins` (every copy of an away id out of the answer, the first as the twin), `mergeAway` (one away element against its twin, or gone) and `mergeMoved` (exported: the same rule for one element against a twin a script hands it, with nothing kept, which `admin--editor--section-choice#streamed` uses when a stream redraws the chosen card, so a clean region in the rail takes the redrawn card's values and its base and its next save is not refused as stale; one holding text is left as it is). `mergeUnits` takes no `away`: a section's own answer is a 204 and the list says what is true ("an away region with no unsaved text is replaced where it stands, and the list gets no copy", "an away region holding unsaved text is the same element afterwards, with its text, its focus and its caret", "an away region whose section the answer lacks is returned in awayGone when clean, and kept with its unit marked gone when unsaved", "a catch-up leaves an away region of the same version alone", "an answer that holds an away id twice draws neither copy in the list", "a clean field with the focus in an away region is replaced, and the focus and the selection come back to it").
- **A part that holds a kept unit** cannot be replaced without taking the unit away: it is reported `missing`, and the caller fetches the list ("a part that holds a kept unit is not replaced and is reported missing, so the caller fetches the list").
- **The unit with the focus is never the one moved.** The one time kept units are moved is when two of them changed places, and then what stands in the focused one's way is moved instead ("when two kept cards change places the one with the focus is not the one moved, and it still takes typing", "when two kept cards change places and the focus is in the other one, that one is not moved either").

**What the person did to the screen is put back** around a merge: which cards are collapsed, which panel of a section is open (and its tile's `aria-expanded`), which section is chosen (`data-chosen`, put back by id on the card that replaced a clean one, and in `RUNTIME` beside `data-editing-away` for a card that stays), and where the focus is. **A clean form that has the focus is still replaced**, because a hold by someone else must be able to make it read-only. The focus goes back to the same control once it exists (an editor mounts a moment after its card arrives, so this waits up to 3 seconds, until the person focuses or presses somewhere else), and an `input` or `textarea` gets its selection back. In a rich-text field the caret and the selection are lost ("a clean text field that has the focus is replaced, and the focus and the selection come back to the same field", "a focus that waits for a field to mount is not given to it once the person has gone somewhere else", "the open panel of a section is open after a merge and its tile says so; a collapsed card stays collapsed", "a chosen section's card keeps data-chosen through a merge", "a kept chosen card keeps data-chosen, never taken off and put back").

**The scroll correction.** `anchored` keeps the block the person is in, or else the first one in view, where it is on screen: at once, and again on each animation frame for up to 1.5 seconds, while the cards redrawn above it mount their editors and grow. It ends for good as soon as the scroller is anywhere but where the correction last put it (a key, the wheel, the browser following the caret, any script), when the time is up, or when `anchored` is called again: one correction at a time. The first version corrected once and the block still moved when the editors above it mounted; the second fought the person's own scrolling ("the block being read does not move on screen when a card is added above it", "a scroll made by anything else while the correction runs is not undone", "two merges in a row leave one correction, and the block ends where the second found it").

## The live script

`admin--editing--live` (`app/javascript/controllers/admin/editing/live_controller.js`) listens on the record's stream, fetches with a plain GET and hands the answer to the merge. It writes nothing, sends nothing through the write queue and uses no Turbo request, visit, frame or stream (`test/system/admin/editing_live_test.rb`: "live refresh uses no Turbo request: no visit, no frame load, no stream").

**What a signal leads to.** A signal from this tab (`origin`) is skipped. `gone` fetches the record. `structure` fetches the list. `saved` fetches the block with `mark=1`, or the record when the signal names no block. A `holder` signal is first handed to the hold script (the event `admin--editing--live:holder`) and then fetches the same part, the block's or the record's, without a mark: what the part says about its holder is drawn by the server for this viewer. A kind it does not know is ignored.

**One GET at a time.** The first build let a list fetch and a block fetch run side by side and counted "newest wins" per part: an older list answer was drawn over a newer block answer, which showed a colleague's old text on an old base, and the next save of that card was refused. Now the script has at most one GET on its way. What is asked meanwhile is owed and coalesced: two asks for one part are one, the list swallows the blocks asked beside it (it contains them), and a block ask folded into a list ask keeps its mark by being fetched with `mark=1` after the list is drawn. Reads are drawn in the order they were sent ("a slow list answer and a newer block answer end with the newest content and the newest base on screen", "a slow block answer and a newer list answer end with the newest content and the newest list", "two asks for one block with the first answer slow: both are drawn, in order, the newest last", "a block's mark is not lost when its ask is folded into a list ask").

**An answer is drawn only if it is the newest thing this tab knows.**

- A read that has not been sent yet is skipped when something newer was asked for the same content. One already on the wire is drawn (it is newer than the screen) and the newer ask goes out next; dropping it would let a colleague who saves every second starve the block.
- A read waits until this tab's write queue is quiet (`quiet()` in `saving/queue_core.js`: nothing queued or in flight and no form with an edit still to send; it takes no turn, counts as no write and tells no form to send early). An answer during whose flight this tab queued a write (`writeCount()` moved) is dropped and asked for again: it was read before that write ("an answer fetched before this tab's own write was queued is not drawn").
- The list and the blocks are not fetched or drawn while a block is in someone's hand: `admin--editor--blocks` sends the event `admin--editor--blocks:moving`, true while *any* of a drag, a keyboard pick-up or a move that has not been answered is open, false only when none is. It is a state judged again at every place one of the three ends (holds overlap: a second drag begins before the first move is answered), not an edge that is relayed. What was owed is paid when the last one is over ("what a colleague saved during a drag is drawn after an accepted drop", "a keyboard pick-up that is put back says so: what was owed is drawn then, and not before", "a pick-up made before an earlier move was answered keeps the list from being redrawn under it", "putBack lets go of a keyboard pick-up, and the list that was owed is drawn", "a lane destroyed under a drag lets go of it, and the list that was owed is drawn").

**A hidden document fetches nothing.** It owes the list and the record and pays once when it is seen ("a hidden tab fetches nothing, and catches up once when it is seen").

**Catching up.** Whenever the subscription is confirmed, the first time and after every reconnect, the list and the record's form are fetched once as a catch-up: a signal sent while the socket was down was not heard. A subscription that is **rejected** (the viewer's access ended) fetches the record once, so that its 404 says so ("a confirmed subscription fetches the list and the record's form once", "after the socket drops and comes back the editor catches up", "a subscription that is rejected fetches the record once, and its 404 says the page is gone").

**The predicate.** What the merge is told is unsaved: a form whose autosave controller is not `clean()`, or has something `outstanding()`, or is not `unchanged()` (the write queue's own three questions); and a unit whose block has a menu or a dialog open: a menu of its card or tile, a dialog inside it, and the layout's rich-text link dialog and media picker when they work for a field of it (they stand outside every unit and know their opener). A unit kept only for a menu or a dialog is fetched again when the last of them closes, by a listener on each element that holds it, with no polling; a watcher whose elements have all left the document is ended and the block fetched again. A unit marked `data-editing-discard` is nobody's to keep ("a card whose menu is open is kept while it is open, and refreshed when it closes", "a card with the rich-text dialog open for it is kept while it is open, and refreshed when it closes", "a card kept for one dialog is refreshed when the LAST of several dialogs on it closes", "a tile whose menu is open is kept while it is open, and block and tile are refreshed when it closes").

**Retries, the timeout, and when the part says "answered".** A read gives up after the `timeout` value (10 seconds). A failed read (nothing came back, a 5xx, or a 4xx other than 404 and 410) is owed and asked again after 1, 3 and 9 times the `retry` value (1 second); after that it waits for the next signal, connect or `visibilitychange`. A drawing that throws is reported to the console and does not stop the line. The region's `data-live-part` is `answered` once the subscription is confirmed or rejected **and** that first read has been attempted (drawn, dropped for a newer one, failed or timed out), and `waiting` while the connection is down; a test waits for it, so a part that could stay `waiting` behind a slow read would hang the suite ("a read that fails is asked again, and the part has answered meanwhile", "a read that never comes back times out, the part answers, and it is asked again").

**A 404.** For the record or the list it means the record is gone or not the viewer's to read: the script lets its stream go, drops what was waiting and appends the sentence that the page was moved to the trash, deleted or cannot be opened, with the way back to the header's notice area, once; a "Changed elsewhere" bar already there stays. One block's 404 may only be a block this page never had: the list is fetched and says which ("a page trashed under me says so and offers the way back, and the part has answered", "the missing bar is appended: a Changed elsewhere bar already in the notice area stays").

**The "Updated" mark** stays for `NOTICE` and fades over 200ms; with reduced motion it is removed without a fade. It is not announced. An added, moved or deleted block gets no mark and no motion ("the mark stays for the notice, then the fade starts, and the mark is gone", "with reduced motion the mark goes when its time is up, without fading", "an added block appears, a moved one snaps to its place and a deleted one goes, with no mark").

**A displaced unit** costs one in-page check a second and no request until its form holds nothing unsaved; then the list is fetched once and the block takes its place ("a block moved into a section while I have unsent text stays, and takes its place once, after my save").

**The editors carry `turbo-cache-control: no-cache`**, so no snapshot of one is kept and nothing these scripts wrote into the page comes back with Back.

## Reservations

**The table.** `edit_reservations` (`Editing::Reservation`): `subject_type` and `subject_id`, unique together; `record_type` and `record_id`, the page or post whose stream a change of holder is signalled on, indexed for the one read a screen makes; `user_id` (cascading) and `tab`, the tab's opaque id; `changed_at`, the holder's last saved change or claim; and for a pending take-over `requested_by_id` (nullified when that person is deleted), `requested_tab` and `requested_at`. It has no timestamps and **no foreign key to its subject**: a deleted block's row stays until it expires. A request exists only while `requested_by_id` is set.

**One cutoff.** A row is live while `changed_at > now - HOLD` and counts as no row from that instant on, whatever the sweep has done. The claim takes a row whose `changed_at <= now - HOLD`: the same boundary from both sides, so at that instant exactly one reading holds ("a row is live until HOLD after its last change: one instant before it is refused, at it the row is taken"). The clock is the caller's (`now:`), handed to the statement as a value and never the database's `NOW()`.

**The statements.** Each is one statement on the unique index, runs no callback and writes nothing but its own row: no `touch`, nothing of the record, no audit event, no content version (each says `# version: none (a reservation is not content)` for `test/lib/version_bump_test.rb`) (`test/models/editing/reservation_test.rb`: "every statement is one statement, takes no lock and writes nothing but its own row").

| Statement | Does | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| `claim(subject:, record:, user:, tab:)` | inserts the row, or takes an expired one, or renews the tab's own; a live row of another tab is left as it is | `:taken`, `:renewed` or `:refused` |
| `release(subject:, user:, tab:)` | deletes the row of that user and tab | whether it deleted one |
| `request(subject:, by:, tab:)` | writes a take-over request on a live row of another tab, where one may be written | `:requested`, `:pending` or `:none` |
| `withdraw(subject:, by:, tab:)` | clears that tab's own request, and no other's | whether it cleared one |
| `hand_over(subject:, user:, tab:)` | the holder's tab lets go: the row becomes the requester's | whether it passed |
| `pass_over(subject:, by:, tab:)` | the requester takes a row whose request is `PASS_OVER` old | whether it passed |
| `holder_of(subject)` | reads the live row with its user | the row or nil |

**`CLAIM`** is `INSERT … ON CONFLICT (subject_type, subject_id) DO UPDATE … WHERE … RETURNING`, raw SQL because no Arel node says it. The `WHERE` of the update lets it through for an expired row or the tab's own; Postgres re-checks it against the newest version of the row after waiting for a concurrent writer, which is what makes two simultaneous claims of one expired row end with exactly one holder. The answer `renewed` needs two things: the row as it stood when the statement began was this tab's and live (a CTE reads the statement's snapshot), **and** this statement updated it rather than inserted it (`xmax <> 0`). The second matters when the tab's own release commits between the snapshot and the insert: the row is then new, the answer is `taken` and the others hear `claimed`. A pending request survives the holder's own renewal and goes with an expired row. That it is one statement is what the two-connection tests hold: the reviewer ran a read followed by an upsert through the same test and it let two holders through in 600 of 600 runs ("two tabs claiming a free subject at once: one takes it, the other is refused, and there is one row", "an expired row claimed by its holder and by a stranger at once: exactly one holds afterwards", "a claim that inserts is taken, even from the tab whose own release it raced: the release commits between its snapshot and its insert", "the holder's renewal keeps a pending request; a claim of the expired row drops it").

**`REQUEST`: the first requester keeps the request.** One `UPDATE` with a `CASE` over the live rows of other tabs, whose answer comes from the row the statement locked, not from a snapshot (`RETURNING … AS mine`). `:requested` means the request is now this tab's (new, or the same tab asking again, which keeps `requested_at`); `:pending` means another tab's request stands and is younger than `2 * PASS_OVER`, and nothing changed; `:none` means there is no live row of another tab (released, expired, or handed to the asker meanwhile), and the caller simply claims. A request older than `2 * PASS_OVER` that nobody passed over is replaced: its tab is gone. Without the rule two requesters could postpone each other, each one's request replacing the other's and starting its clock again. An earlier form of the statement counted from the snapshot and answered `:pending` for a row that was gone or was already the asker's own ("the first requester keeps the request: another asking while it stands is pending and changes nothing", "a request older than twice PASS_OVER that nobody passed over may be replaced, by another tab or starts afresh by the same tab", "a request that finds the row released meanwhile is none: the caller simply claims", "a request that finds the row handed over to the asking tab meanwhile is none: it is the asker's own now").

**Why they take no lock, and the one transaction that does.** None asks for a lock. What Postgres locks is the one row a statement touches (an `ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE` locks the conflicting row even when its `WHERE` refuses), until the end of the transaction the statement is in. In autocommit that is the statement itself, **which is how every caller must use these**: a claim made inside a service's transaction would keep the row locked for everyone else's claim until that transaction ends. So `Admin::EditingClaims` claims *before* the service, outside its transaction. The one exception is `Editing::TakeOver`'s short transaction, which locks this very row first (below). The tests run inside a transaction and cannot tell autocommit from not, so the rule has a static guard only: `test/lib/editing_guards_test.rb`, "a reservation's statements are called only by the three callers the reference names, in a transaction only in Editing::TakeOver". The three are `Admin::EditingClaims` (a save's claim, its give-back, a release), `Admin::EditingController` (the withdrawal) and `Editing::TakeOver`; the first two open no transaction and take no lock in their own file. It does not see a transaction opened further up the call stack, which is why its list of callers is closed: a new caller is read by a person before it is added.

**Not a service.** Claiming, renewing, releasing and withdrawing are not `ApplicationService`s and are not audited, as presence's marks are not: a reservation is not content, nobody asks for one by name, it is gone in two minutes, and audited it would flood the log. What stands in for `authorize!` is `Editing::Rights` at each door. The one audited write is `Editing::TakeOver`.

**`Editing::Holds`** is the live reservations of one page or post, read **once for a whole screen** (`Editing::Holds.on(record, viewer:, tab:)`): the rows, then their users, then their requesters, three statements at most however many blocks there are. Every card, tile and form asks it. `of(subject)` is the live row; `other(subject)` is the row that makes the subject read-only for this viewer: someone else's, **or the viewer's own in another tab**. A hold is "mine" only when it is this person's in this very tab; with no tab id every hold is other (`test/queries/editing/holds_test.rb`: "my hold in my tab is not other; in another tab of mine it is, and it is still mine as a person", "with no tab every hold is other", "it costs the same statements for 1 hold and for 20"; `test/integration/admin/editing_test.rb`: "the editor page reads the reservations in the same few statements for 3 blocks as for 30, some held").

**`Editing::Rights` and `AccessControl::OwnerRule`.** A reservation is claimed, released and taken over exactly where a save would be allowed. The rule is `AccessControl::OwnerRule.refusal(owner)`: `<owner>.edit`, and `<owner>.publish` too once the owner has a `published_at` (live or scheduled), because the write changes live content. It was `ContentBlocks::OwnerPermissions`' own; it moved to the model layer so that the services and `Editing::Rights.missing(actor, record)` ask the same rule and cannot drift, and a model may not reach up into the services (`test/models/editing/rights_test.rb`: "edit without publish lacks nothing on a draft and publish on a published page", "it agrees with what the save services refuse, for the fixtures' actors").

**The sweep.** `Editing::Sweep` deletes rows not renewed for `SWEEP_AFTER`, every 5 minutes, in every environment (`config/recurring.yml`). It signals nothing and nothing depends on it (`test/jobs/editing/sweep_test.rb`).

## A save is a claim

`Admin::EditingClaims` (`app/controllers/concerns/admin/editing_claims.rb`) is included by the three autosave controllers, `Admin::EditingController` and `Admin::TranslationConfirmationsController`.

**The order in an autosave action** (`Admin::PagesController#update`, `ContentBlocksController#update`, `PostsController#update`):

Steps 1 to 3 are one method, `claim_before_save`, which the three actions call before they read their form and which answers the claim for step 4 (an action whose refusal it rendered ends with `return if performed?`); An action reads its parameters between step 3 and step 4 and uses them after the save, which is why the save is not a block of the same method.

1. **Claim** (`claim_for`): the request's tab takes the subject, renews it, or is refused. A `:taken` signals `claimed`; a renewal signals nothing.
2. **Refusal** (`refuse_held`): another tab holds it live. A 409 like a stale save's, so the autosave script pauses the form the same way; nothing is written and nothing audited. Its streams put the status line into the state `:held` ("● NOT SAVED · DANA WEISS IS EDITING", or for the person's own other tab "● NOT SAVED · OPEN IN YOUR OTHER TAB"), put the `lost` notice into the strip's slot and remove the form's error toast.
3. **The stale check** (`refuse_stale_autosave`), as before. A stale refusal gives back a claim *this very request* just took.
4. **The service** (`saving_under`): a claim this request took is given back when the service answers denied or raises. A 422 keeps it (the person is still typing). A renewal is never given back.

The hold is checked before the stale check, so a tab that does not hold the subject gets the hold's refusal and its notice, whatever its base is (`test/integration/admin/editing_claims_test.rb`: "a save claims the block for its tab, and a save from a tab that does not hold it is refused and writes nothing", "a refused hold is checked before the stale check", "a stale save by a tab that took the block with this request lets it go again, and one by the holder keeps it", "a claim this request took is given back when the service denies the save, and kept when it answers 422", "a claim is given back when the service raises", "the status line names the holder in capitals, and the person's own other tab as such").

**Who claims nothing.** A request with no tab id (a script, a client without JavaScript, the MCP server) neither claims nor is refused. Someone who may not save the record claims nothing either: the service answers them with its 403, and a person who can only read can never hold a block against the people who can edit it ("a save with no tab id is saved and claims nothing, also while someone holds the block", "someone without the right to edit claims nothing: the save is the service's 403 and there is no row", "a write through the MCP channel holds nothing and is not refused"). The holder's own other tab is refused like anyone else, and so is a second person who sends the holder's exact tab id ("the holder's own other tab is refused like anyone else", "a second person sending the holder's exact tab id is refused like anyone else, and their release deletes nothing").

**The check and the save are not one transaction**, like the stale check beside it: two saves within one request's length can both pass, and the stale check stays underneath.

**The five POSTs** of `Admin::EditingController`, all under `editing/:kind/:id`, all with an optional `block_id` (absent: the record itself; a block that is not this record's is a 404), all 404 outside the read scope and 403 without the right:

| Request | Answers |
|---|---|
| `POST claim` | 204 when the tab holds the subject now, or sent no tab id (nothing was claimed); 409 with the refusal's streams when another tab holds it |
| `POST release` | 204 always for someone who may edit; deletes only this tab's own row and signals `released` when it deleted one |
| `POST take_over`, `POST hand_over` | 200 with `{ "state": … }` (below); 403 when the service denies; 422 without a tab id or a signed-in user |
| `POST withdraw` | 204 whether or not a request stood; 422 without a tab id. The holder is not told |

("claim: 204 and a row; 409 with the same streams when held; 403 for a reader; 404 outside the scope, for a trashed page and for another page's block", "claim answers 404 for a page outside the viewer's read scope, never 403", "release deletes the tab's own row and signals released; someone else's release deletes nothing and signals nothing", "withdraw: 403 without the right, 404 out of scope, 422 without a tab id", "each of the five POSTs is a 404, never a 403, outside the read scope, for a trashed page, for a block of one and for a trashed post".)

**Instant actions are not editing.** Publish, Unpublish, Schedule and "Still correct — mark up to date" take no hold, and are refused only while **another tab** holds the subject they change (`held_elsewhere?`): a publish would move the record's `updated_at` under the holder's open form. Publish, Unpublish and Schedule redirect back to the editor with the sentence as an alert ("Dana Weiss is editing this post. Try again when they have finished."); a translation confirmation answers a Turbo Stream request with a 409 and an error toast, and any other with a redirect back and the sentence. Nothing is written, audited or signalled. For a page only a hold on the **settings** counts, not one on a block. A request without a tab id is never refused. The hold is asked before the service's own `authorize!`, so a forged Publish by someone in the read scope without the right gets the sentence and no `access.denied` while a hold exists; accepted, since they see the strip that names the holder anyway The same holds for "Change address" (`PATCH pages/:id/slug`, `refuse_instant_redirect`: a page's slug is immediate and moves its address, so it is refused while another tab holds the settings). **The three publishing actions are not alike** (`docs/reference/publishing.md`): "Publish changes" and "Cancel scheduled change" write no row an editor holds and are not refused by a hold (the dialog names who is editing and says only what has been saved is published); "Discard changes" writes every part of the record back and is refused by a hold on ANY part of it, in the controller (`refuse_discard_under_hold`), never the service ("discard is refused while another tab holds a block of the page or its settings, says who, and writes nothing", "publish is not refused by a hold: it writes no row an editor holds"; `test/integration/admin/publishing_test.rb`). ("publish, unpublish and schedule are refused while another tab holds the post, and proceed, taking no hold, while it is free", "a page's publish while someone holds one of its blocks proceeds, and while someone holds the settings it is refused", "an instant action without a tab id is never refused", "a translation confirmation is refused while another tab holds the record, and a free record is confirmed"; `test/system/admin/editing_claims_test.rb`: "pressing Publish while a colleague is editing the post is refused, says who, and the post stays a draft").

**What ignores a hold altogether:** moving, adding, duplicating and deleting blocks, a block's move into or out of a section (the hold stays with the block), and trashing. They hold nothing and do not ask. A section's **layout** is not among them, whatever the spec's D4 says: it is saved by the section's own settings form, an autosave like any other, so it claims the section's block and is refused while another tab holds that form ("a section's settings form is its block's"). Only the section's three settings are held by that; the fold a narrower layout causes writes the other blocks with `update_columns`, so it moves no held block's base. **A move of the page in the tree** asks no hold either and needs none: it does not move the settings form's base (below, "The page settings form's own base"). ("moving, adding, duplicating and deleting ignore a hold", "a block moved into a section while held is still held, by the same tab"; `test/system/admin/editing_holds_test.rb`: "a held page's blocks can still be added, moved and deleted by the colleague, and my form keeps my text".)

**Bulk publish skips held posts.** `Admin::PostsController#bulk` reads the live reservations of the selected posts once, outside the per-post savepoints, leaves those posts out and says "1 post being edited by someone was skipped." From a list every hold is someone else's, tab id or not, and a skip is not a refusal. A bulk trash is not held back, as a single trash is not ("bulk publish skips a post someone is editing and says so; a free post is published and an expired hold does not skip", "bulk publish of only held posts changes nothing and says why").

## Read-only is rendered, not switched

A card, a section's settings form, the page settings form and a post's form are rendered for their viewer. What a reservation does to each is asked in one module, `Admin::EditorScreen::HeldUnit`, which the five components include (the block card, the section card, the tile, the page settings form, the post form): each says what it is about (`held_subject`, `held_record`) and the module answers `hold`, `held?`, `read_only_hold?`, the strip and its slot, `held_marks`, `fields` and `editing_version`. Held by someone else (`holds.other(subject)`) and otherwise editable for the viewer (`read_only_hold?`), a form has the strip, read-only fields, a rich-text field mounted read-only with no toolbar, no "Still correct" action and **no autosave controller** (a held block's card also carries the held edge, `data-card-edge="held"`: the people outline, 2px inside the card, drawn by the stylesheet from the attribute; a form `editing/freeze.js` freezes in the browser marks its nearest card the same way unless that card already shows another edge), so it registers nothing with the write queue and can send nothing. Becoming editable again is another fetch (`test/system/admin/editing_held_test.rb`: "a held block and the held page settings are read-only: the strip names the holder, nothing can be typed, no toolbar, no save", "Enter in a held form's text field and in its read-only rich text sends no request and navigates nowhere"; `test/integration/admin/editing_test.rb`: "the fetched card is editable for the tab that holds it and read-only for everyone else, the holder's other tab included").

**`Admin::EditorScreen::HeldFields`** makes the fields read-only in one pass over the rendered form (parsed as an HTML5 fragment and written out again), rather than through a read-only option on each of the eight components that draw a field: a field component added later is covered the day it is rendered in an editor, and the editable and the held form cannot drift apart.

| Control | Becomes |
|---|---|
| `textarea`; an `input` of a type in `READONLY_TYPES` (text, search, tel, url, email, password, number, date, datetime-local, month, week, time) or with no type | `readonly`: selectable, at full contrast |
| an `input` of type `hidden` | left alone, so the form carries the same names and values |
| every other `input` (checkbox, radio, file, range, color, a button, a type this list has never heard of), every `select`, every `button` | `disabled` |

**The people hue** is how a held or discussed block is told from the rest, and it is the stylesheet's, drawn from marks the server writes and no script adds. *One people outline:* `data-card-edge="held"` and `data-notes-active` (the block whose thread is open in the Notes tab) draw the same 2px outline in `--cs-people`, inside the card (`outline-offset: -2px`), so a discussed block does not read as focused and a block both held and discussed has one frame; the strip says the rest. A section has no `data-notes-active` and draws the outline outside its frame, 2px away, from its frozen settings form alone (`.admin-section-card:where(:has(> .admin-section-card__settings form[data-editing-frozen="true"]))`), so the merge, which never writes the edge, never has to. A chosen section (`[data-chosen]`) and an unavailable type win over it. *The glow:* while a card's header holds a mark (`[data-editing-mark]`, not yet `admin-editing-mark--leaving`) the card takes a 4px ring of the people hue at 20%, faded in over 200ms and out over 600ms on `--cs-ease`; a held card shows its frame alone, and under `prefers-reduced-motion` there is no glow and the mark goes at once. The transition spares a dragged card, whose own shadow is the drag's. *The marks* (`app/javascript/editing/marks.js`, which `hold_controller.js` and `live_controller.js` call so neither grows): `showMark(root, blockId, template)` clones a template of the live region into the block's own unit, found by its card id or its panel's (a section's tile has no place for one), `after` milliseconds later the mark fades over 200ms, and `clearMarks(root)` removes every mark under `root` and stops its timers before Turbo caches the page. "Updated by Dana Weiss · 2 min ago" is drawn by the server (`UpdatedMarkComponent`: the colleague's 22px avatar, without the here ring, since a save's author need not be present now, then 12px words in `meta`) and "Taken over by you · just now" is the same component drawn into a template of the live region (`UpdatedMarkComponent.new(taken_over: true)`); both stand for `Editing::NOTICE` (6 seconds) in `[data-editing-mark-place]`, where the take-over mark stays beside the ordinary status line of Take over and does not repeat it.

**Actions are an allow-list.** Every `data-action` token on every element of a held form is removed unless the controller action it names is in `HeldFields.allowed_actions`: the CMS's four that only read or only move focus (`CMS_ACTIONS`: `admin--editor--slug-preview#update`, `admin--rich-text--editor#containFocus`, `admin--ui--menu#select`, `admin--ui--menu#close`) and each block type's declared `read_only_actions` (today the Code type's `admin--editor--code-limit#update`, the counter under a code field which reads the field and writes nothing but its own line, `docs/reference/content-blocks-and-types.md`). It is an allow-list, never a deny-list of controller prefixes: a writing action added to a field later is dead in a held form from the day it is written, and the test keeps its own list of what may survive, so a new survivor fails it until someone decides. A disabled, hidden submit button is put first in the form (`guard: true`): a form whose default button is disabled does not submit when Enter is pressed in a field, and a form with one text field and no button would. The round trip through the parser loses one thing, a newline at the start of a `textarea`, `pre` or `listing`, which is put back (`test/components/admin/editor_screen/held_fields_test.rb`: "text controls are readonly, the rest is disabled, hidden fields and values are untouched", "every action not on the allow-list is removed, from any element, with any target, option or key filter", "each input type is made read-only the way it can be: readonly, disabled or left alone; an unknown type is disabled", "the guard is a disabled submit button first in the form, so Enter in a field submits nothing", "a newline that starts a textarea or a pre survives the parse, which drops the first one", and one test per field component that the rewritten markup differs only in what it should).

**The strip** (`Admin::EditorScreen::HoldStripComponent`) has three kinds. `held`: on the people tint with a 1px line under it, the holder's 26px avatar (`:other` with the solid here ring, or `:own` when it is the viewer's own other tab), "Dana Weiss is editing this block" (or "this post", or "the page settings") at 13px with the name in weight 600, and "Take over" as a small secondary (below 480px of the card it wraps under the sentence, at the start); neutral, never an alert, since nothing is wrong. `lost`: the viewer's own save or claim was refused; a warn alert, "Dana Weiss is editing this block. Your changes weren't saved." with "Copy my text" and "Discard". `deleted`: the block was deleted under unsaved text; "Dana Weiss deleted this block. Your changes weren't saved." with "Copy my text" and "Remove". Every sentence the hold script shows later is rendered here, as text or as an attribute, so the script holds no wording. A name is drawn as text, never as markup. "Take over" is also the first item of a held card's and a held section's "⋯" menu, the keyboard and touch path to it (`test/components/admin/editor_screen/hold_strip_component_test.rb`).

**Who is "other".** The viewer's own other tab is someone else: the strip shows their own avatar and "You're editing this in another tab", with "Take over"; a lost notice reads "You're editing this in another tab. Your changes weren't saved."

**A reader** (someone who may only read) sees the strip too, because it explains why the content may change while they read. For them it has no "Take over", the card has no held edge, and their form is the view-only one it always was (`test/system/admin/editing_holds_test.rb`: "a reader's editor claims nothing and shows who is editing, without Take over").

**Bulk translate leaves held units alone.** `Admin::Ui::TranslateAllComponent` does not count the empty fields of a held block or of the held page settings, and is not offered at all for a held post; the script (`admin--translation--translate`) skips a field inside `[data-editing-held]`, `[data-editing-frozen]` or `fieldset.admin-held` for the bulk run, Use all, Discard all, the per-field Translate button and Use (`test/components/admin/ui/translate_all_component_test.rb`; `test/system/admin/translations_test.rb`: "Suggest translations and Use all leave a block someone else holds alone: it is not counted, asked for or filled"; `test/system/admin/editing_holds_test.rb`: "bulk translate and per-field translate skip a frozen form").

## The hold script

`admin--editing--hold` (`app/javascript/controllers/admin/editing/hold_controller.js`) is the tab's side of a reservation: one per editor, on the region, beside the live script. It hears the forms of the screen through events and owns no form. Everything it sends is a `POST` put into the write queue (`enqueue`), with the one exception below, `pagehide`.

**A claim on the first change.** The autosave controller says a form changed (`admin--saving--autosave:dirty`, sent from `queue()` and `saveNow()` before the debounce is even started, with the form's subject), and the hold script queues `POST claim`, so the claim is answered before that form's first save is sent. Reading reserves nothing ("the claim is sent through the write queue, ahead of the block's first save, and once for three bursts of typing", which gives a write of its own the queue's turn and sees the claim wait for it; "reading reserves nothing: opening, focusing and leaving a field without a change sends no claim").

**Renewal while typing.** A save renews the hold on the server, but a person can type for longer than a hold without a pause that saves. So the autosave says `dirty` on every change, and the hold script claims again when its last claim for the subject is older than half a hold: at most one renewal per half hold, and never two on the way at once. If somebody took the subject meanwhile that claim is refused ("typing for longer than half a hold without a pause renews the hold: one renewal per half hold, and it never lapses", "a hold that this tab claimed long ago is claimed again on the first change, and refused when it was taken meanwhile"). The autosave does not say `dirty` for text that is exactly what is on the wire now: the `focusout` of a card that was just collapsed is not typing, and a claim after the release would hold the block again.

**Release, and why it waits.** A hold is released when a block's form is closed (a section's panel through `admin--editor--section:closed`; a collapsed card, and the panels of a collapsed section, through `admin--editor--blocks:toggled`) and when the editor is left. The first version queued the release at once; it then went *ahead* of the follow-up of a save still on the wire, and that follow-up claimed the block back with nothing left to release it. So a release waits, outside the queue, until none of the subject's forms has anything `outstanding()` (a save on the wire, a follow-up, a retry), and is queued only then; a new claim cancels a release that waits. While it waits the subject is in `releasing`, not in `held`. The wait is a 25 ms loop, because the autosave controller has no event for "nothing left to send" ("closing a block's form in a section releases it, after its last save, and the colleague's tile loses its avatar", "collapsing a card releases it", "collapsing a section's card releases the holds of the blocks in its panels too", "a release waits for the form's follow-up save: the order on the wire is save, save, release, and nothing holds the block afterwards", "a claim on the wire when the form is closed is answered, and the release follows it").

**Leaving.** What the tab owes the server on leaving is one function, `owed()`: a release for every subject it still holds there, which is what is in `held` **and** what is in `releasing`, each once (both are emptied, so a release that was waiting finds its token gone and is not sent a second time). The first build walked only `held`: a form closed with a save still on the wire and then left kept its hold for two minutes with nobody to release it, and colleagues who pressed "Take over" waited out the pass-over time for a tab that was gone. A Turbo visit away disconnects the controller, which queues those releases behind the saves the visit was drained for, with values read before it went (the tasks run after it is gone). At **`pagehide`** nothing can wait for a queue: the release of each owed subject is a `fetch` with `keepalive`, and so is the withdrawal of a take-over request it made (below). **These are the only writes outside the queue**; they carry the tab header and the CSRF token, which `sendBeacon` cannot ("a Turbo visit away releases what this tab held, and Back to the editor shows no strip of my own", "reloading the editor frees what this tab held, and the block is editable again without a click", "leaving the editor with a held block asks nothing and loses nothing", "a release that was waiting for its form's last save is sent when the editor is left by a Turbo visit, once, and the row is gone", "a release that was waiting for its form's last save is sent at pagehide, once, and the row is gone").

**A claim that fails is not a refusal.** A claim answered with nothing (the network, the 10-second timeout) or a 5xx leaves the subject in the script's `held` map with time 0: the save behind it claims on the server, the next change claims again, and closing or leaving releases. A `POST` has a timeout because it sits in the write queue, where a request that never answers would hold every save of the screen ("a claim that never answers gives up, and the save behind it goes: the subject stays held, to be released", "a claim answered with a 500 is not a refusal: held with time 0, claimed again on the next change, and released on close").

**Freezing in place.** The one form that turns read-only in place is one holding unsaved text when its claim or its save is refused (a tie, or a tab that woke after a take-over). On the 409 of a claim the script stops the form at once (`lose`): it calls the autosave controller's `pause()`, which ends the form's saves as a 409 does without a request (a save already queued behind the refused claim finds the form paused and sends nothing), and `freeze(form)` from `app/javascript/editing/freeze.js`, which applies `HeldFields`' rule to a form that is already on screen: the same control table, the same allow-list, the same guard button, the form's own `submit` refused, each rich-text editor locked, and `data-editing-frozen="true"`. Then the answer's streams draw the `lost` notice. The text stays where it was typed. **The rules have one source:** `HeldFields.rules` is rendered into the region as JSON and handed to `freeze`, so a type or an action allowed on the server is allowed in the browser the day it is added, and the other way round. Freezing removes `data-action` tokens and cannot be undone; only another fetch brings an editable form back ("a tie: my claim loses, my text stays in read-only fields with the notice, Copy my text copies it and Discard shows the block as it is", "a save refused because the hold lapsed and was taken keeps the text, read-only, with the notice", "Enter and typing in a frozen form send nothing, its controls are dead, and its autosave is paused with nothing queued", "a frozen form's data-action tokens are the server's allow-list, from the same list", "a form frozen in the browser is the form the server renders read-only: the same controls, the same attributes, the same actions, from one list", "pause() during a retry's backoff leaves nothing outstanding: the queue is quiet at once"; `test/components/admin/editor_screen/held_fields_test.rb`: "the rules the browser's freeze reads are this module's own lists, and nothing else").

**"Copy my text", "Discard", "Remove".** "Copy my text" copies every text control that differs from what it was rendered with, and a rich-text field whose document differs as its paragraphs in plain text; when nothing differs it copies every text field, so it never copies nothing from a form that has text. It says "Copied" for 2 seconds, in a polite status beside the button and outside the alert, and only for text that was copied; when the clipboard refuses it says so. "Discard" gives the refused text up: the live script marks the subject's units `data-editing-discard` and **clears their version**, so that no catch-up can find them "unchanged" and keep the refused text, and fetches the part; the notice is taken away for the read and comes back saying "Couldn't load the block. Try again." if the read did not draw it. "Remove" takes a deleted block's card, tile and panel away. "Discard" and "Remove" act at once, with no confirmation ("Copy my text says so when the clipboard refuses, and 'Copied' is gone after two seconds", "Copy my text does not say Copied when there is nothing to copy", "Discard, and the document hidden before the answer is drawn: the card is the server's once it is seen", "Discard keeps the notice, and says so, when the card could not be read; the next try draws it", "Discard on the page settings clears the status line that said the save was refused, and the form is editable again").

**Expiry on the others' side.** A renewal sends no signal, and neither does a lapse. So a `held` strip says in how long its hold lapses (`data-editing-expires-in`), and when that time is up (plus 250 ms) the hold script asks the live script for the part again, through its one line of reads: it comes back editable, or held again with a new time. One timer per strip; a hidden document owes the read ("an expired hold frees the colleague's card without anyone doing anything", "while the holder is still there, an expired-looking strip is drawn again with a new time", "a hidden document does not fetch when a hold runs out, and catches up when it is seen", "when a hold lapsed and the colleague changed the block, my open form shows their text, read-only, held by them").

**A `claimed` signal for a unit in which this tab has unsaved text** shows nothing at once (the merge keeps the unit as it is): the person learns at their next change or save, by the 409. With the renewal above that is the narrow case of a tab that was offline or asleep.

**A deleted block's notice.** When the list merge reports a unit gone, the hold script asks the live script for the notice (the part `notice:<id>`, the block's own address, through the same line, with the same retries, owed while the document is hidden). A 410 draws its body into the block's strip slot; a 404 draws the plain sentence "This block was deleted. Your changes weren't saved." from a template in the region ("a block deleted under my unsaved text: the notice names who deleted it, Copy my text works and Remove takes the card away", "a block deleted together with its section, under my unsaved text in its open panel, gets the same notice", "a deleted block's notice is read again when the first read fails, and is the plain sentence when no event names it"). A section's own settings form is a unit that carries no `data-block-id`: the live script names its block by its region's `data-section-id`, so a section deleted under unsaved settings gets the same notice, in its card's slot while the region stands under its head, and in a slot of the region's own while the region stands in the page editor's rail, where the card is gone (`admin--editor--section-choice#merged`). Remove takes the card, the tile, the panel and a section's settings region away, wherever each stands (`test/system/admin/section_choice_test.rb`: "a section deleted under unsaved text in its settings under its head, with nothing in the rail, shows its notice in the card", "a section deleted under unsaved text in the rail keeps its text there, with the notice and its Remove").

**A notice is always shown.** A notice nobody sees is text lost without their knowing: where a form is frozen or a notice drawn, a section's hidden panel is opened through the section controller's own `open` (so its tile says so) and a collapsed card is expanded ("a lost notice in a hidden section panel opens the panel, and in a collapsed card expands it").

**A post's notice area is shared.** A post's strip and its "Changed elsewhere" bar both stand in `#editor-notice`. While that bar shows it stays: the hold script cancels (at `turbo:before-stream-render`) a stream that would put a hold's strip over it, since the form is dead until a reload and a reload shows the true state. The other order cannot happen to one tab: a held post's form has no autosave controller, so it sends nothing that could be stale ("a post whose form is stale keeps the Changed elsewhere bar when a hold's notice arrives", "a post's lost claim shows the notice in the notice area when the form is not stale").

## Taking over

`Editing::TakeOver` (`app/services/editing/take_over.rb`) is the one audited write: an `ApplicationService` in `EXPECTED_SERVICES`, asked the rights through `AccessControl::OwnerRule` with `authorize!`, so a refusal is the service's 403 and one `access.denied`. It needs a signed-in person and a tab id. One call serves both sides.

**The requester's call** (`POST take_over`), against the row as it stands:

| The row | Outcome |
|---|---|
| none, or an expired one | `claimed` (signals `claimed`; no request, no event) |
| a live row of this tab | `claimed` (renewed, no signal) |
| a live row of another tab, no request | `requested` (written; the holder's tab is told by the signal `requested`) |
| … with this tab's request, younger than `PASS_OVER` | `requested` (its time kept, the holder told again) |
| … with this tab's request, `PASS_OVER` old or older | `taken`: the row is passed over on what was saved, audited with `answered: false`, signalled `taken` |
| … with another tab's request, younger than `2 * PASS_OVER` | `pending` (nothing changes, nothing is sent) |
| … with another tab's request, `2 * PASS_OVER` old or older | `requested` (replaces it: its tab is gone) |

**The holder's call** (`POST hand_over`):

| The row | Outcome |
|---|---|
| this tab holds it live and a request stands | `handed`: the row is the requester's, audited with `answered: true`, signalled `taken` |
| this tab holds it and nobody asked (or the requester's user was deleted) | `nothing` |
| another tab holds it, no row, an expired row, a row already passed over | `nothing` |
| the holder lacks the right now | denied; the row is untouched, and the requester's second call takes it after `PASS_OVER` |

`pending` is not in the spec: it was added when the first-requester rule was. A release with a request pending deletes the row and the request with it; the requester's next call finds no row and claims. Anyone who may save the subject may take it over, including back, and the same person's other tab taking over from themselves is audited like any other transfer. Someone with `publish` but not `edit` can neither take over nor hand over (`test/services/editing/take_over_test.rb`: "asking writes the request and tells the holder's tab; nothing is audited and nobody's row has moved", "the hand-over passes the row, audits once on the block with both ids and no text, and tells everyone", "asked again before the pass-over time it is still a request, with its first time kept; at the pass-over time the row is the requester's, not answered", "two requesters: the second is pending and changes nothing, the first's pass-over succeeds", "the holder releases while a request is pending: the requester then simply claims", "a holder who has lost the right cannot hand over, and the requester gets the block after the pass-over time", "without the right it is denied, with one access.denied, and the row is untouched", "no tab id, or nobody signed in, is a failure and changes nothing").

**The lock.** The statements run in one short transaction with the subject's reservation row locked first (`Reservation.of(subject).lock.first`), so the answer is about the row the statements then change. It locks that row and nothing else: never a content row, and no other service names `Editing::`, so there is no cycle with a content write. Nothing is sent or rendered inside it; the signal and the audit notification go out after the outermost commit. With no row there is nothing to lock and the unique index settles the race: a claim refused between the request and the claim is retried, three times at most, and the call then answers what is true of the row, never `pending` without a request. The lock is what the last of these tests holds; with `.lock` removed it fails ("the row is read under its lock: a hand-over goes to, and is audited for, the requester who replaced a stale one while it waited", "a hand-over and then a pass-over: handed, then claimed, and one event marked answered", "a pass-over and then a hand-over: taken, then nothing, and one event marked not answered"). Left as it is: `Users::Destroy` of the *requester* while a transfer of that person's request runs could deadlock (the transfer's update checks the user row the destroy holds while the destroy waits to nullify the reservation); Postgres aborts one of them. The single statements have the same cycle with or without this lock.

**The audit event** is `page.taken_over`, `post.taken_over` or `block.taken_over`, on the content record, with `snapshot: nil`, never coalesced, and metadata of two ids and a flag: `from_user_id`, `to_user_id`, `answered`. No name, no text. **It has two sentences**, because the event's actor is whoever called the service: the holder who answered ("Dana Weiss handed over editing a block on “Pricing”") or the requester who passed over ("Olga Braun took over editing a block on “Pricing”"; for a page or a post, "… took over editing “Pricing”"). The first build logged a hand-over as the holder having *taken over*. The other person is in the metadata's ids, never in the sentence (`Audit::Sentence#handed_over?`; `test/models/audit/sentence_test.rb`: "an answered take-over reads as the actor having handed editing over, an unanswered one as having taken it"; `test/services/editing/take_over_test.rb`: "the event names nobody and nothing: its metadata is two ids and a flag"). **A take-over is not a change of content:** the three actions are in `Dashboard::Feed::NOT_A_CHANGE`, so the dashboard's "Mine", "Changed by …" (`Audit::ChangeSince`) and "Updated by …" (`Editing::LastChange`) skip them, no content row is written and nothing a stale check reads moves ("a take-over is not a change: the dashboard's 'by', 'Changed by …' and 'Updated by …' skip it, and nothing a stale check reads moves"). They are `SILENT` for `Editing::Signals`: the service sends its own `holder` signal.

**In the browser, the requester.** "Take over" queues `POST take_over` and disables the button a tick later (in the same tick the card's menu, which closes on a click that reached an enabled item, would stay open and keep its unit from being drawn); a second press while this tab is asking is nothing. `claimed` and `taken` end in the unit being fetched (it comes back editable, from what the holder saved) and the focus going to its first field, unless the person has moved on to another field meanwhile. `requested` shows "Asking to take over…" beside the button, and after the pass-over time the tab asks once more, of the same holder only; the server then answers `taken`. `pending` says "Someone else is already taking this over" and leaves the button ready. A refusal, a failure or a read that did not draw the unit says "Couldn't take over. Try again." and leaves the button ready; nothing is frozen (`test/system/admin/editing_take_over_test.rb`: "from a tab that does not answer: after the pass-over time the block is mine on what was saved", "an expired hold is simply mine at the press: no waiting, no event", "someone else is already taking this over: it says so, once, and sends one request", "a double click on Take over sends one request", "a take-over that fails for the network or a timeout says it did not work, and the button is ready", "the focus goes to the first field when the person has not moved, and stays where it is when they have").

**The waiting state, and what ends it.** While waiting, a `taken` signal ends it in the fetch above. A `released` signal means the holder let go instead: the tab claims at once, without waiting out the pass-over time. A `claimed` signal, or a strip naming another person, means a third person was first: it says "Someone else is editing this now", asks nobody, and the button is ready. **A request ends with its asker:** a Turbo visit away, `pagehide` (with `keepalive`) and the unit going away each `POST withdraw`, so that the holder's tab does not hand the subject to a tab that is gone. The holder is not told of a withdrawal: its hand-over answers `nothing` and it takes its lock back ("a request that ends with its holder letting go: the asker claims at once, without waiting out the pass-over time", "a third person claiming while I wait ends the waiting, says so, and asks again for nobody", "leaving while asking withdraws the request: a visit away, and a page that is closed"; `test/integration/admin/editing_claims_test.rb`: "withdraw clears the asking tab's request, answers 204, and the holder's hand-over then finds nothing").

**In the browser, the holder: the light, reversible lock.** A `requested` signal for a subject this tab holds starts the hand-over. The first build waited for the whole write queue to drain (`drained()`), which is about every form of the screen: a person typing without a pause never gave it a quiet instant, so the hand-over was never sent, the requester passed over after five seconds and the holder's last words were frozen with the `lost` notice although they were awake and answering. Now the script **locks that subject's forms lightly** (`lockForms`: text controls `readOnly`, rich-text editors not editable) at the moment it sends their flush, so nothing more can be typed into them, and waits on *their own* state, not the screen's: none outstanding, then clean and unchanged, then `POST hand_over` through the queue. The lock is reversible, unlike `freeze` and `pause`, which are final: it removes no `data-action`, pauses no autosave and leaves disabled fields alone (a disabled control leaves the form's data, and the form would look changed). Tiptap's `setEditable` emits an update by default, which the autosave took for a change and saved; `lock()` and `unlock()` of the rich-text controller pass `false` for it. While it waits no claim renewal is sent for the subject. On `handed` the lock stays, the unit reads "Taken over by …" and is fetched read-only as the server draws it (asked again up to three times if that read fails). If the save fails or is refused, the pass-over time runs out, the requester is passed over first or the hand-over answers `nothing`, the lock is taken back exactly (what it locked, nothing else, and the focus where it was), and what follows is a take-over that was not answered: this tab hears `taken` (or is refused at its next save) and the form is frozen with the `lost` notice and its text ("taking over a block someone is typing in: their unsaved text arrives first, the block is mine with it, and theirs says who took it", "a holder who is typing hands over: the lock stops the typing, everything typed is in the block I get, and nothing says it was lost", "a holder who is typing in another block still hands this one over, and the other stays editable and saved", "a holder whose save is refused (422) gets its fields back, and is frozen with the notice once it is passed over", "the holder cannot hand over: nothing is handed over while text cannot be saved, the pass-over happens, and the holder's text is kept, frozen with the notice", "a tab that does not hear it was passed over, and whose hand-over came too late, keeps what it types after, read-only, with the notice").

**"Taken over by …"** shows for `NOTICE` in the tab the subject was taken from, whether it handed over or was passed over while awake with nothing unsaved, then becomes the ordinary strip ("the notice becomes the ordinary strip after its time"). **The time is the subject's, not the strip's** (`taken`: the holder and until when). A strip is part of a card that is drawn again whenever anything about the block is heard, and the first build kept the sentence with the first strip only: it was gone with that strip, a second after the take-over, when the person who took over saved their first words. Every strip of the same holder that connects before the time is up reads the sentence for what is left of it, and nothing is announced again ("the notice stays for its time when the card is drawn again meanwhile, and is not announced again"). The holder's tab is not told of its own hand-over by a signal (it is the signal's `origin`): its own answer tells it.

**Announcements.** A change of holder is announced politely, once, in the unit's `[data-editing-announcer]` ("Dana Weiss took over this block"): when a `taken` signal was heard, and in the tab that handed over or was passed over. The requester hears nothing: it did it. A release followed by a claim is no take-over and is not said. It is said 150 ms after the strip appears, because a live region that arrives with its text is not always read ("a change of holder is announced once, to a third person, even when the card is fetched twice"). **The strip that says it is the new holder's.** A third person's tab reads the card when it hears `requested` and again when it hears `taken`, a moment apart, and the first answer can have been drawn by the server while the old holder still held the block and reach the page after the second signal: its strip names the person the block was taken *from*. The first build said the sentence of the first strip to connect after the signal, so on a slow machine a third person heard "Dana Weiss took over this block" of a block Dana had just lost, or, when that strip was replaced before its 150 ms were up, nothing (CI, 2026-10-11). Now the tab remembers whose strip it showed when it heard `taken` (`announcing`, subject to user id) and says the sentence of the first strip that names someone else; a strip that still names the old holder says nothing and uses nothing up ("a read that was on its way when the block was taken, and still names the one it was taken from, does not use the announcement up"). Two things follow. A third person hears nothing when someone takes a block over from their own other tab: the strip names the same person before and after. And the region is part of the card, so a card drawn again arrives with an empty one: the sentence was said, but it is not *in* the region afterwards, and a test asks what was said (`watch_announcements`, `said`), not what the region holds. The `lost` and `deleted` notices are `role="alert"`; the "Updated" mark is not announced.

**A take-over raises no leave prompt and moves no base** ("a take-over raises no leave prompt and moves no base").

## The slot on a block

`editor.block.markers` is drawn by a block's card (in the list and in a translation tab's group; not on a panel, whose tile carries it) and by a section's tile. Two components are registered, in this order: `Admin::EditorScreen::HolderAvatarComponent`, then notes' `Admin::NotesScreen::MarkerComponent`. The avatar draws **on a tile only** (`placement: :tile`): a 22px avatar (`:sm`, with the solid here ring) of whoever holds the block, with the shared tooltip "Dana Weiss is editing this block" and the same words for a screen reader; on a card the strip says it. There is no "Take over" on a tile: opening the tile shows the strip. The viewer's own hold in this tab draws nothing; their hold in another tab draws their own avatar; a reader sees it too.

The page's holds are read once for the screen and handed to the slot as `holds:`, like any other context; the avatar asks them and nothing else, so the slot costs no statement. It has no loader and reads no `Current`: whose tab a hold is "mine" in is the holds' own knowledge (`Editing::Holds#other`), and a host that hands no holds draws no avatar (`docs/reference/slots.md`, `docs/reference/presence.md`). The card and the tile do not draw the notes marker themselves, but both still name notes for the "Add note" item of their menus, a named exception (`test/lib/notes_boundary_test.rb`: "the block card and the tile draw no marker themselves: they draw the slot"; `test/components/admin/editor_screen/holder_avatar_component_test.rb`: "a host that hands no holds draws no avatar, reads nothing and asks no Current"; `test/integration/admin/editing_test.rb`: "the page editor costs the same statements for 3 blocks as for 30 with both features on every block, and the avatar's slot costs none"; `test/system/admin/editing_holds_test.rb`: "a held block in a section shows its holder's avatar on the tile, and opening the tile shows the strip").

## The page settings form's own base

Every block write touches its page, so the page settings form's stale check compares against the page's `settings_changed_at` and not its `updated_at`: otherwise someone in the settings would be refused after a colleague saved a block, although nobody else had changed the settings, which would break the promise that a held form is yours.

`pages.settings_changed_at` is the settings form's base (`Admin::AutosaveResponses#autosave_base`; every other form holds `updated_at`). **What moves it:** a `before_save` of `Page`, whenever the page is new or a column outside `Page::SETTINGS_BASE_IGNORES` is about to change. **What does not:** a `touch` (it runs no save callbacks), `update_columns` and `update_all` (they go around them on purpose: `Translations::Confirm`'s bookkeeping must not make an open form stale), and **a move in the tree** (`parent_id` and `position` are on that list beside `updated_at` and the column itself). Descendants that `Trash::Move` and `Trash::Restore` change with `update_all` keep theirs while the branch root's moves; harmless, since no settings value changes (`test/models/page_test.rb`: "settings_changed_at moves when a column of the page changes, and not when the page is only touched", "a block's save leaves its page's settings base where it was", "a move in the tree leaves the settings base of the moved page and of its new siblings where it was"; `test/integration/admin/page_editor_test.rb`: "the settings form is saved on its base although a block of the page was saved since", "the settings form is refused when the settings themselves were saved since"; `test/system/admin/editing_live_test.rb`: "the settings form takes a colleague's saved subtitle, and mine is saved afterwards although they saved blocks in between").

**What a block's answer does not carry.** A block write does not move the page form's base, so a block's answer carries no new base and a block request sends none: there is no `Admin::ContentBlocksController#page_base_streams`, no `pageBaseId` value in the autosave script and no base header on `admin--editor--blocks`' requests (`test/integration/admin/content_blocks_test.rb`: "a block's answer carries no base of the page settings form").

**A move in the tree is not a change of the page's settings.** `Pages::Move` saves the moved page's `parent_id` and `position`, and does not move `settings_changed_at`. The tree stands beside every page editor, so a move that did would give whoever holds the page's settings form a final 409 on their next save, "Changed elsewhere", though nothing they could edit in that form had changed: the one place where a person holding a subject could still have their typing refused by a colleague's ordinary action, which is exactly what Publish is refused for. Both columns are ignored by the base. What else a move writes does not reach it either: the siblings it renumbers are written with `update_columns` (`Page::TreeRanking#place_among_siblings`), the hierarchy rows are closure_tree's own table, and a page's path is `public_paths` rows, not a column of the page. A real change of the settings by a colleague still refuses (`test/integration/admin/tree_test.rb`: "a settings form held while a colleague moves that page in the tree saves afterwards", "a settings form is still refused when a colleague changed the settings, moved in the tree since or not").

**What the tree does not forward.** A move moves no base, so `admin--pages--tree` sends no `X-Page-Base-Updated-At`, `Admin::TreeController` answers no stream of the hidden field and the script has no `pageBaseFor`; the hidden field `page-base-updated-at` is written by the settings form's own answers alone. The move and its Undo toast are still sent through the write queue, as writes of the page ("a move answers no base of the page settings form, whatever a client sends", "moving the open page leaves its settings form's base where it was, so its next save isn't stale"; `test/system/admin/tree_test.rb`: "moving the page that is open, and undoing it, leaves its editor able to save").

**`Audit::ChangeSince` for a page** counts only the page's own events, because only those can have made the settings base stale. Counting its blocks' events named a block editor ("Changed by C") for a settings change B had made. For a post it still counts the post's and its blocks' (`test/queries/audit/change_since_test.rb`: "a block saved after a page's settings change is not named as the cause of a refused settings save"). A tag merge touches a page with `touch_all` only, so it does not make an open settings form stale (`test/integration/admin/tags_test.rb`: "after a merge an open block card is refused as stale, and the page settings form, which the merge did not change, still saves").

## Where Stimulus strained

The scripts are Stimulus controllers on server-rendered HTML, like the rest of CS Studio, and this is the screen where that model is at its limit: several stateful forms, rich text, and a server that redraws the page under them. The limits are written in the header of `hold_controller.js` and on the roadmap ("The browser scripts: keep Stimulus clean, and say where it ends"). In short: the hold script keeps per-subject state in plain collections (the Maps `held`, `releasing`, `asking`, `handing`, `lost`, `taken`, `announcing` and `timers`, the Set `claiming`) that must outlive the elements the server replaces; four controllers cooperate by custom events with no owner of the picture, and the hold script reaches into the autosave, section and rich-text controllers through the application; a strip is a target of a controller far above it, used as the hook for "a server answer put a notice here"; and two waits are polling loops (25 ms), because the autosave controller has no event for "nothing left to send". `editing/merge.js` is the price of the server redrawing an editor, paid once.

## What is not built

- **Library images** in the media panel: not held, not refreshed; they keep "saved, also changed elsewhere". Their audit actions are `SILENT`.
- **Reservations for writes through the MCP server.** They hold nothing and are not refused by a hold; they appear through live refresh, and one that lands on a block someone is changing makes that person's next save stale, as before.
- **Co-editing** of one rich-text field: step 4 of the roadmap's collaboration item, and only if this proves insufficient.
- **Anything on the dashboard, in the lists or in the mind graph**: no count of held blocks, no marker in the page tree or the posts list (presence covers who is on a record).
- **A rate limit on claims.**
- **A colleague's publish or unpublish refreshes the form, not the header**: nothing fetches the header, so its status chip and its primary action stay as they were until a reload.
- **Tags.** Renaming, deleting or merging a tag touches the posts that carry it and sends no signal (the event names the tag, not the posts): an open post form is refused as stale, as before.

The gaps the build left are on the roadmap ("Live editing"), with their reasons. The ones a change here is most likely to meet: a top-level card that stays expanded has no "close", so its hold is freed by leaving the editor, by collapsing the card or by the two-minute lapse, and by nothing sooner; a read caused by a `requested` signal can, for a moment, replace the holder's lightly locked clean card with an unlocked one before `handed` arrives (what is typed in that moment is refused by the server with the `lost` notice, so nothing is lost or saved twice); a save still on the wire at `pagehide` can reach the server after the `keepalive` release and claim the block for the old tab id, so the reloaded page reads "You're editing this in another tab" until that hold lapses or the person takes it over; a holder typing without a pause in *another* block sees "Taken over by …" on the handed block only when it pauses, since the live script's reads wait for the tab's writes to be quiet (its fields are locked at once); after `handed`, if every re-read fails the lock stays and no notice shows; a drop made while a colleague changed the list is refused as stale and the list redrawn (`test/system/admin/editing_live_test.rb`: "a drop made while a colleague changed the list is refused as stale and the list is redrawn, and nothing was drawn during the drag"); a clean focused rich-text field loses its caret when it is replaced; a subscription is not re-judged when a right is taken away; a person whose right to edit is removed keeps a hold until it lapses or is taken over.

## Rules a change can break silently

Moved word for word from `CLAUDE.md`, where "above" and "below" meant the list these rules stood in: a rule named that way may now be in another reference file. Each names the test that guards it; a rule or a part of one with no guard is also said in `CLAUDE.md`.

- An editing signal is sent only by `Editing::Signals`, through `Live::Signals`, and carries a kind (`content` or `holder`), a block's id or none, a reason and, when the request named its tab, the `origin`, never text. Its source is the audit log's after-commit notification, so no content service names the feature: every action in `Audit::Sentence::ACTIONS` is in `Editing::Signals::MAP` or in `SILENT` with its reason, and a new action must be placed in one of them. `origin` is `Current.editing_tab`, which `Admin::BaseController` reads from `X-Notes-Tab` for every CS Studio request, never `Current.notes_tab` (a content request that set that one would make the tab that deleted a block skip the refresh of its own notes), and `heard` never raises: it runs in the request that made the write (`test/models/editing/signals_test.rb`: "every action the app records is mapped or silent, with a reason, and none is both", "a block's save signals its page: content, saved, the block's id and nothing else", "the tab that made the change is named, and a junk id is left out", "nothing is sent for a write that is rolled back, and a signal that fails does not fail the write"; `test/models/notes/signals_test.rb`: "nothing in the app broadcasts but Live::Signals, and only a ...::Signals module calls it").
- Every write an editor sends carries the tab's id in `X-Notes-Tab`: Turbo's requests get it from the one listener in `app/javascript/editing/requests.js`, and the three hand-built writes (the autosave, the block reorder, the page tree's move) add `[TAB_HEADER]: TAB` themselves. A write without it claims nothing, and its own tab fetches what it has just drawn (`test/lib/editing_tab_header_test.rb`: "each hand-built write of an editor sends the tab header", "Turbo's own requests get the header from one listener").
- A form that holds unsaved changes is never replaced, removed or moved by a live refresh, and neither is anything else of its unit; everything else is replaced by what was fetched. `app/javascript/editing/merge.js` is the one place that says so, and it decides by ids, `data-editing-subject`, `data-editing-version` and the caller's `unsaved(form)`, never by comparing HTML (a fetched form carries a fresh CSRF token, a mounted editor has added elements). A kept unit is the same element afterwards, so the list is reconciled around it, which needs an id on every ancestor of a unit up to the block list (an ancestor without one is "not in the answer": it is kept and the unit inside is marked deleted, and nothing raises); a block is one subject with one form on screen, so a kept unit whose block the answer has elsewhere stays and nothing of that block is drawn in its new place; and every version is written through `Editing.version`, which `Admin::EditorScreen::HeldUnit#editing_version` alone calls (`test/integration/admin/editing_test.rb`: "every ancestor of every unit and part up to the block list, and of the record's form up to the region, has an id", which holds the markup the server draws today and not an id a script takes away; `test/lib/editing_guards_test.rb`: "every data-editing-version in the app is written from a method that calls Editing.version", a source grep that does not see a version handed in through a variable; `test/system/admin/editing_merge_test.rb`: "a card that holds unsaved text is the same element with its text after a list merge, and a clean one is replaced", "one kept card while the others are reordered around it is never taken out of the document", "a kept card whose block was moved into a section stays where it is, is not gone, and the block has one form", "a catch-up of a page with a section leaves no id on the page twice, whatever it kept", "a catch-up leaves a card of the same version as it is, with its menu open, and replaces one that changed").
- A live fetch of an editor is a plain GET through the read scope (`Admin::EditingController`: 404 outside it and for a trashed record, 204 for a section, 410 for a deleted block, `Cache-Control: no-store`), never a Turbo request. `admin--editing--live` has one GET on its way at a time, waits for this tab's write queue to be quiet (`quiet()`), drops an answer during whose flight this tab queued a write (`writeCount()`), fetches nothing while the document is hidden, and neither the list nor a block while a block is in someone's hand (`test/integration/admin/editing_test.rb`: "outside the read scope, in the trash, of another page and of an unknown kind every part is not found", "every answer, a 404 included, is kept by no cache"; `test/system/admin/editing_live_test.rb`: "live refresh uses no Turbo request: no visit, no frame load, no stream", "a slow list answer and a newer block answer end with the newest content and the newest base on screen", "an answer fetched before this tab's own write was queued is not drawn", "a hidden tab fetches nothing, and catches up once when it is seen"; `test/integration/trash_visibility_test.rb`: "a trashed page's and a trashed post's editing parts are not served").
- An edit reservation is the other write that is not an `ApplicationService`: claiming, renewing, releasing and withdrawing are statements of `Editing::Reservation`, each ONE statement on the unique index of the subject, with no callback, writing nothing but its own row (no `touch`, no audit event, no content version: each says `# version: none (a reservation is not content)`). None asks for a lock, but Postgres locks the row a statement touches until the end of the transaction it is in, so every one is called in autocommit, outside a service's transaction: `Admin::EditingClaims` claims before the service runs (`claim_before_save`, then `saving_under` around the service). The one audited write, and the one transaction, is `Editing::TakeOver`, which locks the subject's reservation row first and no content row (`test/models/editing/reservation_test.rb`: "every statement is one statement, takes no lock and writes nothing but its own row", "two tabs claiming a free subject at once: one takes it, the other is refused, and there is one row", "an expired row claimed by its holder and by a stranger at once: exactly one holds afterwards"; `test/services/editing/take_over_test.rb`: "the row is read under its lock: a hand-over goes to, and is audited for, the requester who replaced a stale one while it waited"; `test/lib/version_bump_test.rb`; `test/lib/editing_guards_test.rb`: "a reservation's statements are called only by the three callers the reference names, in a transaction only in Editing::TakeOver", which is the only guard of "in autocommit" and a static one: the tests run inside a transaction, and a transaction opened further up the call stack than the caller's own file is not seen, so a new caller is read by a person before it joins that test's list).
- A save is a claim, in this order: the three autosave actions claim for the request's tab, refuse a tab that does not hold the subject (a 409, nothing written, nothing audited), then run the stale check, then the service; a claim that very request took is given back when the save is refused as stale, denied or raises, and kept on a 422. A request with no tab id (a script, the MCP server) neither claims nor is refused, and someone who may not save the record (`Editing::Rights`, the services' own rule in `AccessControl::OwnerRule`: `edit`, and `publish` once the record has a `published_at`) claims nothing. Publish, Unpublish, Schedule and "Still correct" take no hold and are refused while another tab holds what they change; moving, adding, duplicating and deleting blocks ignore a hold; bulk publish skips a held post and says so (`test/integration/admin/editing_claims_test.rb`: "a save claims the block for its tab, and a save from a tab that does not hold it is refused and writes nothing", "a refused hold is checked before the stale check", "a stale save by a tab that took the block with this request lets it go again, and one by the holder keeps it", "a claim this request took is given back when the service denies the save, and kept when it answers 422", "a save with no tab id is saved and claims nothing, also while someone holds the block", "someone without the right to edit claims nothing: the save is the service's 403 and there is no row", "publish, unpublish and schedule are refused while another tab holds the post, and proceed, taking no hold, while it is free", "moving, adding, duplicating and deleting ignore a hold", "bulk publish skips a post someone is editing and says so; a free post is published and an expired hold does not skip"; `test/models/editing/rights_test.rb`: "it agrees with what the save services refuse, for the fixtures' actors").
- Every claim, release, withdrawal, take-over, hand-over and save of an editor goes through `app/javascript/saving/queue.js`: `admin--editing--hold` queues its POSTs (`enqueue`), the claim ahead of the form's first save and the release only once none of the subject's forms has anything left to send. The one exception is `pagehide`, where nothing can wait for a queue: the release of what the tab holds, and the withdrawal of a request it made, are each a `fetch` with `keepalive`. Leaving releases everything the tab still holds on the server, once each: what is in `held` and what `release()` had parked while a form's last save was outstanding (`owed()`) (`test/system/admin/editing_holds_test.rb`: "the claim is sent through the write queue, ahead of the block's first save, and once for three bursts of typing", which holds the queue with a write of its own and sees the claim wait for it, "a claim on the wire when the form is closed is answered, and the release follows it", "a release waits for the form's follow-up save: the order on the wire is save, save, release, and nothing holds the block afterwards", "a release that was waiting for its form's last save is sent when the editor is left by a Turbo visit, once, and the row is gone", "a release that was waiting for its form's last save is sent at pagehide, once, and the row is gone", "reloading the editor frees what this tab held, and the block is editable again without a click"; `test/system/admin/editing_take_over_test.rb`: "leaving while asking withdraws the request: a visit away, and a page that is closed"; no static guard).
- A form someone else holds is rendered read-only, not switched: it has no `admin--saving--autosave` controller, so it registers nothing with the write queue, and its fields are rewritten once by `Admin::EditorScreen::HeldFields` (text controls `readonly`, everything else but a hidden input `disabled`, a disabled submit button first). Its `data-action` tokens are an allow-list (`HeldFields.allowed_actions`: the CMS's four, `HeldFields::CMS_ACTIONS`, and each block type's `read_only_actions`): a writing action added to a field is dead in a held form unless someone adds it there, and the test keeps its own list of what may survive. A built-in type's `read_only_actions` is a promise read by review (an outside type may declare none in step 1, refused at registration): the test checks that each names a method of a controller file, and only a reviewer can see that it only reads (`test/components/admin/editor_screen/held_fields_test.rb`: "the allow-list is the CMS's four and every type's declared read-only actions, each a method of a controller file", "an action a type declares survives in a held form, and only while the type declares it"). The one form that turns read-only in place, one holding unsaved text when its claim or save is refused, is frozen by `editing/freeze.js` from the same lists, which the region renders (`HeldFields.rules`) (`test/components/admin/editor_screen/held_fields_test.rb`: "every action not on the allow-list is removed, from any element, with any target, option or key filter", "text controls are readonly, the rest is disabled, hidden fields and values are untouched", "the rules the browser's freeze reads are this module's own lists, and nothing else"; `test/system/admin/editing_held_test.rb`: "a held block and the held page settings are read-only: the strip names the holder, nothing can be typed, no toolbar, no save"; `test/system/admin/editing_holds_test.rb`: "a form frozen in the browser is the form the server renders read-only: the same controls, the same attributes, the same actions, from one list").
- A take-over is not a change of content: `page.taken_over`, `post.taken_over` and `block.taken_over` are in `Dashboard::Feed::NOT_A_CHANGE` (so "Mine", "Changed by …" and "Updated by …" skip them) and in `Editing::Signals::SILENT` (the service sends its own `holder` signal), the event is on the content record with `snapshot: nil` and metadata of two ids and a flag, never a name or text, and its sentence has two verbs, "handed over editing" when the holder answered and "took over editing" when the requester passed over (`test/services/editing/take_over_test.rb`: "a take-over is not a change: the dashboard's 'by', 'Changed by …' and 'Updated by …' skip it, and nothing a stale check reads moves", "the event names nobody and nothing: its metadata is two ids and a flag"; `test/models/audit/sentence_test.rb`: "an answered take-over reads as the actor having handed editing over, an unanswered one as having taken it").
