# CS Studio: shell, dashboard, audit log and small screens

> Describes: `app/controllers/admin/` (base, dashboard, audit events, system status, jump, mind graph), `app/components/admin/shell/`, `app/components/admin/dashboard_screen/`, `app/components/admin/audit_screen/`, `app/queries/dashboard/`, `app/queries/jump/`, `app/queries/post_graph.rb` and `app/queries/post_graph/`, `app/javascript/mind_graph/`, `app/javascript/controllers/admin/mind_graph/` (`graph_controller.js`, `swatches_controller.js`), `app/components/admin/mind_graph_screen/`, `app/components/admin/shell/tree_subjects.rb` and `app/helpers/admin/shell_helper.rb` (the tree column's subject), `app/views/admin/mind_graph/`, `app/models/audit/sentence.rb`, `app/components/public/preview_bar_component.rb`.

## Admin (CS Studio)

Our own admin, mounted at `ContentSpiel::ADMIN_PATH` (`config/initializers/admin_path.rb`, default `cs-studio`, configurable via `ADMIN_PATH`, one URL segment, can't be a reserved path), behind the same session-based auth as the rest of the app (no separate admin auth) plus the permission checks described in `docs/reference/access-control-and-audit.md`. Controllers live under `app/controllers/admin/` (`Admin::BaseController` and per-module controllers: `DashboardController`, `PagesController`, `PostsController`, `ContentBlocksController`, `TagsController`, `TagSuggestionsController`, `SettingsController`, `LanguagesController` (the Languages card's instant actions only; there is no Languages screen), `AiKeysController`, `UsersController`, `UserSuggestionsController`, `GroupsController`, `ApiTokensController`, `AuditEventsController`, `AccountsController`, `PasswordsController`, `SessionsController`, `SystemStatusController`, `TranslationsController`, `TranslationConfirmationsController`, `MindGraphController`, `LinkTargetsController`, `JumpController`, `MediaController`, `MediaAssetsController`, `StarterContentsController`, `TrashController`, `TreeController`, and the seven notes controllers, `NotesController`, `NoteThreadsController`, `NoteMessagesController`, `NoteMentionsController`, `NoteReactionsController`, `NoteFilesController` and `NoteInboxController`, described in `docs/reference/notes.md`). Every write goes through a service object (`app/services/`, e.g. `Pages::Save`, `ApiTokens::Issue`), never straight from a controller — see individual services' own comments for authorization and audit-event conventions. `Pages::Save` and `Posts::Save` take only the keys in their `ATTRIBUTES` lists in `attributes:`; any other (`parent_id`, `position`, the trash columns, a translated attribute) raises `ArgumentError`, because moving and trashing belong to `Pages::Move` and `Trash::Move`. `Admin::BaseController#require_permission(key)` gates whole admin areas (403 on refusal, rendered inside the CS Studio layout, never a bare error page); record-scoped controllers load through `AccessControl.scope` instead, so an out-of-scope id 404s; views use the `can?(key, record = nil)` helper (`app/helpers/access_control_helper.rb`) to hide buttons a signed-in user couldn't act on anyway. Content views use autosave (`admin--saving--autosave` Stimulus controller, `Admin::AutosaveResponses#autosave_stream`) rather than a submit button, configuration screens an explicit Save; `docs/reference/save-rule-and-write-queue.md` says which is which and how a stale save is refused.

CS Studio's start page is the **dashboard** (`Admin::DashboardController#show`, dashboard spec `2026-09-30-dashboard-design.md` and the E4 spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-06-studio-e4-design.md`), not the pages list: `root to: "dashboard#show"` inside the admin scope, so `admin_root_path` means the dashboard. The Pages module is at `/<admin>/pages`, which is not a list but a redirect: `Admin::PagesController#index` sends a viewer who can open Home to Home's editor (303, the editing language kept, the flash kept) and otherwise shows the empty state "Choose a page from the tree" beside the tree; `Admin::BaseController#pages_landing_path` is the same decision for every redirect that means "the pages landing" (the trash's post-move redirect, starter content, a page's own delete). Sign-in and setup completion deliberately land on `admin_root_*`, the dashboard. It's open to every signed-in user (`StudioModules`' `permission: nil` entry, registered first), including one whose groups hold no grants at all: every list it shows is already read-scoped through `AccessControl.scope`/`AccessControl.holds?` the way every other admin list is, so a user who can read nothing simply sees empty panels rather than a 403.

**The page, in the document's order**, which is the focus's order at every width: a heading row (`.admin-dashboard__head`: "Dashboard", then in `.admin-dashboard__buttons` "New page…", a secondary, and "New post…", the screen's one primary, last; each a link to its standalone New page that opens the layout's `new-page-dialog` or `new-post-dialog`, drawn only for `pages.create` or `posts.create`), the mail-failed notice for `settings.manage` (`.admin-dashboard__notice`), then `.admin-dashboard__grid` with the **main column** (`.admin-dashboard__main`: Waiting for you and Notes for you in `.admin-dashboard__actions`, then Pick up where you left off) and the **rail** (`aside.admin-dashboard__rail`, named "At a glance": Publishing on its own, Languages behind …, What changed recently with "Full audit log →" for `audit.view`). What needs the person comes first and the rail, information, is read second. There is no quick-create row: a draft is begun from "New post…" or "Start a draft", in the New post dialog.

**The rail and its threshold.** `.admin-dashboard` is the container `admin-dashboard` (its own width, never the window's: the sidebar and the tree take room the window does not show), and the rail docks under `@container admin-dashboard (min-width: 1200px)`, the editors' rail's threshold (`@container admin-editor`, `docs/reference/design-system.md`, "E4: the right rail"). A container query cannot read a custom property, so 1200px is written in both blocks and a test holds them to one. No `@media` rule names the dashboard and no column count is `auto-fit`: a wider dashboard widens the columns and adds none. Docked, the grid is `minmax(0, 1fr) var(--cs-rail-width)` with `column-gap: var(--cs-rail-gap)` and `max-width: var(--cs-content-max)`, and `margin-inline: auto`, the main column taking what the rail leaves, the whole stopping at 1680px: at the content's left edge below that, and centred past it, the spare width split evenly either side (`test/system/admin/dashboard_test.rb`: "at 2400px the dashboard stops at the content max, centred, with four draft tiles a row"); the rail is one column on `--cs-rail` with `--cs-shadow-2` and `--cs-radius-rail`, and on that ground its panels have no card, no shadow and no tinted band (their headings keep their warm ink, and their rows, empty line and footer lose their inset with the head's); the heading row and the notice stop with the composition and are centred as it is (`max-width: var(--cs-content-max)`, `margin-inline: auto`), so "New post…" stands over the rail's right edge. Below the threshold the rail's panels follow the main column as cards (elevation 1, the warm head), one column, two from 40rem of the dashboard's width. The main column is a container of its own (`admin-dashboard-main`): Waiting for you and Notes for you stand side by side from 44rem of it, and the draft tiles take two columns from 32rem, three from 48rem and four from 64rem. **The docking band:** because the threshold is the dashboard's width and the sidebar appears at the window's 1280px breakpoint, a widening window docks the rail at 1263–1279px (the dashboard is the window less 63px), un-docks it at 1280–1502px while the sidebar takes its room (the window less 303px), and docks it again from 1503px. That is the rule applied honestly, the rail docks whenever the content has 1200px; the editors' rail flips the same way at its own widths, and a window query, which would hide it, is refused.

**The panels.** Each is an `Admin::DashboardScreen::PanelComponent`, a `<section>` labelled by its heading: a head with the heading (`h2.text-h3`) and one line under it that says the panel's job, tinted by the panel's **kind** (`KINDS`: `:action`, something waits on the viewer, `--cs-head-action`; `:shortcuts`, a way back into work, `--cs-head-cool`; `:information`, what is happening, `--cs-head-warm`; stylesheet rules on `.admin-dashboard-panel--<kind>`, not utilities), then at most `LIMIT` (5) rows. Waiting for you and Notes for you are action panels, Pick up where you left off is a shortcuts panel, and the rail's three are information panels.

- **Waiting for you** (`Admin::DashboardScreen::WaitingPanelComponent` in `turbo-frame#dashboard-waiting.contents`, `Dashboard::WaitingForYou`): the readable records that wait on someone, for an open thread (`Dashboard::OpenThreads`) or for changes not on the site yet (`Dashboard::Feed#unpublished_changes`, the live or scheduled records whose mark is set, `Publishable.with_unpublished_changes`), the newest first. **A record both discussed and unpublished is ONE row carrying both reasons**, its open-thread count and the "Unpublished changes" chip, so a page is never listed twice. The filter is three links in the head (`Admin::Ui::TabLinksComponent`, labelled "Show": Everything, Open threads, Unpublished changes; the current tab is drawn as the filter bar's inverted segment, ink fill and paper words and count in weight 600 with no inset ring, as it is on Notes for you's scopes, the Trash's tabs and the form builder's), each navigating the frame to `GET <admin>/dashboard/waiting?filter=…` (`Admin::DashboardController#waiting`, the partial alone for that frame and a 303 to the dashboard for any other request); the filter shown as current is the query's own, so an unknown one reads as Everything, and the filter is the frame's `src`, never the page's address. The frame's answer replaces the link that was pressed, so after a load a filter's press started (its `turbo:click`, taken with the request it starts), and only then, `admin--dashboard--waiting` puts the focus on the filter now current; a live reload never moves the focus (`test/system/admin/dashboard_test.rb`: "a filter pressed from the keyboard leaves the focus on the filter it chose"; `test/system/admin/note_inbox_test.rb`: "a live reload of Waiting for you leaves the focus where it is"). The dashboard is never restored from Turbo's page cache (`turbo-cache-control: no-cache`, as the editors): a snapshot would list a page just published still offered for publishing, so Back fetches the dashboard afresh, its filter back on Everything (`test/system/admin/dashboard_test.rb`: "Back to the dashboard shows what waits now, not what waited when it was left"; `test/integration/admin/dashboard_test.rb`: "the dashboard is never restored from Turbo's page cache"). Under a filter a record appears when it has that filter's reason, with that reason alone. Each filter has its own empty line ("Nothing is waiting for you.", "No open threads.", "No unpublished changes."). **Beside the heading, a neutral count** (`text-count`, never accent: it is everybody's work, not what is unread for the viewer) of every record waiting under the current filter (`WaitingForYou#total`, a record with both reasons counted once), so a record beyond the five rows is not invisible; it **follows the filter and is hidden at zero**, where the empty line speaks. A row (`WaitingRowComponent`) is the title, a meta line (the kind's marker, round in the content type's colour for a post and square for a page or an image, the kind's name, who wrote last among its open threads, when it last changed), then the open-thread bubble (`Admin::Ui::NoteMarkerComponent`, always the read look: Waiting for you lists open threads, not what the viewer was sent, so it never shows the accent's unread bubble), the chip and the verb. With publish rights on the record the verb is "Review and publish…", a second link (a small secondary raised over the row's stretched link) to the editor with `?review=publish`; otherwise it is the words "Open thread" (a row with a thread) or "Open".
- **Notes for you**: `docs/reference/notes.md`, "The inbox", and below. Each of its rows ends in "Open thread" inside its one link, under the time in the row's last column, so the row's three lines keep the room of one column in a narrow panel.
- **Pick up where you left off** (`Dashboard::Feed#drafts`, `layout: :tiles`): the viewer's drafts as tiles (`Admin::DashboardScreen::DraftTileComponent`, an `.admin-tile`, CS Studio's one tile look: the kind, the title as a link stretched over the tile, a post's excerpt in two lines, "edited …" and "Continue"), "→ All drafts" to the posts list's `scope=drafts`.
- **Publishing on its own** (`Dashboard::Feed#going_live_next`): the scheduled pages and posts, the soonest first, each row led by the scheduled date (`Admin::Ui::ScheduledDateComponent`, the month and the day on the scheduled fill, hidden from a screen reader since the row's `<time>` says it in words), "→" to `scope=scheduled`.
- **Languages behind …** (`Admin::DashboardScreen::TranslationPanelComponent`, from `Dashboard::TranslationNeeds`): headed by the site's primary language named in its own language ("Languages behind Deutsch"), per target language the viewer's readable pages, posts and — for a `media.upload` holder — images from `Translations::StateIndex.summary`, each language's progress (`Admin::Ui::LanguageProgressComponent`, the one the Translations tiles draw: the bar `ok` on the `line` track, then "↻ 1 outdated" in warn ink before "12 missing" in meta, or "All translated"), plus the most recently changed outdated item; hidden with one language.
- **What changed recently** (`Dashboard::Activity`, below).

**One link per row or tile, and each said once.** A Waiting for you row and a draft tile press as one: the title is a link whose `::after` is stretched over it, with the focus ring drawn on that `::after`. The verb at the end ("Open thread", "Open", "Continue") is words, not a link, because a link cannot hold a second one; it is **dim at rest and accent while the row or tile is pointed at or focused** (`.admin-row-verb`), since accent on a row at rest would say "unread and addressed to you". Notes for you's verb follows the same rule. `Admin::DashboardScreen::OneLink` writes the link's ARIA: the link is **named by its title and its reasons** (`aria-labelledby` its own id, then the count's and the chip's) and **described by its verb** (`aria-describedby`), and the marks and the verb beside it are `aria-hidden`, so a screen reader meets one link, "Pricing 1 open note Unpublished changes", described "Open thread", and reads none of it twice. A row whose verb is "Review and publish…" has no description; a tile has no reasons, so its name is its title.

"Mine" in the drafts panel means `author_id` for a post or, for any record including a page, that its *newest* audit event's user is me — "newest" ignoring `access.denied` (a refused attempt is no change), a note's events and a take-over of an edit reservation (`Dashboard::Feed::NOT_A_CHANGE`) and, for a page or post, also counting events about any content block it currently owns, since editing a block is editing its owner. It is decided in SQL by one `LATERAL` join to each record's newest audit event rather than a Ruby-side filter-after-load, so each list stays one query per model whatever the data size. **The page's statements are bounded**: 46 for the whole dashboard and 18 for Waiting for you's frame alone, the same for one set of every kind of row as for twenty (`test/integration/admin/dashboard_test.rb`, "the dashboard renders in the same number of queries with 1 or 20 records per list"; `test/integration/admin/dashboard_waiting_test.rb`, "Waiting for you costs the same statements with one set of every kind as with twenty"; each test builds every kind: an image thread, a page thread, a page and a post from the feed alone, a post with both reasons, for a viewer who may publish both). **A panel with nothing in it says one quiet line**, left-aligned in dim where its first row would be (`PanelComponent`'s `p[data-panel-empty]`, not `Admin::Ui::EmptyStateComponent`, which stays for every other list): "No drafts.", "Nothing scheduled.", Waiting for you's line for its filter, "No recent activity.", and no hint under a short list. The `empty_action` slot is drawn after the line, only while the panel has no rows: "Start a draft" in Pick up where you left off (a link to the standalone New post page that opens the `new-post-dialog`, drawn for `posts.create`) and "Drafts →" in Publishing on its own (the posts list's `scope=drafts`, for whoever holds `posts.read`, since that list holds every draft the viewer can read and "Your drafts" would claim a filter it does not have). The link is ink and underlined, because ink on dim text is not 3:1. Every word on the dashboard is a key: the view's under `admin.dashboard.show.*`, the components' under `admin.components.dashboard_screen.*` (`config/locales/admin/dashboard_screen.en.yml`).

**What changed recently** (`Dashboard::Activity`) is the newest *content* audit events (pages, posts, blocks, library images; never `access.denied`, `session.*`, `user.*`, `group.*`, `token.*` or `settings.*`; the allow-list is `Dashboard::Activity::ACTIONS`), one sentence each — "<name> published “<title>”" with the title linking to the record's editor (`Admin::DashboardScreen::ActivityRowComponent`) — looking back `Dashboard::Activity::WINDOW` (90 days). The read scope is applied *in the event query itself* (`audit_events.subject_id IN (readable ids)` per subject type, a block event through its readable owner, images for a `media.upload` holder), never by over-fetching and filtering in Ruby, which under-filled the feed and leaked how much hidden activity there was; a record the viewer can't read, a trashed one or a deleted one leaves no row at all ("not found, not forbidden"), a trashed record's whole history disappearing with it until it is restored. `index_audit_events_on_recent` serves the `ORDER BY last_occurred_at DESC` scan. The query returns data only: a `Dashboard::Activity::Row` is `(event, record, sentence, at, span_end, count)`, the sentence an `Audit::Sentence` (see the audit log below) whose actor is facts, not a name — the viewer ("You"), a user, a `user_id` that names nobody any more, or nobody — and `ActivityRowComponent` hands it to `Admin::AuditScreen::SentenceComponent`, which is where a user is named, through `Admin::Ui::UserNameComponent` (the one place CS Studio names a user): the `name_or_email`, "a deleted user" when `user_id` names a user who does not exist (`audit_events` has no FK on it; a token event whose user is gone reads the same), "System" only for an event with no user at all. A change made through an API token shows its *user* (round avatar) "via <token name>" beside an MCP chip (`Admin::Ui::ChannelChipComponent`, which draws an unknown channel as a plain chip and nothing for none), the name being the `audit_events.actor_label` written when the event was recorded (`Audit::Event#via_label`; it survives the token being deleted, and an event recorded before tokens had names reads "via an API token"), the token itself never loaded. A coalesced burst shows the time it last extended, the whole span as its tooltip. A **run** is one row with a `count`: events of `Dashboard::Activity::COLLAPSING` (`Audit::Sentence::COUNTED`: blocks added or updated, uploads) by the same person on the same record (any image, for uploads), each at most `Audit::COALESCE_WINDOW` older than the run's newest, read as "added 4 Code blocks to “…”" / "uploaded 12 images". The runs are joined in Ruby over the events the one query already read (`READ_FACTOR` times the limit, then the first `limit` rows), after unreadable and gone records are left out, so the statement count is unchanged; a feed whose newest events all collapse shows fewer rows than the limit. A row never states a false number: when the read was full (`limit * READ_FACTOR` events) its last run may have been cut short, so that run is dropped when other rows stand beside it, and a LONE cut run is a floor, "60 or more" (`at_least:` on `Audit::Sentence.build`), showing the newest event's own times and not a start the read never reached; a complete run beside a cut one stays exact. **The row's actor** is `Admin::Ui::ActorAvatarComponent`, the audit log's rows' and Trash's "Trashed" column's too, so one person looks the same in all three: a slot of 28px here (`size: :lg`) and of 22px in the audit log's and Trash's denser rows (`size: :sm`), holding, for a user, the shared `Admin::Ui::AvatarComponent` at the slot's size with two letters, `PersonName.initials(PersonName.of(user))` (the rule notes and presence use, so an account with no name gets the letters of the part of its address before the at sign), in the `:own` tone (filled ink) for the viewer's own events, as notes draws them, and `:other` for anyone else; the viewer's own row shows their own letters, not "You", and the sentence beside the circle keeps the full address. A deleted user is the `:empty` circle, a hairline and never dashed (dashed is away; an event records no user's name, so there are no letters to draw); System is not a person and is the neutral settings icon chip (`Admin::Ui::IconChipComponent`, 24px in the 28px slot, 20px in the 22px one) centred in the slot; an event nobody made (a failed sign-in, a visitor's send) has no circle, an empty slot that keeps the sentences aligned (`test/components/admin/dashboard_screen/activity_row_component_test.rb` and `test/components/admin/audit_screen/row_component_test.rb`: "a person is the shared avatar with two letters; the viewer's own is filled; the system is the neutral icon chip; an event nobody made draws no circle", "the same user's circle is the same in the audit log and on the dashboard, but for its size"). Nobody draws a circle of a person's letters but `AvatarComponent` (`test/lib/admin_design_rules_test.rb`, "nobody draws a circle of initials but AvatarComponent", with `HAND_BUILT_AVATAR_ALLOWED`, a list that may only shrink: the users list and the two scripts that build a circle in the browser).

**The audit log** (`Admin::AuditEventsController`, `audit.view`, never content-scoped) tells every event as one sentence through `Audit::Sentence` (`app/models/audit/sentence.rb`: plain data -- actor facts, parts, a route name + params for the subject, a `security` flag -- never markup or a component; `Admin::AuditScreen::SentenceComponent` draws it), the dashboard's feed words its rows through the same class so the two can't differ (only the feed passes a count and collapses a run; the log tells every event on its own, `docs/reference/access-control-and-audit.md`, "Counted sentences"). A sentence for an action outside `Audit::Sentence::ACTIONS` (a plugin's) is the generic "<actor> did <action> on <subject>"; the actions in `Audit::Sentence::SECURITY_ACTIONS` (a failed sign-in, a refusal, an owner change, a token issued or revoked, a user or group deleted) carry the `security` flag, and the row and the event page's Action draw a warn "Security" state chip (`Admin::Ui::StateChipComponent`) before their key, which is meta like any other key, so the colour is never the only cue. `test/models/audit/sentence_test.rb` greps `app/` for every recorded action name and fails for one without a sentence (interpolated and variable call sites are listed explicitly there). A subject links only when the viewer may open it, decided for the whole page of events in a bounded number of queries (`Audit::Sentence::Targets`); an unreadable, trashed or deleted one is the label the event stored, as plain text, and a failed sign-in never echoes the attempted address. A refusal says what it was refused on when the event names a subject ("was refused permission to edit posts on" and the subject), and a schedule taken off a page or post reads "removed the schedule from". The list is `last_occurred_at DESC, id DESC` in one card under day bands in the site time zone (`Admin::AuditScreen::DayListComponent`: each band a paper group row that sticks under the top bar while its day's rows scroll, held inside its day's section so the next day's band takes over; the card is `isolate` and clips nothing, so the bands stick to the window and stand over the rows' raised subject links at `raised-top`), filtered by `Audit::Filter` (user, action, type, channel, a `range` preset or custom `from`/`to`, a `subject_type`+`subject_id` chip; junk is "all"), "Load older" over `offset` (it replaces the page; "Newer" goes back). A row's actor is the dashboard's (`Admin::Ui::ActorAvatarComponent`, above). A coalesced row shows its span ("16:05–16:31", `Admin::AuditScreen::TimeSpan`), and `audit_events.occurrences` (1, incremented by `Audit::Coalescer` each time an event merges into the row) is the "N saves" on the event page ("N times" for anything but a `*.updated` event: twelve refusals are not twelve saves). The event page (`EventHeaderComponent`, `ChangesComponent`) shows field changes as word diffs per language (`Translations::SourceDiff` drawn by `Admin::Ui::WordDiffComponent`, shared with the translation reference card), "Changed" for a body, and Older/Newer through the same filtered order, every link on the page keeping the filters. The event is found by id whatever the filter says: one that exists but lies outside the active filter (a link carrying `range=today`, opened the next day) is answered 303 to its own filter-free address, the language kept, where Older and Newer walk the whole log; only an id that names nothing is a 404. Components live under `Admin::AuditScreen::`, never `Admin::Audit` (see "Key Conventions").

Every CS Studio and MCP request also runs inside the **site time zone** (`site_settings.time_zone`, default `"UTC"`, stored as a Rails zone name — an IANA identifier like `"Europe/Berlin"` is normalised to its Rails name on save, because the Settings select's option values are Rails names and every Save resends the whole form; the select also always lists the stored value, so a value written around the model is resent unchanged rather than replaced by the first option): `SiteSetting#effective_time_zone` resolves the stored name to an `ActiveSupport::TimeZone`, falling back to UTC for a value that doesn't resolve (a blank or no-longer-valid stored zone) rather than letting `Time.use_zone` raise and 500 every request; `Admin::BaseController` and `McpController` each wrap their actions in `around_action :use_site_time_zone { Time.use_zone(Current.site.effective_time_zone) { ... } }`. That's what lets the existing `Time.zone.parse`/`strftime` call sites (schedule inputs, "Saved HH:MM", the dashboard, the trash and audit screens) show and parse local time without change — storage stays UTC throughout, and MCP's own dates are still emitted as ISO-8601, now with the site's offset rather than always `Z`. The public site (and `/preview`) still renders dates in UTC — tracked in the roadmap, since it needs the render cache to vary on the zone.

**Notes on the dashboard and in the sidebar** (`docs/reference/notes.md`, "The inbox"). The dashboard has two panels about notes, each a frame of its own that can be fetched again by itself: **Notes for you** (`turbo-frame#notes-for-you`, `Dashboard::NotesForYou`: the viewer's deliveries on records they may read now, unread first, five rows, "N NEW" in accent, "Mark all read" and a link to the full list at `<admin>/notes`) and **Waiting for you** (`turbo-frame#dashboard-waiting`, `Dashboard::WaitingForYou`: one row per readable record that has open threads or unpublished changes, with its open-thread count in the bubble's read look, filtered by Everything, Open threads or Unpublished changes). The sidebar's Dashboard entry carries the viewer's unread count (`#sidebar-badge-dashboard`, `Admin::BaseController#notes_badge_count`): the one filled badge of the sidebar, accent because accent in a marker means "unread and addressed to you" and nothing else; Trash's stays a plain mono number. It costs exactly one statement per page load. `admin--notes--inbox` on `<body>` fetches the badge and, on the dashboard, both frames again when the person's stream says something was addressed to them (`#reload`): a frame drawn with the page has no `src` and is given its address (the layout's `data-admin--notes--inbox-waiting-url-value` and the card's), while a frame that has one, because a filter link of Waiting for you navigated it, reloads THAT `src`, so the person's filter stays; on any other screen there is no frame and nothing is fetched (`test/system/admin/note_inbox_test.rb`: "a filter chosen in Waiting for you survives a live reload of the panel", "a live note on a screen without the dashboard fetches no panel"). What changed recently lists a note's start and its resolve only (`Dashboard::Activity::ACTIONS`), and "Mine" and "Changed by" never count a note as a change to the record (`Dashboard::Feed::NOT_A_CHANGE`). Presence is not shown on the dashboard, in the mind graph or in the media grid (`docs/reference/presence.md`, "What is not built"); the dashboard's panels and the sidebar's badge stay direct code, named exceptions in `test/lib/notes_boundary_test.rb` with the trigger that would make each a slot.

## Shell

**Shell.** The sidebar's modules are a registry, `StudioModules` (`app/models/studio_modules.rb`), filled in `config/initializers/studio_modules.rb` in `to_prepare` after `reset!`, as `Admin::Slots` is, so a development reload fills it again. An entry is a name (its label is `admin.modules.<name>`), an icon and its chip family (`Admin::Ui::IconChipComponent`, required), a route helper's name, a permission (a key, any of an Array, or nil for everyone), a `group:` (`:content`, `:people`, `:system`, headed by `admin.module_groups.<group>`), an optional `visible:` callable and an optional badge (`StudioModules.badge(name, style: :plain | :filled) { |viewer:| count }`: the Dashboard's unread notes, `:filled`, the accent pill, and the Trash's count, `:plain`; the style is the entry's `badge_style`, an unknown one is refused, and the sidebar reads it by name and names no module). Every module names its family and `register` requires it, so a gem's module names one too: Dashboard, Pages and Mind graph `:structure`, Posts and Tags `:lists`, Media `:media`, Forms `:form`, Translations `:text`, Users and Groups `:people`, Trash, Settings, AI assistant, System status and Audit log `:code`. A missing family is Ruby's missing keyword, and `:neutral` (a block type's chip when it names none), `:accent` or an unknown family is refused by name. A gem registers its module through `ContentSpiel::Plugins.studio_modules { |modules| modules.register …; modules.badge(…) { |viewer:| … } }` (`lib/content_spiel/plugins.rb`): the block is handed `register` and `badge` alone, with the registry's own arguments, is kept for the life of the process, and is replayed by `config/initializers/studio_modules.rb` after the built-ins on every `to_prepare`, as `block_types` replays a type. A module's name is `owner/name` under the block types' owner rules (the key of its label, `admin.modules.<owner/name>`, in the gem's locale file), and a hook sets the badge of its own modules only, never a built-in's (`docs/reference/content-blocks-and-types.md`, "A sidebar module from outside"); a hook that raises stops the boot naming its place, and on a development reload is logged with the built-in modules registered and the other initializers' `to_prepare` run (`test/lib/content_spiel/plugins_test.rb`: "a module registered ahead of the application's prepare, as a gem's own to_prepare would, is wiped by it", "a module and its badge registered through the hook survive every prepare, after the built-ins", "a module hook is handed register and badge alone, and a module without a family, with a bare name, a second owner or a taken owner is refused, naming the place of the call", "a module hook sets the badge of a module it registered and of no other: a built-in's badge stays the built-in's", "a module hook that raises on a reload is logged, and leaves the built-in modules and every other registry filled"). Tests: `test/models/studio_modules_test.rb`, `test/components/admin/shell/sidebar_component_test.rb`. `Admin::BaseController#admin_modules` is the entries this viewer may see, as rows for the sidebar and ⌘K (`StudioModules::Entry#to_row`: `{ name:, label:, icon:, family:, path:, permission:, group: }`, `name:` keying the badges and the badge ids `sidebar-badge-<name>`; `Admin::JumpHelper.routes` takes every module's path), `#admin_module_badges` their counts, one statement each; `Admin::Shell::SidebarComponent` renders the groups as sections (Dashboard stays first) and names no module, reading each badge's style from the registry; the sidebar also holds the ⌘K field, the account block and the theme switch. The account block is `Admin::Shell::AccountIdentityComponent` (`#account-identity`: `name_or_email`, then "email · role" on one line, the role being "Owner", up to two group names then "+N", or "No groups" — its own component because the account screen's Save replaces just it by stream, and a link to that screen), then Your account and Sign out. ## System status

**System status** (`Admin::SystemStatusController`, `system_status.view`): "N of M checks passed · last run HH:MM" (plural by the number of checks) under the heading, then one card of rows divided inside it (`setup/_system_check_results` with `list_class:`; the wizard keeps the partial's ruled list), warnings first, each check an `Admin::Ui::SystemCheckRowComponent` (the setup wizard draws the same rows with `copyable: false`), whose state is `Admin::Ui::StateChipComponent`, ok "OK" or warn "Warning" (`admin.components.ui.system_check_row.*`); there is no summary chip. A check may carry a console `command`, shown in the copy box (`Admin::Ui::CopyValueComponent`, on `admin--ui--clipboard`), its Copy named for the check; the row's required `index:` keeps the command's id one of a kind. "Run checks again" is a GET with prefetch off, because the storage check writes and deletes a probe.

Two checks come first and are about where the site is reached. **Site address** names the configured origin (`SiteAddress.current`: "This site answers at https://www.example.org."), or warns that none is set, with what follows from that and `PUBLIC_HOST=https://www.example.org` to copy (`Setup::SystemCheck::SITE_ADDRESS_COMMAND`, a fixed string like the mail command). The warning has two wordings, because what follows differs: addresses in pages follow the request everywhere, but a password reset mail takes its link from the request in development and test and is not sent at all anywhere else, and "any host name is accepted" is said only in the second, since development refuses a made-up name by Rails' own list (`docs/reference/hosting.md`, "The reset mail"). Both screens hand the check `reset_link_from_request: PasswordsMailer.request_origin_used?`, a boolean, so the model names no mailer; a caller that hands nothing gets the wording that reset mails are not sent. **HTTPS** is in the list in production only, and only when the caller said how the request came: both screens hand over `request_ssl: request.ssl?`, and a caller that hands nothing (the console) gets no row. With an `https` address plain HTTP is redirected before it reaches the app, so the warning is seen only where the address is an `http` one or missing. An address that is configured as `http` on a page that was reached over HTTPS is a warning too ("The address is http but this page was reached over HTTPS: set PUBLIC_HOST to the https address."), so that the two rows do not both say OK while every address the site prints is still http. "Encrypted" is what `request.ssl?` says, which is the proxy's word (`X-Forwarded-Proto`) or `ASSUME_SSL=1`'s, not something the application can see for itself. And the **Mail** row is `MailSettings#state` in `MailStateSentence`'s words, the Settings card's own sentence: OK for Working, a warning for the other three, a failure said by its short reason and never the server's line, a copyable console password command (`MAIL_FALLBACK_COMMAND`, through `Users::UpdatePassword`) while mail is not ready, and "Open Mail settings →" for a viewer who holds `settings.manage` (`Setup::SystemCheck` is handed `mail_settings_link:`, the result carries `link: :mail_settings` and `Admin::Ui::SystemCheckRowComponent` resolves it; the wizard hands none). The same fact raises `Admin::Ui::MailFailedNoticeComponent` at the top of the dashboard, for `settings.manage` only and only while the last send failed (`test/models/setup/system_check_test.rb`: "the Mail row answers each state with its words", "System status shows a failure's sentence and never the server's line"; `test/integration/admin/dashboard_test.rb`: "the notice is shown to settings.manage only, and only while the last send failed").

**The plain-HTTP notice** (`Admin::Ui::InsecureNoticeComponent`, `#insecure-notice`) says the same thing where a person would otherwise not look: in production, on a request that is not `request.ssl?`, one warn alert (the compact warn `Admin::Ui::NoticeComponent`, `role="alert"`). The `product` layout draws it for everyone above the panel of sign-in, both password pages and the invitation, where a password is typed; the `admin` layout draws it at the top of `<main>`, outside `#content`, for someone who holds `settings.manage` or `system_status.view` (`Admin::BaseController#insecure_notice_viewer?`, navigation and not a gate; each of the two rights is enough alone, and `languages.manage`, which also shows the Settings link, is not one of them), since an editor can do nothing about it. The `setup` layout draws it for everyone at the top of every wizard page, since the wizard takes the setup token and the first account's password (`test/integration/admin/insecure_notice_test.rb`, `test/integration/setup_early_steps_test.rb`).

## The preview bar

**The preview bar** (`Public::PreviewBarComponent`, rendered first in the public layout on `/preview…` only): a fixed 36px bar, "PREVIEW · <title> · <Draft | Scheduled | Live> · <language>", Edit and Exit preview. It is the public site's chrome: the public stylesheet only, no admin JavaScript, and its colours are the theme tokens `--th-preview-bg` / `--th-preview-ink`, which do not switch with light and dark. Edit opens the record's editor in the previewed language. Exit preview goes to the record's public URL in that language when it is live, and otherwise to CS Studio's list (the pages landing for a page, the posts list for a post) — there is no public page to leave for.

**The tree's rows** show a page's state with one small named mark after the title (`Admin::PagesScreen::TreeStateMarkComponent`): a hollow 6px ring in `meta` for Draft, a filled `warn` dot for Scheduled, a `dim` dot for live with unpublished changes, and nothing for a plain live page. It is `role="img"` with an `aria-label` and a tooltip in the words the status chip and the unpublished chip use, so colour is never the only cue; a row has ONE mark (a scheduled page with unpublished changes draws the scheduled dot, named "Scheduled · Unpublished changes"), and it reads the row's own columns, so it costs no statement. The tree draws no chip (`UnpublishedChipComponent` has no compact mode). The selected row has a sunken ground, a 600 title and a 3px inset accent bar (`.admin-tree-row--selected`, an inset shadow, so the title does not move; a selection, not a marker). The "○N" and "↻N" markers rest clipped (still read by a screen reader; shown on hover, on focus within the row and for a coarse pointer) and are always shown on the selected row; that is all the selection reveals: the handle and the "+" show on it as on any row, never by the selection (`test/lib/admin_design_rules_test.rb`: "no tree rule reveals the handle or the + from the selected row"). One ring marks the focus, on the focused control and never on the row ("no tree row draws an outline from :has(:focus-visible)"; `test/system/admin/tree_test.rb`: "a focused control in a Pages row draws one ring, its own, and the row none"). "+ New page" is a small secondary button. **What redraws the tree.** The tree reads the viewer's visible, kept pages in one statement with only the columns its rows read (`PageTree.rows`, `PageTree::COLUMNS`, `app/queries/page_tree.rb`; a column a row starts reading raises `ActiveModel::MissingAttributeError` until it is named there), ordered `position, id`, which is `TreeOrder`'s order (`tree_component_test.rb`: "the tree's read, TreeOrder and the drawn tree give each parent's children in one order"). Its shape changes only through a move, a create or a trash, which redraw the whole tree (`Admin::TreeController#tree_stream`, a redirect). The tree column outlives a frame navigation, and a frame answer carries no tree (`app/views/admin/pages/_tree.html.erb`, the views' check and never the component's, so a stream's `replace("tree")` always draws it; `pages_tree_row_test.rb`: "an editor opened in the frame sends no tree"; `test/integration/admin/tree_test.rb`: "a move asked from inside a frame still answers the whole tree"). So the selected row is kept by the tree's own script: the page editor names its page in `#content` (`[data-tree-open-page]`), and `admin--pages--tree` marks exactly that row (the shared row's classes, handed over as Stimulus classes from `TreeItemComponent::CLASSES[:row_selected]` and `[:reveal]`, with `aria-current="page"`; the selected title's weight is the block's `.admin-tree-row--selected` rule, not a class the script writes) as each row connects and after every load of the content frame, so a redrawn row, a move's redrawn tree and a restored snapshot are marked by the same rule. Nothing replaces the link that opened the page, so it keeps the focus (`test/system/admin/tree_test.rb`: "opening a page from the tree and saving it leaves that page's row the one selected", "a page opened from the tree by the keyboard keeps the focus on its link", "Back to a page opened from the tree selects that page's row"). A settings save changes one row, so its answer replaces `#tree-row-<id>` alone (`Admin::PagesController#tree_row_stream`, `TreeRowComponent.for_page`, which reads that page's slice in a fixed number of statements and draws the markup the whole tree draws for it: `tree_row_component_test.rb`; the slice starts from the page's row read afresh, `PageTree.row`, because the unpublished-changes mark is written beside the save by a statement of its own and the record the request holds does not carry it: `pages_tree_row_test.rb`, "a save of a live page answers its row with the unpublished-changes mark"); the `<li>` and its children list stay where they are, and a retitled page keeps its place, since the order is by position and never by title (`TreeOrder`, `TreeNodeComponent#children`). The save's answer reads none of the tree's pages and is the same size at any number of pages (`test/integration/admin/pages_tree_row_test.rb`: "a settings save costs the same statements for 3 pages as for 30, answers the same bytes and reads no tree").

**Folding, the open set and find** (`docs/reference/design-system.md`, "Tree", has the look and the rules; here is how Pages uses them). A parent's row has a disclosure (`admin--tree--disclosure#toggle`: a click, Enter or Space; Alt+click opens the whole branch) and its `<li>` carries `data-folded`, `data-tree-root` and `data-tree-on-path`; a folded node's children list is `hidden`, and a leaf is never folded, so its empty list stays a place to drop a page. **Which branches are folded is the open set**, the cookie `cs_tree_open` (`TreeOpenSet`, `app/values/tree_open_set.rb`, whose comment has the format: page ids joined by `.`, the newest first, a bare id opened beyond the default and `-` before an id folded where the default would show it open). The browser writes it through `tree/open_set.js` alone, and the server reads it to draw the first paint (`TreeComponent` takes `open_cookie:`, as do `TreeRowComponent.for_page` and the move's and a settings save's answers), so a folded branch is folded from the first frame and a restored Turbo snapshot is drawn again from the cookie when the disclosure controller connects. The reader holds the value to what it may be: bytes that are not valid text, a value over 4,096 bytes, entries past the first 300, tokens that are not an id, and ids the viewer cannot read, set nothing (`test/values/tree_open_set_test.rb`; `test/integration/admin/pages_tree_row_test.rb`: "a cookie that is not text leaves the Pages screen drawn"). The cookie's path is the admin path (`data-tree-open-path`), so a visitor's page never sees it and a change of `ADMIN_PATH` starts every browser unfolded. The rule is one for the server, the script and the graph's list (`TreeOpenSet#open?`): the path to the selected page is open whatever the cookie says, then a folded entry folds, then the root is open, then only an opened entry opens. **The path is a view state and is never stored:** as a page is opened (on connect, and on `follow` after a content-frame navigation) `openPathIn` forgets the folds on its path and moves the `data-tree-on-path` marks to the new page's path, then the column is scrolled so the selected row is in view, by setting the column's own `scrollTop` with one row's height to spare and only when the row is out of view (never `scrollIntoView`, which would move the window).

**A move and the folds.** `admin--pages--tree#save` writes the new parent open (`setOpen`) before every move is sent, a drop, a keyboard step and the nest (Right Arrow) alike, because the move's answer redraws the whole tree from the cookie and a page dropped into a folded or childless parent would otherwise vanish into the fold; a refused move leaves that entry, which is harmless. Right Arrow into a folded sibling unfolds it and the announcer says "Opened <title>. Position n of m." (`moves.opened`, one key holding the whole sentence). **A drag over a folded parent opens it** after 600ms (`OPEN_ON_HOVER_MS`) when the pointer rests in the middle half of its row: SortableJS moves the slot past a row as soon as the pointer enters it, so the middle half and the near quarter (the slot's side) hold the slot in place and only the far quarter lets it pass, and a pointer that leaves the row cancels the pending open. The opened parent is marked as the drop target (`CLASSES[:drop_target]`, `.admin-tree-drop-target`), stays open in the cookie (the redraw after the drop shows the page under it), and Escape ends opening on hover for the rest of that drag. A timer, a mark and the pointer listener are taken back at drop, at Escape, at `turbo:before-cache` and at disconnect (`test/system/admin/tree_test.rb`: "dragging a page over a folded parent opens it after a moment, and the page drops among its children", "a drag that leaves a folded parent before the moment does not open it", "a slot resting beside a folded parent does not open it", "a drag across an empty leaf list after passing a folded parent does not open it"; `test/system/tree_test.rb`: "moving a page right into a folded sibling opens it and says so", "a page moved into a page with no children is shown under it after the tree is redrawn").

**Find** (`Admin::Ui::TreeFindComponent`, `admin--tree--find`, `tree/find.js`; `test/system/admin/tree_find_test.rb`) sits under the head and above `#tree-status`, drawn only when the tree has a root. It runs on the client over the rows the tree drew, which is every page the viewer can read (`PageTree.rows`); `docs/reference/design-system.md`, "Tree", says where it would move at a size. A query matches a title anywhere in it, folding case and accents (NFD, marks dropped, the locale's lower-case, per character so a match maps back onto the original text); a query that starts with "/" matches the page's address instead (`data-tree-path`, from `PageTree.paths`, one statement over `public_paths` in the tree's language, a page with no live address there matching no address). The matched text is wrapped in `<mark>` built with `textContent` and `splitText`, never `innerHTML`, so a title with markup or a script is text ("a title with markup is marked as text"). Non-matching rows are `hidden`, the ancestors of a match stay, and a folded branch that holds a match is opened through `setFolded`, which stores nothing: emptying the field (or Escape in it, or the page being left) takes the marks, the hidden rows and the opened branches back and draws every node by the person's open set; a branch the person folds while filtered stays folded when the query runs again, and that fold is theirs and stored. The count is said in the status line (`role="status"`) 300ms after the last keystroke and only when the count changed, the figure drawn in the Stimulus class `figure`; the line holds the count only and keeps one line of room (the Stimulus class `reserve`, `min-h-[1lh]`) from the first keystroke for as long as the field holds a query, set in the same `input` that narrows the tree and taken off in the same step that gives it back, so the count written later moves no row under a pointer aiming at it ("the count's line and the moving sentence stand from the first key…", which measures at the column's least width too; `mind_graph_search_test.rb`: "the find's status line keeps its one line from the first key…"); "No pages match" shows and the tree is hidden when nothing does. **While the tree is narrowed it does not move:** the frame sets `data-filtered` on `ul#tree-list`, every Sortable is disabled, the handles are `display: none`, the keyboard pick-up is refused, and "Clear the search to move pages" (`tree_find.no_moving`, drawn by the server as the `moving` target after the status line and outside the live region, since it is a standing instruction and not news) shows in the same step as the narrowing, taking whatever height it wraps to, and is the field's `aria-describedby` while it shows; it is hidden and the description taken off in the step that gives the tree back. The keys are the controller's alone: "/" focuses the field from anywhere but a text-entry control (a text-like input, a textarea, a select, an editable element or an open dialog; a checkbox, a radio or a button leaves the key to the find), with a modifier held, or while the field is not visible (the shut drawer); Down Arrow goes to the first match, Up Arrow from it comes back to the field, Enter opens the first match, and Escape clears a non-empty field (stopping the key) and is not taken in an empty one, so it still closes a drawer. Before Turbo's snapshot the field, the hint, Clear, the status line and the filter are all taken back (`turbo:before-cache`), and `tree:changed` (a redrawn row, a disclosure) runs the query again once per animation frame. The mind graph's column draws the same find over its list (`noun: :nodes`; "Mind graph", "The find field and the last line").

The region has no height, sticky or scroll of its own: the column keeps its own scroll (below), so a long list scrolls the column and not the region. The mind graph's list fills, so its last line stands under a short tree and the column's ground reaches the window's foot; the Pages tree is as tall as its rows (`shell_helper_test.rb`: "the mind graph's list fills the column, and the Pages tree does not"; `test/system/admin/tree_column_test.rb`: "the mind graph's column takes the tree's width, divider and drag, and its width is the one Pages left", which also holds that the region reaches the column's bottom less its padding and that the column, not the region, scrolls). The width, the divider, the fold and the drawer are one for both subjects: a width chosen on Pages is the graph's, a fold on one screen is a fold on the other, and below 1024px each is the tree drawer (`test/system/admin/tree_fold_test.rb`: "the mind graph's column folds to its strip with its own words, and the canvas widens"; `tree_column_test.rb`: "below 1024px the list is the tree drawer, opened by the top bar's List button").

**The tree column** is as wide as the person made it (`Admin::Shell::TreeDividerComponent`, `admin--shell--tree-width`, `admin/shared/_tree_head`). The divider is the last child of `aside#tree-column`, outside what the screen draws in it (on Pages, outside `turbo-frame#tree`): a `role="separator"` in the Tab order that says its width in pixels (`aria-valuenow`, `-min`, `-max`), an 8px hit area centred on the column's own 1px line and as tall as the window. At rest it is that line; 150ms after a pointer arrived it is 2px `--cs-line-strong` with the `col-resize` cursor (`data-hover`, the script's, so a pointer passing over makes nothing flicker); while dragged and on keyboard focus 2px `--cs-accent`. The pointer drags it, a double click resets it; Left and Right step 16px, with Shift 64px, Home and End go to the limits, Enter resets. A reset is the default (the property and the stored choice both go). The shared tooltip, on focus and hover, reads the subject's sentence with the current figure ("Tree width 272px. Arrow keys to resize." on Pages), and hangs from an anchor 3rem below the top of the divider, which is as tall as the window.

**The column scrolls on its own** from 1024px (`.admin-shell__tree`: sticky at `--admin-top-offset`, window-high, `overflow-y: auto`, `overscroll-behavior: contain`, `scrollbar-gutter: stable`), so a long tree scrolls inside it and the window does not move. A scroller clips what spills out sideways, so the divider, which overhangs the column's edge by 3.5px, is `position: fixed` from 1024px on that edge (`top` at `--admin-top-offset`, `bottom` 0, `left` the sidebar's width where it is a column plus `--admin-tree-column`, minus 4.5px) and the fold strip follows the sticky column. Below 1024px the column is the drawer and nothing changes (`test/system/admin/tree_column_test.rb`: "a selected row deep in a long tree is in view inside the column, and the page did not scroll"). **No guard** for the divider's position at 1024 to 1279px or at a chosen width beyond the default: `--admin-tree-room`'s `100%` resolves against the viewport there and against the shell in the grid, and the two agree only because CS Studio keeps `scrollbar-gutter: stable`.

- **The numbers and their unit.** The design's figures are the component's constants: minimum 220, default 272 (the 17rem the column always was), maximum 480, and never so wide that the editor column has less than 640 beside the modules sidebar (240 from 1280px, where it is a column). They are **pixels at a root font size of 16px**, and the shell is laid out in rem (13.75rem, 17rem, 30rem, 40rem, 15rem), so the head script and the controller multiply each by the root's computed font size over 16 before they use it (the controller's `scale`). At a browser font of 20px the sidebar is 300px, the tree 340px between 275px and 600px, and a step 20px. `aria-valuenow` and the tooltip say CSS pixels of the real column. The first build counted the figures as pixels in the scripts: at 20px the separator said 272 for a 340px column, Right made it narrower and a drag began with a jump (`test/system/admin/tree_column_test.rb`: "at a browser font size of 20px the separator says the real column, and the keys and a drag start from it"; `test/components/admin/shell/tree_divider_component_test.rb`: "the stylesheet holds every figure in rem, as the constants over the base: none as pixels"). The head script is rendered after the stylesheet's link, so the font size it reads is the one the page is laid out with.
- **Where the width lives.** `--admin-tree-width` on `<html>` is the CHOSEN width; without it the column is 17rem. The stylesheet's `--admin-tree-column` is what the grid shows: the choice held between the limits, so a width chosen on a wide window does not squeeze the editor on a narrow one and is back when there is room; a key that could only be cut back to what is already shown (Right or End at a window-clamped width) writes nothing, so it does not throw the wider choice away. It is on `<html>` because Turbo replaces `<body>` and merges `<head>` and never the root element: a visit, a page picked in the tree and Back find it as it was. It is remembered per browser in `localStorage["cs-tree-width"]`, a convenience: every read and write is guarded, and where storage is refused the document holds the choice for as long as it lives. **Only the head script reads the storage**, before the first paint, so the page never changes width when the controller connects; the controller works from the property and writes the storage.
- **The notes take none of the tree's room.** The editors' notes are the rail's Notes tab, or a slide-over or a sheet over the canvas (`docs/reference/notes.md`, "The Notes tab and the markers"), so the tree never folds or narrows for them, and its limits do not count them (`test/system/admin/notes_rail_test.rb`: "the tree never folds for the notes: opening them at 1280px leaves the tree a column").
- **Where there is no divider.** Below 1024px the tree is a drawer of a fixed 17rem, which shows no divider.
- **The fold.** The hide button, a small tertiary icon button at the end of the column's head (`Admin::Ui::TreeHideComponent`, `.admin-tree-hide`; on Pages inside the tree's frame), "Hide page tree" or "Hide list" by the column's subject, folds the column to a 44px strip (`--admin-tree-strip`, 2.75rem, `TreeDividerComponent::STRIP`), and "Show page tree" or "Show list", the strip's one button (`Admin::Shell::TreeFoldComponent`, `.admin-tree-fold`, the column's first child, outside the frame), unfolds it. Both are `Admin::Ui::ButtonComponent` with `icon:` (the icon, the label as `aria-label` and the shared tooltip's text), both `aria-controls="tree-column"`, and both say `aria-expanded` (written by the script on both as the fold changes); a press moves the focus to the other one. The fold is `data-tree-folded` on `<html>` and "1" in `localStorage["cs-tree-folded"]`, set before the first paint by the head script as the width is, and by the controller from then on, which stands on `aside#tree-column` (`TreeDividerComponent.column_data(subject:)`) with the divider, the two buttons as its `divider`, `fold` and `unfold` targets. The fold is a third state of the width and touches none of it: `--admin-tree-width` keeps the CHOSEN width, and only the grid's track (`--admin-tree-column`) is the strip, so an unfold is back at the chosen width with nothing read from the storage. From 1024px only: below, the tree is the ordinary drawer, the two buttons are not drawn and the fold is back when the window is wide again; the drawer does not offer the hide button, since folding a drawer means nothing (`test/system/admin/tree_fold_test.rb`: "Hide page tree folds the column to a strip, the editor widens, and Show page tree brings it back at its chosen width", "a fold is remembered: the next screen is laid out folded from the first paint", "a folded tree is the ordinary drawer under 1024px, and folded again at 1600px", "a folded tree stays the strip when the notes open, at a docked and at a narrower window", "the fold buttons say what they control"). Folded, nothing but the strip stands between the sidebar and the editor: the editor area starts 68px right of the sidebar (the 44px strip and `<main>`'s 24px padding) at any window and whatever width was chosen, and so does its composition up to the content max; past it the composition is centred in the area, the room left of it equal to the room right of it (`docs/reference/design-system.md`, "E4: the right rail"; `test/system/admin/tree_column_test.rb`: "folded, the editor starts 68px right of the sidebar at 1600px, and at 2600px its composition is centred in the area that does"). The shell's drawer moves the focus to its first control that is drawn (`checkVisibility()`), so the strip's button, first in the column and not drawn in a drawer, never takes it. The room a fold gives is the editor area's, so it is what docks the page editor's rail at a 1600px window (`docs/reference/design-system.md`, "E4: the right rail"; `test/system/admin/editor_rail_test.rb`: "folding the tree at 1600px docks the rail").
- **A drag** begins with a press of the primary button without Ctrl (Ctrl-click is the context menu on macOS, which swallows the release) and is listened for on the window until it ends; the press also captures the pointer (`setPointerCapture`), which keeps the moves coming outside the window. The capture is an addition, not the mechanism, because Chrome drops one without a `lostpointercapture` on a mouse move that names `buttons` but no `button`, which is what Ferrum's `mouse.move` sends (measured 2026-10-13, Ferrum 0.18.0; with `button: "left"` the capture holds, which is how the tests' `move_pressed` sends its moves). **A drag always has an end**: the release; a mouse's move with `buttons === 0` (let go where the page did not hear it), the window's `blur` and a lost capture, all three keeping the width; and `pointercancel`, which puts the width back where the press found it and stores nothing.
- **What is taken back.** `data-dragging` and `data-hover` on the divider and `data-tree-resizing` on `<html>` go with the pointer, and again at `turbo:before-cache` and on disconnect. The width and the fold are the document's state and stay.
- **In tests** (`test/system/admin/tree_column_test.rb`): every width is one the test set, compared to a pixel; the keyboard reaches the divider by Tab from the tree's last control (a focus given by script is not always a keyboard's to the browser, and the tooltip shows for a keyboard's only); the divider is found by its class, because `role="separator"` is also a line in a menu; and the teardown removes `cs-tree-width` (and `cs-tree-folded`) as a notes test removes `cs-notes-open`, because the browser is shared. A test that looks for the drawer's "Pages" button names the drawer trigger (`[data-admin--shell--drawer-target=trigger]`): the fold's two buttons are `aria-controls="tree-column"` too.

## Mind graph

**Mind graph** (`Admin::MindGraphController`, the `PostGraph` queries in `app/queries/post_graph/`, `admin--mind-graph--graph` and `admin--mind-graph--swatches` over the modules in `app/javascript/mind_graph/`, the components in `app/components/admin/mind_graph_screen/`). Two specs: the canvas (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-mind-graph-canvas-design.md`, which holds the first measurement and what the build decided: motion, pinning, the card beside its node, the label as part of the target, the list beside the canvas) and pages (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-12-pages-in-the-mind-graph-design.md`, whose "As built" holds every place the build left the approved text, and the measurement of 2026-10-13 at 204 pages and 2,005 posts). How the site hangs together: the pages and their tree, what each page's list blocks point at, and how posts relate through related-post links and shared tags, drawn on one 2D canvas and laid out by d3-force in a Web Worker. Read-only: a page is moved in the page tree, a list block is changed in the page editor, relations are edited in the post editor. **It opens for someone who holds `pages.read` or `posts.read` anywhere** (`require_graph_access`; the sidebar's entry is shown for either): a graph of pages alone is a graph. Each kind is then narrowed by its own scope, and everything the screen shows or counts is derived from the pages and posts the viewer may read.

**It shows THE ROWS, not the published copies, by design**. CS Studio is the editors' view: the tree, the posts list and the editors all show the rows, and so does this. A lists line follows a list block's settings as they are being edited, so on a live page with unpublished changes a line can be one visitors do not get yet; the page's own mark says so (the corner dot, below), and that mark is the only cue. The other way round was rejected: reading the copies of live records and the rows of drafts would be two sources in one picture and a second writing of `Publishing::Listing`. Nothing under `app/queries/post_graph/` reads a published copy, and nothing there or in the scripts decides a record's state: a node's `status` and `changes` are `Publishing::State`'s (below). `test/lib/mind_graph_guards_test.rb` holds both, by refusing every line that NAMES `published_at`, one of `Publishable`'s scopes that compare it, or a copy, unless it is one of a short list of exact lines that only carry the column to where the rule is kept. What a name cannot see is held otherwise: a post's row carries a RECENCY, a number, and never the publication time (`PostGraph::Site.rows_of`; a row that carried the time as a place in an array could be compared under any name a block parameter gave it), and no script names a scheduled change's time or reads a clock for a date, so a look cannot be decided in the browser by comparing a time with now ("no script of the mind graph reads the clock for a date or names a scheduled change's time: a look is what the server answered").

**Three views, one screen.** `GET /<admin>/mind_graph` is the **whole graph**: every readable page and the best-connected readable posts, up to a cap. `?page=<id>`, `?post=<id>` or `?tag=<id>`, with `&depth=1|2`, is a **local** view: that page's, post's or tag's neighbourhood. A depth other than 2 is read as 1, and **a page wins over a post, a post over a tag** when more than one is given (`PostGraph::Focus.find`; the data URL carries every focus parameter the address had and the data picks the same one, while the bar's depth links carry only the winner). Changing the focus or the depth is an ordinary visit, so Back works. The third view is the one a large site opens in, the local view around Home with a bar that says why ("A site with too many lines", below): it has no address of its own. Four JSON reads belong to the screen and share its permission and scope (`config/routes.rb`, each `format: :json` by default): `mind_graph/data` with the same parameters answers the graph (and takes `everything=1`, which the page itself does not), `mind_graph/search?q=` the whole site's search, which the list's last line asks, `mind_graph/unlisted` the posts no page lists and `mind_graph/listed?page=<id>&target=<target>` the posts behind one "+N more" pill. **A focus outside the viewer's scope is not found, never forbidden**: `PostGraph::Focus.find` raises `ActiveRecord::RecordNotFound` for an unknown id, a trashed page or post, one outside the read scope, and a tag that neither a readable post carries nor a readable page's list block names (a focus on it would say the tag exists and is used somewhere the viewer can't look; a tag a readable page lists is a node of the graph whatever carries it, so it is a focus too), and `Admin::BaseController` answers the 404 page, or a bare 404 for a JSON read. The page and `data` resolve the focus the same way, so they can't disagree. **An id is decimal digits and nothing else** (`\A\d+\z`, then base 10): without the base Ruby reads a prefix, so `?post=010` would be post 8 and `?post=0x1A` post 26, each a record other than the one the URL names, and `Integer` alone also takes `1_0`, a sign and surrounding spaces. Whatever is not a number names nothing and is a 404 (a word, a list such as `post[]=1`), while `q[]=x` on the search finds nothing and `depth=abc` is depth 1 (`test/integration/admin/mind_graph_data_test.rb`, `test/queries/post_graph/focus_test.rb`). **A focus that went between the request's finding it and the site's being loaded** (trashed, deleted, taken out of scope) is not found either, whatever its kind and before anything more is read: `PostGraph::Local` asks the site's own rows, which everything after is derived from, so the request that loses that race answers the 404 it would answer a moment later and never a 500 or an empty graph (`test/queries/post_graph/local_test.rb`: "a focus tag that went meanwhile is not found before anything but the site is read, and neither is one nothing readable uses any more"). `listed` finds its page as a focus is found, so the same holds for it; its `target` is only ever compared with the page's own targets, so a junk one, or one the page does not list, answers no rows and is echoed nowhere (an empty answer and not a 404: it says nothing a 404 would hide, since "a tag only an unreadable page lists" and "nothing more behind this pill" are the same answer).

**A node's id is its kind and its database id**, everywhere: `page-12`, `post-12`, `tag-12`, a post type's `type-<stored name>`, the one `all-posts`, a pill's `more-<target>`. `PostGraph.node_id` is the one writer on the server (the payload's `id`, both ends of every line, `parent`, a search row, `focus.node_id`), and `mind_graph/ids.js` (`recordId`, which cuts at the FIRST dash) the one place the page takes an id apart, for the URL templates and the notes, which want the record's own id. The server takes one kind of id apart too, with no function for it: a list target's kind is read by its literal prefix `"tag-"` in `PostGraph::Local` (`reached?`, `brought`, the walk around a page) and in `PostGraph::Builder` (`plan`, the type nodes). Nothing ties those five lines to `node_id`: a change of the separator or of the kind's spelling must be made in both, or a page's local view loses its tags and their lines with no error. A post's id was the bare number until this build and only a tag's was prefixed; a page and a post of one database id would have been one node. The data has no `kind` key: a node's kind is the array it came in, and a line's ends carry none (the id says it). The system test's `nid` helper refuses a bare number for the same reason: two trash-visibility assertions that compared a node's id with a record's number went on passing against typed ids while checking nothing.

**The data.** `PostGraph::Global` and `PostGraph::Local` answer one shape, built by `PostGraph::Builder` (the shape is written out at the top of `app/queries/post_graph.rb`): `nodes` (the posts), `tags`, `pages`, `types`, `edges`, in a page's local view `more`, plus `total`, `shown` and `isolated` (whole) or `focus: { kind, id, node_id, title, depth }` and `site: { pages, posts, tags }` (local). A post: `{ id, title, type, colour_index, status, changes, change_at, listed, url, degree, related_count, tag_count, open_notes }`. A page: `{ id, title, path, depth, home, parent, ancestors, order, status, changes, change_at, url, degree, child_count, listed_count, open_notes }`, in tree order. A type: `{ id, name, label, colour_index, count, degree }`, by colour slot, then the retired types by name, then "all-posts" last. A line: `{ source, target, kind }`, of five kinds: `related` (two posts), `tag` (post to tag), `tree` (child to parent), `lists` (page to tag, type or all-posts) and `brings` (a page's local view only: hub to post, and hub or tag to its pill). `degree` is the number of lines the node has *in this response*, so a node's size and its label's priority match what is drawn, not what the site holds; a post's `related_count` and `tag_count` are two halves of it, counted where the lines are made so the card can say "3 related · 2 tags" without the page walking the lines (around a page a post can have one more, its hub's `brings` line). `total` is the posts the view could show (whole: the readable posts with at least one connection; local: the posts the walk reached), `shown` the posts in `nodes`, and `isolated` the readable posts with no connection, which the whole graph leaves out and counts in a sentence ("72 other posts have no connections yet and aren't shown.": "other" and "aren't shown" because the bare count read as a contradiction beside a canvas of connected posts; with nothing drawn at all it is `script.isolated_only`, "2 posts have no connections yet."). There is no colour in the data: a post and a type carry the content type's `colour_index` (nil for a type that is not registered, which is how a retired type's hub is told; "all-posts" has none either and is told by its id), and the page draws from tokens. `url` is the record's editor URL, built by the `url_for:` callable the controller passes (the route helper, with the editing language kept; called with a `Page` or a `Post`): a query knows no routes (`test/lib/dependency_direction_test.rb`), and `actor:` and `url_for:` have no default so nothing draws the whole graph, or a graph without links, by forgetting one. Posts and pages are loaded with the columns a node is made from (`Builder::COLUMNS`, `Site::PAGE_COLUMNS`), never a body. A post and a page also carry `open_notes`, the number of their open threads, which `PostGraph::Builder` reads for the ids it was handed in one grouped statement a kind (`Notes::Counts.by_container`) and the page draws as a neutral badge; the graph's data says nothing about what the viewer has read, which is said on the rows of the node's card, from the notes' own endpoint. **What went between the two reads goes whole** (`Builder#build`): the lines are planned from the site as it was loaded and the nodes are read afterwards, through the scope again, so a tag deleted in between has no name and a post trashed or taken out of the viewer's scope in between has no node. Both are dropped with every line that touches them and a pill on a gone tag, and every count is made from what is left (a node's `degree`, a post's `related_count` and `tag_count`, a tag's `count`, and the callers' `total` and `shown`); a focus post that went is not found. The page looks every end of a line up among its nodes, so a line to nothing fails the whole load: as first built for posts the node went and its lines stayed (`test/queries/post_graph/global_test.rb`: "a post that is trashed between the site's read and the nodes' goes whole: no node, no line to it, and every count is of what is left"; `local_test.rb`: "around a post, around a tag and around a page, a post trashed between the site's read and the nodes' goes whole, and a focus post that goes is not found").

**What the three counts mean** is easy to take for something else. A tag's `count` is the READABLE posts carrying it. A type hub's is the readable posts of that stored type, and "All posts"' is EVERY readable post, one of a retired type included: "the site total" of the design, as this viewer reads the site, and not what a block with no filter lists (it lists no post of a retired type). A page's `listed_count` is the readable posts its blocks list, past a block's own `limit` (a block showing 5 of 112 counts 112). None of the three is a pill's arithmetic: a pill counts what ITS target brings in to THIS page beyond the posts drawn. And a post's `type` need not have a hub: `types` holds only what a drawn lists line points at, so no script may assume `type-<post.type>` is a node.

**`status` and `changes` are `Publishing::State`'s**, together one of its five states: `status` is "draft", "scheduled" or "live", and `changes` (nil, "unpublished", "scheduled") tells apart the two further states of a live record, which a boolean could not. `change_at` is the time a scheduled change comes into force, in that state alone. The builder reads the pending copies ONCE for all the nodes of a kind (`Publishing::Version.pending(now)`, `Publishing.pending`'s own scope: when a copy comes into force, never its content) and hands each record's time to `Publishing::State.of(record, now:, pending_at:)`, which then asks the database nothing for `state` and `status` (`docs/reference/publishing.md`). What the caller owes for that: the same `now` in both, and only `state` and `status` asked, never `actions`, which would cost a statement a node. No words leave a query: the controller adds `change_on`, the day as CS Studio writes a short date (`I18n.l` with `admin.short`), to `nodes`, `pages` and the list's `rows` (`with_dates`), and the script formats no date (`test/queries/post_graph/global_test.rb`: "for every combination of the time, a pending copy and the mark, a node's status, changes and change_at are what Publishing::State answers by itself"; `test/integration/admin/mind_graph_data_test.rb`: "a scheduled change's day is in the data as CS Studio's short date, and a node without one has no such key").

**The site, loaded once.** Every view and both list reads start with `PostGraph::Site.load(actor:)`: the readable posts' rows (`[id, content_type, recency, updated_at]`), their tag links, the readable pages (in tree order), and from those, with no further statement, `PostGraph::Tree` and `PostGraph::Listings`. **`PostGraph::Tree`** is the page tree as far as THIS viewer can read it, from `(id, parent_id, position)` rows: closure_tree would ask per page. *What is not among the rows does not exist there*: a page whose parent the viewer cannot read has no parent (so no tree line is drawn to it and its parent's id is never handed out: `Tree#parent`, never the record's `parent_id`), stands at depth 1, is not Home and comes after the tree proper in `order`. Siblings are in the tree's one order, `TreeOrder` (`app/values/tree_order.rb`: by position, a page without one last, then by id), which the public branch, the Pages screen's tree and the New page dialog ask too; that key was written four times, the fourth here, until this build's last task made it one, with a guard that it is written nowhere else (`test/values/tree_order_test.rb`). `order` is a site-wide rank: in a local view it is a sort key, not an index.

**What a page lists** (`PostGraph::Listings`). **A lists line is made from the block, never from the posts.** For each list block of the readable pages (a block whose type has the `lists_posts` capability, in a section or not: its owner is the page), its typed settings say the target: the tag when it names one (found by name whatever the case, as the listing finds it; a name no tag has makes no line, as it lists nothing), else the post type, else the one "All posts" node. One line per distinct target of a page; one "All posts" node however many pages list everything. So a type node joins only the pages that list it, never a post of its type, and no lists line joins a page to a post anywhere. **Which posts a page lists** is worked out in Ruby from the site's rows by asking `BlockTypes::PostList.matches?(settings, content_type:, tag_names:)`, the per-post criteria with no record, which `lists?` is too: the listing, a post's address and the graph cannot come to disagree, and `test/queries/post_graph/listings_test.rb` holds the parity ("the posts a page lists are the listing's own, for every shape of settings"). **"A page's first list block" is the first as a person reads the page**: `Listings.blocks` returns each page's list blocks in `BlockOutline#reading_order` (the sections loaded in the same statement, for their place alone), so a block dragged above an older one is the first; as first built it was the first by id, and the hub line and the "Open in Posts" filter came from whichever block had been made first. `target_of(page, post)` is the target of the first block that lists the post, so a post two blocks list is counted once, with the upper one. **It knows nothing of rights**: it is handed the pages and posts the viewer reads, so "listed", a page's `listed_count` and "Not listed on any page" are **true for the viewer, not for the site**. Someone who reads part of the tree is told a post is unlisted when a page they cannot read lists it; saying otherwise would disclose that page (`test/queries/post_graph/rows_test.rb`: "unlisted is true for the viewer: a post only a page they cannot read lists is a row, and a trashed post is none"). Two tags whose names differ only in case (an old row, a write around the model) are one name to the listing, so they are one target here, the tag of the lower id, and the posts of both are listed; the public page and a post's address go through `Publishing::Listing`, which takes one tag per name, so the application's two listings disagree about such a post, an older matter that is on the roadmap. **Its work in Ruby is bounded by counts a test holds, because no statement shows it.** As first built every page's posts were sorted for that page and the criteria were asked once per distinct filter AND post: at 204 pages and 2,005 posts the graph's data took 0.58 s where every page lists everything and 0.89 s where every page has a filter of its own, on every read that loads the site. Now the posts are put in order once, the criteria are asked once per distinct filter and distinct KIND of post (its stored type and which of the named tags it carries: all the criteria read), and pages whose blocks say the same share one frozen list: 0.15 s and 0.39 s ("the criteria are asked once for each distinct filter and each distinct kind of post, however many posts and pages there are", "pages whose list blocks say the same share one list of posts, the same object, and it is the list each would have had alone").

**Two steps: `plan`, then `build`.** `Builder#plan` says WHICH LINES the response holds, from what the caller has loaded, and asks the database nothing; `Builder#build` makes the nodes of a plan. The callers decide which posts, pages and lists lines; the builder decides nothing about access, and the rules that keep the graph from disclosing anything hold there because of what it is given: a line is made only between two nodes of the response (a tree line needs both pages drawn and the parent readable), and a tag's `count` is the readable number. The split exists for the next paragraph: the lines can be counted before a node is built.

**The cap, the cut and the cost.** The cap is `PostGraph::CAP`, 2,000 posts; it is not a setting, and raising it means measuring again. **Every readable page is drawn, always**: pages are few and are the frame the rest hangs on, and a tree with pages missing is not a tree. The two queries cut their posts differently. **Global** ranks every readable post by its connections (related-post links in either direction, plus one for each of its tags that at least two readable posts carry or that a readable page lists) *among all readable posts, before the cut*, keeps the top 2,000 with a count above zero, and breaks ties by newest `updated_at`, then id, so two requests cut the same; tags and lines are then derived from the kept posts only. **A lists line is not a post's connection**: a page points at a tag, a type or "All posts", never at a post, so a post only a page lists is still isolated, and a drawn post says whether a page lists it (`listed`). **Local** walks outward from the focus one level per depth, stepping only onto readable posts and pages (so an unreadable or trashed one is neither a node nor a bridge to what lies behind it); over the cap it keeps the focus, then nearer posts before farther ones, and among equals those with more connections *inside the neighbourhood*. A tag on thousands of posts is why a local view needs the cap at all. Over the cap the screen says what it kept, in a sentence per view because the two cut differently: "Showing the 2,000 best-connected of 5,791 posts." (`script.capped`) and "Showing the 2,000 nearest of 2,695 posts around this one." (`script.capped_local`, chosen when the response has a `focus`; never said around a page, where `shown` and `total` are the posts drawn and always equal); the list's last line, the whole site's search, is how a post the view doesn't draw is reached. Every figure in those sentences and in the isolated one is grouped for the messages' language (`Intl.NumberFormat` on their `@locale`): the count goes into its sentence as `%{figure}`, and `count` only chooses the plural form. One consequence of ranking before the cut: a kept post whose only link is a tag whose other posts were cut is drawn with no line and a `degree` of 0. **The cost is a fixed number of statements whatever the size, and each figure is a CEILING**, since Rails sends no statement for an empty `IN ()`. The whole graph: at most thirteen (the site's five: the readable posts, their tag links, the readable pages, the list blocks with the sections that place them, the tags the blocks name; the relations; the builder's seven: the tags' names, the pages' addresses, open notes and pending copies, the posts' open notes, pending copies and rows). What makes it vary: a site where no list block names a tag does not ask for the tags blocks name (twelve), one with no page or no post drawn does not ask for that kind's notes, and a viewer who is not an owner costs one more, constant, their grants, read once a request. The answer that says where to open instead (below): six, no node built. A local view: at most the thirteen, plus three per depth level around a post or a tag (the relations touching the frontier, the frontier posts' tags, the frontier tags' posts); **around a page the walk asks nothing** (it is the site's rows), so nine at depth 1, where no post is drawn, and thirteen at depth 2, and seven for a page that lists nothing on a site with no list block. Each of the list's two reads: at most eight for a pill's rows (the site's five and the nodes' three) and nine for the unlisted posts (one more: the relations that touch its rows, for what each row says of its post's connections), five with no row. The search: six. Never one per page, per block or per post: each has a `capture_sql` test at two sizes far apart (`test/queries/post_graph/global_test.rb`: "the statement count is thirteen for a small site and for one ten times its size in pages, blocks, posts, tags, notes and pending copies"; `local_test.rb`: "a page's local view costs nine statements at depth 1 and thirteen at depth 2, around two children and one sibling and around twelve children with their children and eight siblings"; `rows_test.rb`: "the unlisted read costs nine statements and a pill's eight, on a small site and on one with thirty more pages, their blocks and sections, and thirty more posts"; `search_test.rb`: "it costs six statements whatever the number of pages, posts, tags and list blocks, and loads no page and no post whole"). **A statement test must vary what could cost a statement.** The local view's first one varied only posts, with no child and no grandchild, so a statement per child would have gone unseen; and the first posts-scope assertions were vacuous in the same way (next paragraph). A new read of pages or posts for the graph goes into `PostGraph::Site`, not into a loop. Measured on 2026-10-13 at 204 pages and 2,005 posts (the pages spec's "As built" has the whole table and how each figure was taken): the whole graph's data 0.14 s and 844 kB (55 kB gzipped) for 2,202 nodes and 6,204 lines, of which the statements are about a tenth; where to open instead 0.05 s; a local view, a search and each list read under 0.06 s.

**Rights and the trash, at every read.** A page is in the graph only through `AccessControl.scope(actor, "pages.read", Page.all)` and a post only through the posts' scope, which also leave the trash out; what is outside is not a node, not in a count, not a search row, not a list row, and at no line's end. A tree line needs both pages readable, so a page whose parent is outside the scope (or in the trash) is drawn without one. A tag that only an unreadable page lists is no node. The legend names only the kinds and the post types the viewer can read something of (below). Both views, the search and both list reads are in `test/integration/trash_visibility_test.rb`, for a trashed page as for a trashed post. **Each of these needs a test that can fail.** As first built, it was missed twice: nothing failed when `PostGraph::Site` stopped asking the posts scope for rights, and the local view's posts-scope assertion held nothing about a pill's count, a hub's count or a page's `listed_count`, because the viewer it used could read every post. The tests that hold it now give the viewer a real partial scope and search the whole body for what must not be there (`test/queries/post_graph/local_test.rb`: "around a page a viewer who reads only some of the posts it lists is shown and counted those alone: the nodes, each pill, each hub's count and the page's listed count"; `test/queries/post_graph/global_test.rb`: "a post outside the viewer's posts scope is no node, in no count and at no line's end, and a tag only such posts carry is no node"; `test/integration/admin/mind_graph_data_test.rb`: "around a page, behind its pill and among the unlisted, a viewer is shown and counted only the posts they read").

**The tag rule.** A tag is derived only from readable posts and pages, and its `count` is the number of *readable* posts carrying it: never the tag's true total, which would disclose posts the viewer can't see, and not the smaller number drawn under a cap, which would understate it. A tag is a node when at least two readable posts carry it (`PostGraph::MIN_TAGGED_POSTS`) *and* it has enough lines in this response to say something: two in the whole graph, where a tag with one drawn post connects nothing, and one in a local view, where a post's own tags are its neighbours. Two exceptions: **a tag a drawn page's list block points at is a node whatever its count** (the lists line needs its end; the viewer can read the page, and its block names the tag), also with one post or none, and the focused tag of a local view is always a node. A listed tag's `degree` counts its lists lines. No line points at a node that isn't in the response.

**A page's local view.** Depth 1: its parent, its children, and the tags or post type its blocks list; no post (`nodes: []`, `total: 0`, so "no connections" must look at pages too). Depth 2 adds one more step along the tree (the grandparent, the grandchildren and the siblings, each with its tree line) and the posts it lists: the `PostGraph::LISTED_IN_VIEW` (20) newest OF THE PAGE, not of each target (by recency: when published, a draft when last changed). Each is joined by a faint `brings` line to the hub that brings it in (the type or "All posts" of the first block that lists it), NOT to the page; a post a TAG brings in has its "Shares a tag" line to that tag, which is its hub, and no other (two lines between the same two nodes would be one drawn twice). **"+N more" is one pill per target**: `more: [{ id: "more-<target>", count, page, target }]` (absent, not empty, when there is none), one for each target that brings in more than the view draws, each counting only what its own target brings in and hanging off it by a `brings` line; the pills' counts add up to what the twenty left out, a target that brings in nothing more has no pill, and a pill can hang off a hub none of whose posts is among the twenty. A drawn post also has its `tag` line to every listed tag it carries and its `related` line to any other post among the twenty; what it does not show is a tag the page does not list or a related post outside the twenty (the view is the page's). A neighbour page's lists line is drawn only to a target the focus itself reaches, so its card can say "lists 112 posts" with no line on screen. From a post or a tag the walk also steps along a lists line: the pages that list a tag are its depth 1. The answer also says what the site holds (`site:`, from the site's rows with no statement more: the pages and posts of the viewer's scope and the tags they can be shown), for the summary of the view a large site opens in (`test/queries/post_graph/local_test.rb`: "depth 2 around a page draws the twenty newest posts it lists, each joined to the hub that brings it in, and counts the rest behind more", "a page with two list blocks has a pill per target, each on its own hub or tag with its own count, and a target that brings in nothing more has none").

**The list's two reads** (`PostGraph::Rows`). `unlisted`: the readable posts no readable page lists. `listed(page, target)`: the posts one target of that page brings in beyond the twenty drawn, the rows behind that pill, each with `via` (the target), and the controller adds `posts_url`, the Posts screen filtered by the type and the tag of the block that points at the target (the tag named AS ITS ROW NAMES IT: a block finds its tag whatever the case and the Posts screen's filter does not, so a block spelled "News" for the tag "news" would have opened an empty list). Newest first, the first `PostGraph::ROW_LIMIT` (200) with the whole `total` (no paging: past 200 the answer only says `total` is more than its rows). A row is a post's node exactly as the graph's data has it, with no line counted (`degree`, `related_count` and `tag_count` 0), so the page adds a row that is not drawn to the canvas without another request. Each is its own read and neither a filter over the drawn nodes: a post no page lists is very likely a post with no connection, which the whole graph does not draw.

**Search.** `PostGraph::Search`, which the list's last line asks ("The find field and the last line", below), answers up to ten of each, in this order: readable PAGES whose title matches in any language (`title_matching_any_locale`), in tree order (a tree is read by its order, not by a score; the first ten of the tree), each with its address in the editing language, else the default's (two pages may share a title, and the path tells them apart); readable posts by title, each with its type's `colour_index` for its dot; and tags by name (`Tag.name_containing`, those that start with the text first), a tag when a readable post carries it OR a readable page's list block names it, the two ways `Focus` finds a tag, so a result never leads to a 404. Every row carries the typed `id` and `record_id`, and the controller adds each row's local-view URL from the row's `kind` and `record_id`. A page is loaded with its id and title only and a post with its id, title and type (`test/queries/post_graph/search_test.rb` reads that from the statements). Three groups, not the list's four: a row opens its local view, and a post type has none. It reads the tree triple of every readable page and every list block of every readable page (to know which tags a page names) on each request: 7 to 23 ms at 204 pages (by the text and the shape of the site), which is on the roadmap as the first thing to cut if the search is ever slow.

**The page holds no data.** `show.html.erb` is a frame: in the tree column the list's head, the find field and the list's empty region with its hidden last line ("The layout of the screen", below), and in `<main>` the heading, the focus bar of a local view (or the hidden bar of the view a large site opens in), the legend, an empty canvas with the zoom control and the hidden "Release N pinned", the notice, and the card's and the tooltip's containers; it opens no dialog of its own. The script fetches `data-url`. The reason is size: at the cap the JSON is the better part of a megabyte, and inline it would sit in every load of the page and in Turbo's page cache. What the page does ask the database is small and independent of the site's size: the focus, whether this viewer can read a page at all, and the distinct content types of their readable posts, for the legend and for "No pages or posts yet." (said for *this* viewer: one who can read neither sees it whatever the site holds). The element carries the reads' addresses and the URL templates, all built by the route helpers so that no path is written in JavaScript: `data-url`; `data-open-url` (a page's local view's data, with `__ID__`) and `data-everything-url` (the data with `everything=1`); `data-unlisted-url` (only for a viewer who reads a page: for one who reads none every post is one no page lists, and the filter would say nothing) and `data-listed-url` (with `__ID__` and `__TARGET__`); the card's templates for a post's, a tag's and a page's focus, a tag's posts, and a post's and a page's open notes as JSON (each built for id 0 with that segment swapped, because the route takes digits only); the whole site's search is the last line's own `data-url`, in the column. It also carries `data-button-classes` and `data-dot-classes`, which are `Admin::Ui::ButtonComponent`'s small primary, secondary and tertiary (`ButtonComponent.classes`) and `Admin::Ui::ContentTypeDotComponent`'s own class strings (the card's actions, the two Retry buttons, tertiary, and a type's dot in the card and the list are built in the script, and are handed the shared classes instead of holding copies; "Show everything" is the component itself, tertiary and small), `data-tree-classes` (`Admin::Ui::TreeItemComponent::CLASSES`, the classes of the list's rows, "The list" below), `data-tree-open`, `data-tree-open-path` and `data-tree-locale` (the open set's first folds, its cookie's path and the tree's language), the parts template (above), `data-type-labels`, and the script's strings as `data-messages` (every key written out in the view, because `test/lib/script_messages_test.rb` reads the literals; the same test reads a backticked `word.word` in a script's COMMENT as a message key, so a comment says "the worker's `add`" and not the dotted form). **The page takes no `everything` parameter**: only `data` does, so no address a person can keep or reload asks for everything (`test/integration/admin/mind_graph_test.rb`: "everything is no parameter of the page: the data url is the same with it"). **The scripts' class names are built only because the Tailwind build is told to read them**: `app/assets/tailwind/application.css` is `source(none)` plus explicit `@source` lines, and `@source "../../javascript/mind_graph"` is one of them. Without it nothing failed and `w-fit`, used nowhere else, was simply not in the stylesheet: each notice line was as wide as the longest one and each link of the card as wide as the card, so a click beside the text navigated. A new directory of scripts that writes class names needs its own line, and `test/lib/tailwind_sources_test.rb` fails until it has one.

**The layout of the screen.** The list stands on the left, in the shell's tree column ("The column hosts two screens", above: the subject `mind_graph`, with the Pages tree's width, divider, fold and drawer), and the screen's own box is one column at every width: a local view's focus bar (or the opened view's bar), the legend's row and the canvas, one under the other. The column holds, in this order, the head (`h2` "Pages, posts and tags", `show.column_heading`, and `Admin::Ui::TreeHideComponent.new(subject: "mind_graph")`), the find field (`Admin::Ui::TreeFindComponent.new(controls: "mind-graph-tree", noun: :nodes)`) and the list's region, `div[data-mind-graph-list][role=region].admin-shell__tree-region`, a direct child of `#tree-column` so that it fills the column (`.admin-shell__tree--fill`), named by `show.list_label`; `node_list.js` fills the region with "Not listed on any page" and the tree, and the region ends with the server's last line. The column is drawn for a full answer only, and only when there is a graph (`turbo_frame_request?`, as Pages' `_tree` checks): a frame navigation keeps the column, and a frame answer draws none (`test/integration/admin/mind_graph_test.rb`: "the global view has no focus bar, and a frame answer draws no column"). From 1024 pixels of width (`lg:`) the heading and the box together are `100vh` less `<main>`'s padding and the top bar where there is one (`--admin-top-offset`), so the page does not scroll and the canvas takes what is left. Below that width the box is `70vh` high, at least 480px, and the page scrolls, and the column is the tree drawer, opened by the top bar's "List ▾" as Pages' is by "Pages ▾": a keyboard on a narrow window reaches the nodes through the drawer (`test/system/admin/tree_column_test.rb`: "below 1024px the list is the tree drawer, opened by the top bar's List button"). **The Tab order follows the document**, which is the order on screen: the sidebar, the column (its hide button, the find field, "Not listed on any page", the tree, which is one stop, and the last line), then `<main>`: "Show everything" (inside its bar, hidden until a large site opens around Home) or a local view's bar's links, the legend's toggles, the zoom buttons, the canvas and the card; the legend's marks and status looks hold no stop. Move a part in the markup and the Tab order moves with it (`test/integration/admin/mind_graph_test.rb`: "the column holds the list's head, its find field and its region; the box holds the legend and the canvas; nothing is pinned", "the list stands in the column, before the canvas, and starts empty", "the tab order is the find field, the filter, the zoom buttons, then the canvas", "in a local view the tab order is the find field, the bar's links, the filter, the zoom buttons, the canvas"; `test/system/admin/mind_graph_list_test.rb`: "the Tab order is the find field, the list, then the legend, the zoom and the canvas").

**The worker, and why d3-force is imported by URL.** The layout runs in a module worker (`mind_graph/layout_worker.js`), so zoom, pan and clicks stay smooth while a graph of two thousand nodes is still settling; the page only draws. The worker is started from its own digest asset URL (`data-worker-url`) and is handed the vendored d3-force file's asset URL (`data-d3-url`) with its first message, which it imports dynamically: **an import map does not apply inside a worker**, so `import "d3-force"` can't resolve there, and the file is one self-contained bundle, so the URL is all it needs. Nothing else loads d3-force. Its pin in `config/importmap.rb` is `preload: false` and stays only so `bin/importmap audit` and `outdated` still see the package; `pin_all_from "app/javascript/mind_graph"` is `preload: false` too, and the thin controllers reach `mind_graph/app`, and `mind_graph/swatches` with `mind_graph/theme`, through a dynamic `import()`, so no other CS Studio screen fetches any of it (the swatches controller is loaded on every CS Studio page, as every `controllers/admin/*` file is, and imports the graph's modules only when a swatch is on the page; a static import there would preload them everywhere, and the test below would fail). (Before this, d3-force was `preload: "admin"` and every CS Studio page evaluated it.) `test/integration/public_javascript_test.rb` keeps both off the public pages and out of the other screens' preloads. There is no second code path that lays the graph out in the page: if the worker can't start, the box says "The graph can't be drawn in this browser.", with no Retry (a browser that can't run it once can't run it twice), nothing is drawn, and the list in the tree column still gives every node, and still follows the legend, which is applied to the list, the counts and the canvas's name whenever there is data, a layout or not. A row then opens the node's card in the canvas's top-right corner, without Pin (there is no layout to hold anything in), and the card's links lead on.

**The worker's messages** (documented at the top of `layout_worker.js`). In: `start` (the d3 URL, nodes as `{ id, radius, anchor }`, lines as `{ source, target, kind }`, `reducedMotion`, and `springs` and `ease`), `drag` (a node held under the pointer), `drop` (the pointer let go; the node stays), `pin` (hold a node where it is), `unpin` (let these ids go) and `add` (one more node, placed where the page says, with its lines: a post added from the list). Out: `tick` with one `Float32Array` of x/y pairs covering every node given to `start` and after them every node an `add` brought, transferred and not copied; `settled`; `error`. Three rules here each answer a bug. **Messages are handled strictly in order**, each after the one before it has finished: `start` awaits the import of d3-force, and a message sent right behind it would run before the simulation existed (it threw, or a drag was dropped). **`settled` carries `after`**, the number of messages the worker had taken up when the layout cooled down, and `layout_client.js` passes it on only when that equals the number it has sent: a small graph cools down inside the first slice, before a message sent right behind `start` is handled, and the page would fit the view and call itself settled on a layout it had already asked to change. And **a `drag` wakes a cooled-down graph**: `alphaTarget` only pulls `alpha` up by ticking, and the loop does not tick below `alphaMin`. A message that moves nothing (a `pin`, a `drag` of a node that isn't there) is still answered with a `settled` when the layout is at rest, because the page waits for one that counts it. **There is no `filter` message.** The worker had one for the old radios, which laid the shown nodes out again; the legend's toggles are a filter on what the PAGE draws and send the worker nothing (below), the message was dead from then on, and it was removed with its code, with a guard that holds its absence from the page, the client and the worker alike (`test/lib/mind_graph_guards_test.rb`: "no script of the mind graph knows a filter message for the layout: not the page, not the client, not the worker"): whoever brings a second layout back for a toggle has to take that test away first, and read why. **A tick posted before the worker took an `add` is two numbers short** of what the page holds; the page completes it with the places it has (`graph_data.js`, `placedFor`), so an added node always has a position and no `NaN` reaches the renderer, the card or the camera. An added line one of whose ends the layout does not hold is not handed to the forces: d3 looks a line's ends up among the nodes it lays out and throws for one it does not find, and the worker's error would take the whole graph down for one line.

**Springs, the anchor and the ease are values the page hands the worker** (`mind_graph/layout_parameters.js`, sent with every `start` by `layout_client.js`): `SPRINGS`, a line's spring by its kind (`{ tree: { distance: 40, strength: 0.9 } }`: a tree line is short and stiff, so a subtree stays together; a kind that is not named has d3's own, a rest length of 90 and a strength that falls with the number of lines at its ends), and `EASE` (`{ keep: 0.85, within: 0.5 }`). They are the pages spec's starting values and were not tuned: at the measurement the tree held together as one cluster beside the posts and no page was drawn into a tag's cluster, and how tight that cluster should be is the designer's to say. They are values, not constants in the worker, so that a test can read them and LOWER them: a test of the worker gives it springs and an ease of its own instead of waiting out the real ones ("In tests"). **One node rests at the centre** (`graph_data.js`, `anchorOf`): **Home in the whole graph, the focus in a local view**, where Home, if it is drawn, is a page like any other. The worker holds it at 0, 0 (`fx` / `fy`). It is not a pin: `state.pinned` does not hold it, nothing shows it as pinned and nothing the page forgets or releases can let it go. Dragged or pinned it is held where the person put it, like any node, and `unpin` gives it back to the centre, not to the forces. The design holds Home "while the layout runs", speaking of the whole graph; the one node at the centre is held in every view, so around any other page the focus does not hang off-centre, and a person who asks for the graph around something expects it in the middle. That holds for a post's and a tag's local view too. **Back to the centre is eased**: a released anchor is held a little nearer with every tick, so it travels like every other released node and its subtree is not yanked after it; with reduced motion, and at `start`, it is simply there. The ease starts from where the node is HELD (`fx`), not from where it is: `x` follows `fx` only with a tick, and an anchor dropped and released before the next tick would be taken for a node that never left, and jump. No pinning by depth and no rings by level: they would fight the post clusters (`test/system/admin/mind_graph_drawing_test.rb`: "pages and what they list are drawn, and Home rests at the centre without being pinned"; `test/system/admin/mind_graph_loading_test.rb`: "in a local view the focus rests at the centre and Home does not"; `test/system/admin/mind_graph_data_modules_test.rb`: "graph data: the anchor is Home in the whole graph and the focus in a local view, of whatever kind"; `test/system/admin/mind_graph_layout_modules_test.rb`: "layout parameters: the tree's spring and the anchor's ease are the configured values").

**The pacing** is the worker's, and is what makes the screen feel alive or dead. The simulation's own timer is stopped and the worker steps it by hand at one of three paces. *Frame pace*: one tick, one `tick` message, then 16 ms — a small graph's first layout (up to `SMALL_GRAPH`, 300 nodes; 300 ticks, about five seconds at the 18 ms a tick measured in 2026-10-08 and 6.2 s at the 21.5 ms measured on 2026-10-13, so the figure is the browser's timer more than the code's), a drag, and the settle after a drop, an added post or an unpin, so the neighbours visibly follow a held node and the graph eases to rest. *Fast*: as many ticks as fit in 12 ms, one message, and a zero timeout so a message that arrived meanwhile is handled before the next slice — a larger graph's first layout, until about 600 ms of ticks are left (`FINAL_STRETCH_MS`), which are then shown at frame pace. *Silent*, with reduced motion: no message until the layout is at rest, then one; a dragged node moves alone (`drag` posts the positions last shown with that one node moved, and ticks nothing) and `drop` shows the result in one step. Before this the worker always ran fast, so a small graph was at rest in its first message and nothing was ever seen to move — "it was more alive before" was the owner's first remark. Every run ends: `alpha` decays below `alphaMin`; only a held node keeps the layout warm, and `drop` takes that back. **The page tells the worker about reduced motion with `start`** (a worker has no reliable `matchMedia`), once per load. At the cap frame pace holds. A toggle of the legend starts no layout at all (below), so nothing eases after one. The system tests wait a first layout out, five to six seconds each. An error in the worker is posted to the page and reported with `console.error`, on purpose: the system tests fail on any console error, so a worker that breaks is never a graph that silently stays empty.

**The modules and what each owns.** `controllers/admin/mind_graph/graph_controller.js` is deliberately thin, because every `controllers/admin/*` file is loaded on every CS Studio page: it imports `mind_graph/app`, mounts it, destroys it on `disconnect()` and resets the snapshot's state, the tree column's list among it (below). A `connect()` may be followed by `disconnect()` before the import has arrived, so a token (`this.live`) says whether the connection is still current and a late import mounts nothing. A failed `import()` is left unhandled so it is loud. `controllers/admin/mind_graph/swatches_controller.js` (`admin--mind-graph--swatches`) is the second, as thin: it sits on the legend, paints its swatches (the five status looks among them) on connect and on a theme change, and keeps them apart from what the graph's script writes and `reset()` takes back, so a restored page shows them drawn before its data has come. In `app/javascript/mind_graph/`, each file's header is its contract:
- `app.js`: `mount(element)` builds the parts below and wires them: the list's region and its last line, found in the tree column (`#tree-column [data-mind-graph-list]` and `[data-mind-graph-search-site]`; a missing one throws), the data request (aborted on leaving, timed out after 15 seconds into the same message as a failure, with Retry), the worker, the legend's filter, a post added from the list, the notice's sentences, the canvas's name, the toggles' counts, the view a large site opens in with "Show everything", the state said on the element and the test API (`element.mindGraph`). It handles no key of the find field, which is the find's. `destroy()` stops all of it (the request, the worker, the frame, the observers, the whole site's search), so nothing runs or draws after the screen is left.
- `state.js`: the record every part reads and the renderer draws from, and its pure operations: `takeData` (the data's nodes, lines, sizes and each label measured in its plain and its selected font), `addNode` (a post added from a row, with its hub's line), `resizePill`, `selectIndex` (the width swap and the neighbours by the lines shown), `showOnly`, `clearData`, `screenOf`, `hitAt` (nothing until the layout has placed the nodes) and `labelFor`. It reads no element and chooses no word: the names come in as data.
- `frame.js`: one redraw per animation frame and only when something asked for one: the labels, the hovered node's label, the draw, the card placed in the frame that draws its node, `data-zoom`, `data-labelled` and the zoom readout; and the canvas's size.
- `gestures.js`: what `input.js`'s callbacks do to the graph: press, drag (which pins), drop, click, double click, Escape, Enter, pan and zoom; the hover by its two sources (the pointer's wins over the list row's) and the tooltip.
- `selection.js`: the selection and its card (placed beside the node, measured only when filled or changed, its own Escape and Enter), a row's reveal, Close giving the focus back, and the pins with "Release N pinned".
- `list_reads.js`: the list's groups or a read's rows in their place, the two reads ("Not listed on any page" and a pill's "+N more") with their busy state, failure, Try again and the way back.
- `site_search.js`: the list's last line and the whole site's search, `attachSiteSearch(lastLine, { list, text, findField })`, which answers `stop()` ("The find field and the last line", below).
- `notice.js`: the notice's lines, a line that says what it said left where it is, and the announced summary.
- `ids.js`: `nodeId` and `recordId`, the one place an id is taken apart.
- `graph_data.js`: the payload as the page holds it, plain functions with no canvas and no element: `nodesOf` (nodes of five kinds), `anchorOf`, the label order (`rank`, `orderOf`) and the set named at any zoom (`freeOf`), `visible` and `counts` (the legend's filter), `countAdded` (the figures after a post is added) and `placedFor`.
- `marks.js`: every mark and its size, and the looks table (below).
- `layout_worker.js`, `layout_client.js`, `layout_parameters.js`: the layout, the page's side of it and its values; one worker per graph, terminated by `stop()`.
- `camera.js`: `{ k, tx, ty }` and pure functions: zoom at a point, pan, bounds, fit. Zoom is limited to 0.05 to 4, and a fit never zooms in past 3 (`MAX_FIT_ZOOM`) and leaves a tenth of the canvas empty on each side (`FIT_MARGIN` 0.1).
- `hit_test.js`: the node under a point (below).
- `labels.js`: which labels are shown (below).
- `renderer.js`: draws one frame: lines, nodes (each with its pin mark and its notes badge), labels, rings; the five line styles; and the states: hovered, selected, pinned.
- `theme.js`: the colours, fonts and the card's radius, read from the `--cs-*` custom properties and read again when `<html data-theme>` or the system scheme changes. The script holds no colour value (`test/lib/admin_design_rules_test.rb`).
- `summary.js`: the canvas's name, a pure function (below).
- `swatches.js`: the legend's swatches, its status looks among them, and a row's mark, drawn by the renderer (below).
- `input.js`: the canvas's pointer, wheel and key events as callbacks, a double click among them. It knows nothing of the graph. A pointer ends on `pointerup` or `pointercancel`, and a touch or a pen on `lostpointercapture` too: a touch can lose both of the first two, and its entry left in the table would turn every later touch into a pinch with a ghost. A mouse is left out of that, because its `pointerup` always comes and a capture dropped between the press and the first move would otherwise end a drag that is still held. After a pinch, a new finger arriving while one remains restarts the pinch.
- `card.js`, `card_notes.js`, `node_list.js`, `search_results.js`: the selection card's content and its notes, the list in the tree column (with `showResults` and `closeResults`), and the whole site's results, which `search_results.js`'s `renderResults` draws for the list. All write text with `textContent` and URLs with `href`, never markup: a title, a path and a tag's name are the editor's text, whatever they contain.
- `card_placement.js`: where the card stands: pure numbers, so it is tested without a canvas.

**The camera.** The first view fits everything: the camera follows the layout from its first positions until it has settled, and that fit is final: the graph fills about 80% of the canvas on the axis that binds (a tenth of it empty on each side, `FIT_MARGIN` 0.1), a small graph may be zoomed in up to 3 (`MAX_FIT_ZOOM`), and a box with no extent is centred at that cap; the nodes' centres are the box, so a mark at the edge reaches a few pixels into the margin. "Fit all" is the same fit. From then on — or from the user's first zoom, pan or drag of a node, if that comes sooner — it moves only on the user's input or "Fit all"; a toggle of the legend, a resize and a later reheat of the layout leave it where it is. Nodes are not clamped to the box; the box is a window. The wheel and a trackpad's pinch (a wheel event with `ctrlKey`) zoom towards the pointer, a two-finger touch pinch zooms around the point between the fingers (tested with synthetic pointer events only), a drag on empty space pans, a drag on a node moves that node and pins it (below). The wheel always zooms: no modifier key and no hint, since the page itself does not scroll. With the canvas focused, the arrow keys pan and `+` / `-` zoom. A press released within five pixels of where it began is a click, whatever it was on. A row of the list centres the camera on its node, at the zoom it has. The zoom control — −, the zoom as a percentage, +, "Fit", each named in full by its `aria-label` and tooltip — comes before the canvas in the document, because Tab follows the document, and is positioned over its bottom-right corner, with "Release N pinned" beside it while a node is pinned. The group takes the button's radius (`rounded-button`); the zoom buttons are 28px (`h-7`) in the small type (`text-small`), and the percentage between them is a count, `text-count tabular-nums`, so its digits are of one width and the readout does not shift as the zoom changes (`test/system/admin/mind_graph_drawing_test.rb`: "the zoom readout is a figure in the sans with even digits, beside the zoom buttons").

**The marks** (`marks.js`, where every value below is written once, with its colours read from the theme's tokens: `--cs-graph-*`). Everything is in SCREEN pixels, the same at every zoom, and each kind is a shape of its own, so none is told by colour alone. A **post** is a dot filled with its type's hue (colour slots 1 to 6); a **ring type** (slots 7 to 12, and 13 on cycle) is the same mark, smaller (4/7 of the radius, rounded to half a pixel), inside a 1.5px ring of the hue; a post of a type that is **not registered** is a ring type in `meta` *with a diagonal stroke through it* (before the first redesign it was the same shape as a 7 to 12 ring and differed by colour alone). A **page** is a small card, 14 by 10 with the card's corner radius (`--cs-radius`, read through the theme), **Home** 18 by 13, in neutral ink: the hues mean post types. A **post type** a page lists is a hub: a 12px circle in the type's hue with a surface ring inside it, so it is not one more post; **"All posts"** is the same, filled in `dim`; a ring type's hub is a ring in the hue round a filled centre; a **retired** type's is a DASHED ring in `dim` round a small `dim` centre, so it differs from "All posts" by fill, not by shade, and from a ring type's hub by its dashes. A **tag** is a square edged in the structure hue (`tag`, `--cs-graph-tag`, the sidebar's structure family), so it is told from a post by its shape and from a page by its hue. **"+N more"** is a pill with its words inside it (the count's sans, `500 11px`, the figure grouped), 16 high, as wide as its words need with 8px each side and never under 60. A post's and a tag's **size** follows `degree` in four steps (a post's radius 7, 10, 13 and 16, up to 2, 5 and 12 connections and beyond; a tag's half side 4.5, 6, 8.25 and 10.5: `STEPS`; a post's were a step smaller until the owner compared the screen with the design review's mock), because small differences in size can't be read; a page's, a hub's and the pill's size is fixed. **A mark's size is its outer size**: every outline is traced half its width INSIDE the mark's edge, so an outlined mark is exactly as large as a filled one and has the size the design gives (a draft page is 14 by 10, a ring type's hub 12 across, the pill 60 by 16 with its border in it). As first built strokes were centred on the edge and every outlined mark was 1.5px larger than its filled twin. Two marks are older than that rule and were left as they were: a live filled post's own outline in its hue, and a tag's square.

**The five looks are ONE table**, `LOOKS`, with `lookOf(node)` choosing the row from the data's `status` and `changes`; a page, a filled post and a ring type's inner mark all draw their row through one function, so the five are the same on every mark. A value of the table is a theme KEY, never a colour:

| State | Fill | Outline | Dashed | Centre dot | Corner dot |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `live` | own | none | | | |
| `draft` | `surface` | `edge`, 1.5px | yes (3 / 2.5) | | |
| `scheduled` | `surface` | `dim`, 1.5px | no | `warn`, 4px across | |
| `unpublished` (live, `changes: "unpublished"`) | own | none | | | `dim`, 5px across on a 1.5px surface ring |
| `change_scheduled` (live, `changes: "scheduled"`) | own | none | | | `warn`, the same |

"Own" is `page` for a page and the type's hue for a post (`meta` for a retired type's). A draft's dashed outline is `edge`, the outlined Draft chip's own edge (`--cs-field-line`), so the canvas's draft and the card's chip are drawn in one line. **Warn means "scheduled" everywhere**, as on the status chip: a scheduled record's centre dot and a scheduled change's corner dot; unpublished changes are `dim`. **A draft is dashed on every mark**, so no state rests on colour alone. The corner dot stands one pixel in from the mark's bottom-right corner (on the 45 degree line of a circle, on the OUTER ring of a ring type), so the corners are: notes top-left, pin top-right, changes bottom-right. `changes` is read for a live record only, and a status the table does not know is drawn live, not left undrawn. A hub, a tag and the pill have no state. One test walks five marks by the five states against a second copy of the table written in the test and asserts the 25 drawings are all different (`test/system/admin/mind_graph_drawing_modules_test.rb`: "marks: every mark that has a state is drawn in each of the five looks exactly as the table says, no two alike, and a draft is dashed on every one").

**The theme's keys and the token behind each** (`theme.js`, `TOKENS`): `ink` `--cs-ink`, `meta` `--cs-meta`, `dim` `--cs-dim`, `warn` `--cs-warn`, `surface` `--cs-surface`, `sunken` `--cs-sunken`, `accent` `--cs-accent`, `line` `--cs-line-strong` (the pill's and the notes badge's border), `edge` `--cs-field-line` (a draft's dashed outline), `page` `--cs-graph-page` (a page's mark: ink in light, `--cs-dim` in dark, where ink glares; pages stay neutral, since the hues mean post types), `tag` `--cs-graph-tag` (a tag's square and its label: `--cs-ic-structure`), the five lines `lineTree` `--cs-graph-line-tree`, `lineRelated` `--cs-graph-line-related`, `lineTag` `--cs-graph-line-tag`, `lineLists` `--cs-graph-line-lists` and `lineBrings` `--cs-graph-line-brings` (below), `faded` `--cs-graph-faded` (a number, 0.3 in both schemes: the strength of what a selection leaves out, read with `parseFloat`; a missing token draws at full strength, never not at all), `categories` the six `--cs-cat-<n>` hues, the three fonts (below) and `radius` from `--cs-radius`, read as a number of pixels (a token given in `rem` would be read as that many pixels, with no error). **The script holds no colour value and no strength**: every colour is a token read here (`test/lib/admin_design_rules_test.rb`'s raw-colour rule covers `app/javascript/mind_graph`), and `globalAlpha` is set only to 1 or `theme.faded`, in the exact forms an allow-list names (`test/lib/mind_graph_guards_test.rb`: "no script of the mind graph sets a strength but 1: what fades is the theme's", with "every strength allowance is still written"); every key `LOOKS` and `LINE_STYLES` name is one `theme.js` reads ("every theme key LOOKS and LINE_STYLES name is one theme.js reads"). Every module test but one draws with a theme whose values are their own names (`recording_theme` in `test/support/mind_graph_modules_system_test_case.rb`), which holds "a draft's outline is the key `edge`" and not "`edge` is `--cs-field-line`"; one test gives each token a value of its own and compares the whole object (`mind_graph_drawing_modules_test.rb`: "theme: every key reads the token the design names, the type hues, the fonts and the card's radius included"). **The contrast of every graph token is measured** on `--cs-surface` in both schemes (`test/lib/admin_contrast_test.rb`): related 3.92 / 4.38:1, tree 3.85 / 4.64, shared tag 3.96 / 4.59, lists 8.21 / 8.01, a tag 7.46 / 8.91, a draft's edge 3.60 / 4.17, the six post hues 6.39 to 6.62 in light and 8.45 to 8.74 in dark (each retuned in lightness only, keeping its oklch hue and chroma, so a type keeps its hue on every screen); ink at `faded` stays under 3:1 (1.94 / 2.40), so a faded node never reads as active, and the faintest hue at `faded` above 1.5:1 (1.56 / 1.97), so it is still seen ("what a selection leaves out stays seen and never reads as a control").

**The canvas is in the sans**, CS Studio's one type voice: `font` is `12px` and `strongFont` (the selected node's label) `600 12px` of the page's own family, and `countFont` (the notes count and a pill's words, as `text-count` sets a count on the page) `500 11px`, each a face the stylesheet loads, so a label is measured in the face it is drawn in. 11px is the least. No script of the graph reads `--cs-mono` or names a mono font (`test/lib/mind_graph_guards_test.rb`: "the canvas's text is in the sans: no script of the mind graph reads --cs-mono or names a mono font"; `test/system/admin/mind_graph_drawing_modules_test.rb`: "renderer: the notes count is in the sans at 11px and the widest count, 99+, fits its badge" checks the loaded faces). "99+" is 19.6px in Archivo, so its 26px badge leaves it 3px each side.

**The lines** are five styles (`renderer.js`, `LINE_STYLES`), one path per style and strength, drawn in this order so a tree line is on top of a tag's, each in its own theme key whose token carries its strength: *Shares a tag* dashed `lineTag` (1px, 4 / 3; meta at 80%); *Related* solid `lineRelated` (1.5px; ink at 55% in light, 51% in dark); a hub's *brings* line solid `lineBrings` (1px; `--cs-line`, a grouping line, unmeasured); *Lists* dotted `lineLists` (`--cs-dim`): 1.5px with ROUND caps and a dash of 0.1 / 3.5, which is a row of round dots 1.5px across (with a butt cap it would be a hairline); *Tree* solid `lineTree` (1px; dim at 71% in light, 70% in dark). **A translucent line is written `rgba()`**, its base token's colour with the base named in a comment beside it (`content-spiel.css`), because a canvas takes `rgba()` and `color-mix()` reaches it as a string it cannot resolve; a change of a base token's value is made in its line too (`test/lib/admin_contrast_test.rb`: "a graph line's translucent colour is its base token's colour"), and the contrast test measures the line, not its base. **Below 40% zoom a shared tag's lines are not drawn** (`HIDE_TAG_LINES_BELOW`): zoomed out they are most of the ink and say the least. That is a drawing rule only: the legend's counts and a selection's neighbours do not change with it. The focus of a local view has a dotted ring in the accent colour.

**The states** combine, and none is colour alone. *Hovered* — by the pointer or by its row in the list — is a 1px ink ring and the label in full ink on a backing, shown even where the label rule hides it; hovering dims nothing. *Selected* is a 2px accent ring with a gap, a semibold label, its own edges brightened (drawn in ink at full strength, each keeping its kind's dash and cap), and every node and edge not connected to it at `faded`. Faded text (a label, a pill's words, a notes count) is never the only place a word is read: every node and pill has a row in the list, a count is in words on the card, and pressing a node or clearing the selection brings it back. *Pinned* is a small ink dot with a surface ring at the mark's top-right, drawn with the node at the node's strength; the legend's "Pinned" swatch is the same mark, drawn by the same code. *With open notes* is the count in a small pill at the mark's top-left, opposite the pin (never a ring: a ring is hovered, selected or the focus), always neutral (unread is said on the card's rows); its right edge is fixed 4 px over the corner and it grows to the left, so it never reaches the pin's dot; above ninety-nine it reads "99+". **A page's card (Home's too) carries its badge OUTSIDE it** (K3): the badge's right edge stands 2px clear of the card's left edge, centred on the card's top edge, growing to the left, so it does not cover the card (`notesPill` in `marks.js`; a post's badge, the pin and the hit test stay where they were). **Every badge is drawn after every mark and label**, on a 2px ring of `surface` (the canvas's ground, a theme key, `NOTES_RING`) at its node's strength, so one that lands on a neighbour covers it cleanly; the layout makes no room for badges (Claude Design, 2026-10-25), and in a dense tree a badge covers a neighbour's mark by design. The renderer's culling margin grew with it (`NODE_MARGIN` 30). A ring follows its mark: a tag's is a square, a page's card's a box, the pill's a pill (rounded as the pill is: as first built it was a square-cornered box round a rounded pill, two borders), every other a circle. So does what hangs on a corner: on a box the pin and the notes badge stand on the box's own corners (`marks.js`, `topCorner`), not where a circle as wide as it would have them (a pill's radius is half its width, 30 or more, so its pin would have stood 21px above a 16px-high pill).

**What is not drawn.** A node whose mark lies wholly outside the canvas is skipped, and so is an edge that can't cross it: both ends beyond the same side. Not "both ends outside" — an edge from far left to far right crosses the whole view. This came out of the measurement: zoomed in, most of the graph is outside, and a path of long dashed lines costs the rasteriser by its length whether it is seen or not, which made about one frame in fifty late at the cap. And nothing is drawn before the worker's first positions arrive (`state.placed`): until then every node is at 0, 0, and the first frame after the data came would draw the whole graph as a pile in the canvas's top-left corner — for one frame normally, and for good when the worker failed.

**Hit testing is by containment first** (`hit_test.js`). A node's mark and its label are one target. *What is drawn comes first*: a press INSIDE a mark (within a dot's radius, on a box) is a press on that mark, whatever other target reaches the point with its slack; the nearer centre decides only among marks that hold the point, or, where none does, among the targets that reach it (a mark's radius plus four pixels of slack, never less than 24 pixels across, at any zoom). As first built the nearer CENTRE always won, and a pill is 60 pixels wide or more, so a press on its drawn end was 30 from its centre and a post resting against it took a press that was on the pill's words. A page's card and the pill are pressed ON THE BOX (`boxes`, from `marks.js`'s `boxOf`), grown by the same slack and never less than the least target either way: a circle as wide as the pill would take every press 25 pixels above and below it. Then the boxes of the labels *the last frame drew* (a label that is not drawn can't be pressed), the last drawn first, because that one is on top. Still a linear scan over the visible nodes: at 2,202 nodes a pointer move's handler returns in under 0.1 ms, 0.3 ms at worst, so no quadtree is built (one is warranted only if a frame is late).

**The label rule** (`labels.js`). (1) Hubs first, among the nodes *on the canvas*: the zoom decides how many labels there are — 40 at zoom 1, growing with the square of the zoom — and they go to the best-connected nodes inside the view, so zoomed into a corner the budget is not spent on hubs nobody can see. The cost is that a label can appear or disappear while panning. The last quarter of that reach is drawn fainter, so a label arrives gradually with zoom; with `prefers-reduced-motion` it switches. (2) No overlap: a label is placed only where no earlier one is, and better-connected nodes place first. (3) The selected node, then the focus, then the selection's neighbours are labelled at any zoom and place before everything else — but rule 2 holds among them too: **no overlap wins over "always labelled"**, and a neighbour gives way to the selection however well connected it is. (4) **`free`**: Home, the top-level pages and the post-type nodes (`graph_data.js`, `freeOf`) are labelled at any zoom and do not count against the budget of rule 1; they place after rule 3's and before the ranked rest, and no overlap still wins over "always shown". `chooseLabels` has no default for `free`, on purpose: the wiring only shows on a graph with more free nodes than the zoom's budget, which no small test graph is, so a caller that forgets the argument throws in every frame instead. **The order** labels are placed in (`orderOf`) is Home, then the top-level pages and the type nodes, then everything else by its lines, a page ahead of a post with as many (`PAGE_BONUS`, half a line, a reading). **The colours**: a page's and a post type's label in ink, a tag's in the structure hue (`tag`), a post's in `dim` until it is hovered or selected, both of which are full ink; the pill has no label, its words are inside it. A label's width is measured in the font it is drawn in (`renderer.js`, `labelFont`), in the sans, once plain and once semibold, so the label rule, the hit test and the hover's backing agree with the words drawn. A label sits right of its node, is cut at 32 code points with an ellipsis (so an emoji is never cut in half), and a tag's keeps its count whole: "name (N)". **A label is not placed over a notes badge**: the badges' boxes, ring included (`notesBadgeBox` in `marks.js`, collected by `badgeBoxes`), are `obstacles` that `chooseLabels` refuses a label over by the rule that already keeps labels apart; a label only ever sits right of its node, so one blocked by a badge is not shown (the hovered label is drawn whatever the rule says, obstacles included).

**Selecting, and the card.** A click on a node — its mark or its drawn label — selects it: it and its direct neighbours *by the edges shown* stay at full strength, the rest dims, and the card opens **beside the node** (`card_placement.js`): 22 pixels from the node's edge and vertically centred on it, on the node's left where the canvas's right edge is too near, and clamped 8 pixels inside the canvas. Its place is worked out in the frame that draws the node, from the card's size as measured once when it was filled, so it follows a pan, a zoom and the layout's own movement without lagging, and its style is written only when it moves. It hides while its own node is dragged and returns on the drop. It was in the canvas's top-right corner before, and the owner's remark was that it was far from what it is about; the corner is still where it goes for a node without a place (the worker failed and the selection came from the list). A post's card has its type's dot and name, its status chip, the title, "N related · N tags" in the small label style, and its actions as CS Studio's buttons: "Open post" (primary), "Focus", "Pin" / "Unpin"; and its open notes (`card_notes.js`: "Open notes · N", at most three rows, "+N more" into the post's editor with `?notes=open`, and a field that starts a note with origin view `mind_graph`), read from `GET notes/post/<id>/threads` (`admin_record_note_threads_data_path`, a bare 404 outside the read scope) after the card is shown, so the card is measured again when they arrive. The notes stand under the title and its counts and the actions follow them, on screen and in the document alike (the document order follows the screen), so Tab walks the card as it reads: the notes' rows and the "Start a note…" field, then "Open post", "Focus" and Pin. "Open post" is therefore not the first stop and has a key of its own: Enter on the selected node, on the canvas or on the card as it takes the focus from the list, opens the post (on a control inside the card Enter is that control's). As first built the notes were drawn above the actions but came after them in the document (the actions' row was `order-1`), so the focus order and the visual order disagreed. An unread row is ink for dim, an accent dot and the visually hidden word "new". The card's notes section is a `record` of `admin--notes--live`, so it reads its list again when a thread of the post changes (`docs/reference/notes.md`, "Live delivery"). A click on the node the card already shows does not build the card again, so a note half typed in it stays. **A page's card** (Home's is the same): its mark and its address, the status chip, the title, "4 child pages · lists 112 posts" (`child_count`, `listed_count`: the scoped payload's, nothing is read a second time), its open notes as on a post's card (`data-page-notes-url`; the field says "Write a note on this page"), then "Open page" (the node's `url`), "Focus" and Pin; Enter on a selected page presses "Open page". **A post type's card**, and "All posts"': its hub's mark and "Post type", its name (a retired type's ends "(retired)"), "N posts" (`count`), Pin, and no link: a focus is a page, a post or a tag. **A pill is not selected**: a press on it opens its rows in the list (below). **The chips and Close are the shared components, cloned.** `Admin::MindGraphScreen::PartsComponent` renders the status chips (`StatusChipComponent`: live, draft, scheduled), the `UnpublishedChipComponent`, an `IconChipComponent` per kind (a page `:pages` in `:structure`, a post `:posts`, a tag `:tags` and a post type `:clipboard_list` in `:lists`, all `:small`) and the card's Close (`ButtonComponent`, `icon: :x`, `:tertiary`, `:small`, named by `parts.close`) once, into one `<template data-mind-graph-parts>` at the end of the graph's element, one `span[data-part]` around each (`PartsComponent::NAMES`). `mind_graph/parts.js`'s `readParts(element).part(name)` answers a deep clone of a part's element, and throws `mind graph: no part named …` for a name the template does not hold, so a missing part is a broken page and never a card without its chip. A template's content is no part of the document: the page's text, its Tab order and `reset()` never meet it, and the clones land only inside the card and the list, which `reset()` empties. **A clone is never changed after it is cloned**: whatever a script says beside it goes on the element it is put into. `test/components/admin/mind_graph_screen/parts_component_test.rb` holds each part to its component's own markup. **The status chip**, on a page's and a post's card alike: a record in one state has the shared chip of that state beside what it is, and the chip's own `data-status` names it. A live record with more to say has a row of its own under the first, `[data-chip-row]`, for the eye only (`aria-hidden`), as the editor's header says it: the Live chip, then the unpublished chip, or the words "Change scheduled 14 Oct" (`script.card.status.change_scheduled`, the server's `change_on`); the row's `data-status` (`live_with_changes`, `live_change_scheduled`) names the state, and it wraps inside the card's padding, clear of Close, with longer words too (`mind_graph_selection_test.rb`: "a two-state chip wraps inside the card, clear of Close, in German too"). A screen reader is given the legend's status words in one sentence at the end of the first row, "Live, unpublished changes" or "Live, change scheduled 14 Oct" (one set of words, in three files that are changed together, which `test/lib/mind_graph_guards_test.rb` holds: "the legend's status words are the chip's: the status chip's three labels and the card's two sentences without their day"; `legend.states` in `mind_graph_screen.en.yml`; the chips' own words, `status_chip.labels` and `unpublished_chip.label` in `ui.en.yml`, which the card's chips and a list row's name say; and `script.card.status` in `mind_graph.en.yml`, the change's words and the two sentences). The chips draw from the tokens, so a theme switched while the card is open changes them with nothing cloned again, and a note half typed in the card stays ("the card's chips follow the theme, and what is typed in the card stays"). A mark in the card is a small canvas drawn by the graph's renderer (`markCanvas`, `paintMarks`), not a copy in CSS. **A post added from the list says nothing of its related posts and tags**: its row was read with no line counted, so its two counts are null and the card leaves the "N related · N tags" line out instead of saying "0 related · 0 tags" of a post that may have both. A tag's has "Show posts with this tag" (the posts list with its `tag` filter), "Focus" and "Pin" / "Unpin". What leads somewhere is a link drawn as a button, so it opens in a new tab like any link and a test follows it with `click_frame_link`; Pin is a button. Close is the shared icon button (×, named "Close", with the shared tooltip), *last* in the document, so Tab meets the actions first; the card's own classes in `show.html.erb` (`[&>[data-mind-graph-card-close]]:absolute …`) stand it in the top-right corner over the tooltip host's `relative`, and the first row keeps clear of it (`pr-8`). **A click on the canvas never navigates**; the card's links do. A click on empty canvas, Escape (from the canvas or from inside the card) and Close clear the selection; the last two give the focus back to where the card was opened from, the canvas or the list's row. A press that moves is a drag, not a click, and a plain click sends the worker nothing — a `drag` reheats the layout, and the graph would twitch under every click. A toggle of the legend recomputes the neighbours, and clears the selection when it hides the selected node. The tooltip with the whole title is shown only for a title the label cuts short: the hovered label on its backing already names the node.

**Pinning.** A dropped node stays where it was dropped: `drop` leaves `fx` / `fy` set in the worker, where an `unpin` sent on drop would spring the node back. A node can also be pinned where it stands from its card. Which nodes are pinned is the page's to know (`state.pinned`, indexes) and to show: the pin mark, the card's button reading "Unpin", `data-pinned` on the element, and "Release N pinned" beside the zoom control, which is in the page hidden and is shown, with its pluralised label, only while something is pinned. Three things release: "Unpin", "Release N pinned", and a double click on the node (`input.js`'s `onDoubleClick`; both of its clicks have selected the node already). **Pins live in the page that made them**: nothing is stored, a reload forgets them, and the controller's `reset()` takes the count and the button back on a restored snapshot. **A toggle of the legend touches no pin**: a pin is the layout's, and letting a hidden node go would warm the layout up, which a toggle must not do. A pinned node that a toggle hides is still held, still counted by "Release N pinned", and comes back pinned where it was dropped (`test/system/admin/mind_graph_dragging_test.rb`: "a toggle keeps every pin, also of a node it hides, which comes back pinned where it was dropped"). This reverses the radios' behaviour, where a filter change forgot the pins of the nodes it hid. A node is held where it was grabbed, not by its centre, so a drag by the label does not make the mark jump to the pointer.

**The legend is the FILTER, and a filter moves nothing.** `Admin::MindGraphScreen::LegendComponent` draws three groups. **Show** (`data-legend-section=show`, a visible label hidden from a screen reader, because the fieldsets keep their legends "Show" and "Lines") holds two fieldsets of native checkboxes (`Admin::Ui::CheckboxComponent`), all on: Pages, Posts, Tags, Post types; Tree, Lists, Related, Shares a tag. **Key** (the list is labelled by it: the label is the span `#mind-graph-legend-key`, and the list is `data-legend-keys`) holds the marks that are not controls (the post types' dots, "Retired type" when present, "Pinned", "Open notes"). **Status** (`data-legend-section=status`, labelled by `#mind-graph-legend-states`, the list `data-legend-states`) holds the five looks (below). The marks and the looks hold no stop, so after the column the Tab order is the toggles, the zoom buttons, then the canvas. The radios (All / Related / Tags) are gone, and there is no "Links in text" toggle, not even a disabled one (step two). `graph_data.js`'s `visible({ nodes, edges, on })` is the one place that decides what is shown: a kind of node that is off hides its nodes and every line that touches one; **a kind of line that is off hides its lines and nothing else**, so a node is hidden by its own toggle only; the pill stands for posts and goes with them; a hub's faint `brings` line has no toggle and follows "Lists". It is held over all 256 combinations of the eight toggles in one script call. **A toggle is a filter on what is DRAWN, not a new layout.** The layout is computed once, over everything the data holds; a toggle sends the worker nothing, moves no node, warms nothing up, leaves `data-settled`, the camera and every pin as they are, and what is switched back on comes back where it was. What a toggle changes is `state.visibleNodes` and `state.visibleEdges`, which the renderer, the hit test, the labels, the list and the summary read. **A toggle does not go to the worker as a filter** (the pages spec's way): that was the old radios' way, and a toggle that re-ran the whole layout at alpha 0.8 would with eight toggles, four of them of lines alone, the picture reshuffled when a line was hidden, nodes the toggle did not touch moved, and one could leave the view with no refit (in the review's screenshot the page "Team" was outside the canvas after only tags had been switched off), while "comes back where it was" was asserted only as "has a position". The designer's "the legend is the filter" reads the other way, and so does a person. The price is said in `app.js`'s header and is the designer's to rule on: a cluster does not close up when what held it apart is hidden. The test compares every node's position before and after each of the eight toggles, off and on again, and records every message to the worker (`test/system/admin/mind_graph_legend_test.rb`: "a toggle moves nothing: after each of the eight, off and on again, every node is where it was and the layout was told nothing").

**Which toggles a viewer gets is one rule**, `PostGraph::Kinds.shown(pages:, posts:)`, read off what the builder can answer: a page, a hub, a tree line and a lists line need a readable page; a post, a related line and a shared tag's line a readable post; **a tag needs either**. The tag is the case a rule of "tags hang on posts" misses, and as first built did miss: someone who reads pages and no post is shown the tags their pages list, and without a toggle for them could not switch them off and had no key that says what the square is. The legend asks `Kinds` and holds no rule of its own; `Kinds` is a hand-written table, held equal to what `Global` and `Local` answer by `test/queries/post_graph/kinds_test.rb` on a site built to contain every kind, so a new kind of node or line needs a row there. **A kind is hidden only by a toggle that is switched OFF, never by a toggle that is absent**: as first built "what is shown" was "the toggles that are checked", so a kind the viewer has no toggle for was never shown (a real viewer without a toggle has no such nodes in the data, and only a stubbed payload found it). The post types the legend names are those of the viewer's readable posts: a legend of every registered type would disclose types they can't read. Every toggle carries `autocomplete="off"`: "a new visit starts from the defaults", and Firefox would otherwise put a form's state back on a reload.

**The counts beside the toggles are the script's** (the page holds no data): `counts(nodes, edges)`, the number of each kind and of each kind of line in this response ("Post types" counts the type nodes and "All posts"; a hub's faint lines are no Lists lines), written once per load, with the checkbox's whole accessible name ("Pages, 200", one key). **A toggle changes none of them**: one that is off still says what switching it on brings. `reset()` takes the counts and those names back on a restored snapshot; **the checkboxes' state is the person's and is kept** (`test/system/admin/mind_graph_worker_and_back_test.rb`: "Back to the graph draws it once, with what the legend shows: its counts gone until the data is back, its checkboxes kept"). A toggle can show a count of 0 for a kind the viewer may be shown and has none of.

**Status is not a filter.** The five looks are a key on the legend's row, its third group, after Key: one row per `LegendComponent::STATES` (`Publishing::State`'s names: live, draft, scheduled, live with changes, live with a change scheduled), each the canvas's own drawing of a post in that state (`state-*` swatches) and its name in the chip's words, with no control among them and no date (the day of a scheduled change stands only on a card's chip). The words are `legend.states`, held to the chips' and the card's by `test/lib/mind_graph_guards_test.rb` ("the legend's status words are the chip's: the status chip's three labels and the card's two sentences without their day"; `test/components/admin/mind_graph_screen/legend_component_test.rb`: "the legend's Status group holds the five looks in the chip's words, each with its canvas swatch, after the keys", "the legend opens no dialog and has no button"). The guard reads the English files, the only ones CS Studio has, and its join rule (the Live chip's word, a comma, the second word lower-cased) is English's: a second admin language needs a words check of its own. The swatches are `admin--mind-graph--swatches`' own, so the graph's script writes nothing in them and `reset()` has nothing of them to take back.

**The swatches and the row marks are canvases drawn by the canvas's own marks.** `Admin::MindGraphScreen::SwatchComponent` renders an empty sized `<canvas data-swatch>` (`SIZES`: each swatch's size in CSS pixels; hidden from a screen reader, its name stands beside it), and `mind_graph/swatches.js` holds one scene per swatch, one node or one line, and hands it to `renderer.js`'s `draw`, so `marks.js` and `LINE_STYLES` draw it at the smallest size the canvas draws. A swatch is therefore not a copy of a mark in CSS or SVG that could drift from it: change a look in `LOOKS`, a size, a line's dash, and the legend changes with the canvas. The pages plan had SVG on `.mg-*` classes and the approved spec said so; the swatches were ruled otherwise while building, every `.mg-*` rule that drew a mark is gone from the stylesheet (what is left is `.mg-swatch`, which draws nothing) and the older keys (retired, pinned, notes) became canvases too. A swatch that names no type has no hue (the hues mean post types): the toggles' post is `dim`, as "All posts" is, and the status looks' mark is a post's dot in ink (`KEY_MARK`, a reading). **A row's mark** in the list, a search result and a card is the same thing for a node of the data: `markCanvas(node)` answers a small canvas for a PAGE or a POST TYPE and `paintMarks(root)` draws every one inside `root`, smaller than on the canvas by a scale and nothing else (`ROW_MARKS`: a hub at 10/12, a page at 10/14), so a post type's row shows its hub as the canvas has it. A row's mark shows the KIND, not the record's state: a page's is the live look whatever the page's state is. A post's dot and a tag's square in a row are NOT canvases: they are CS Studio's own type dot and tag square, and a list can hold two thousand posts. Nothing is lazy, and the bound is the pages and the hubs: 208 canvases painted in 1.4 to 1.8 ms at the measurement. Both controllers repaint on a theme change, and the swatches after Back; a change of the device pixel ratio (a window dragged to another screen) repaints nothing, which is on the roadmap. `element.querySelector("canvas")` in `app.js` would have found a swatch (the legend comes first in the document), so the graph's canvas is found by its target.

**The canvas's summary** (`mind_graph/summary.js`, `summaryOf`, a pure function, so every form is a module test with no layout). The canvas is one image to assistive technology (`role="img"`) and its name says what is on it NOW: the counts in view by type, then the lines, then the scope. Until the data has come it is "Mind graph". The forms, in the designer's words:

- the whole graph: "Mind graph: 9 pages, 3 post types, 13 posts and 4 tags, 27 connections. Showing everything.";
- a local view: "Mind graph around Blog, depth 2: 7 pages, 1 post type and 20 of 112 posts. 92 more are in the list." ("N of M posts" and the "more" sentence only around a page that lists more than the view draws: M is the page's `listed_count`, and "more" counts every pill in view together; a kind with nothing in view is left out);
- the view a large site opened in adds "Showing the area around Home. The site has 212 pages, 2,040 posts and 64 tags." (what the viewer can read of the site; no other view says it);
- a kind switched off adds "Tags hidden.", a kind of line "Shares-a-tag lines hidden.", after the scope, kinds then lines: "Showing everything. Tags hidden." are both said, since "everything" means no cap, not no filters;
- nothing in view: "Mind graph: nothing to show. All types are hidden." when every toggle the viewer HAS is off, "Mind graph: nothing to show. No pages, posts or tags yet." when there is nothing, and "Mind graph: nothing to show. 2 posts have no connections yet." when there are posts and no connection among them: the notice's own sentence, a reading, since neither of the designer's two is true there;
- **"Showing everything." is never said at a cap**: a whole graph cut at the posts' cap says the notice's cap sentence in its place.

A sentence is one message, two are joined by one space and nothing else, and the counts are joined by the messages' own `pair` and `series`, so another language can join them otherwise. After a toggle the same words go into the notice's polite live region in an `sr-only` line, which is how the change is announced.

**The list** in the tree column is the keyboard and screen-reader path, and a way to find a node by name for everyone: nothing on a canvas can take focus, so every drawn node is also a row. **One tree, four groups**: the list is an APG tree, `ul#mind-graph-tree[role="tree"]`, named by the region's name, and each group is a level-1 item whose heading is its row: "Pages · N" first, in TREE order (not by connections: a tree is read by its order), then "Posts · N" and "Tags · N", best-connected first, then "Post types · N", a row for each post-type node and for "All posts" in the order the data has them (colour slot, then retired, then "All posts" last), so each has a keyboard path to its card; a row's number there is the type's POSTS, where a tag's row has its connections. **A row is the Pages tree's row** (`Admin::Ui::TreeItemComponent`'s classes, handed over as `data-tree-classes`; `docs/reference/design-system.md`, "Tree"): the disclosure box, the mark box, the title and the figure at the row's end, shown at rest. A page has the disclosure box, holding the disclosure part where its children have rows and empty otherwise, and so do a tag's and a post type's row, with the disclosure where a pill hangs off it, so a group's marks line up; a post's row is flat. Every figure, a row's and a heading's, is grouped for the messages' language (`number`). The disclosure is a part hidden from a screen reader, never a button, because the treeitem is the one control and says itself whether its branch is open (`aria-expanded`): a click on the disclosure folds or opens the branch, a click anywhere else on the row selects. The marked row takes the tree's selected look. **Each group's heading is an item of the tree too**, at the first level, 28px and never `aria-selected`; Enter, Space and a click fold or open it, and its row holds the disclosure part, its kind's icon chip in the mark box (the part `icon-page`, `icon-post`, `icon-tag` or `icon-type`), and the words in the label style with the figure after the middle dot in the count style; `--cs-sp-3` stands above every heading but the first, and a heading adds no step, so the rows under it start at its left edge. **A row's mark** is the node's own (a page's the tree's page glyph and Home's the house, a post type's the renderer's canvas, a post's its type's dot, a tag's the square), and **under the title, on a line of their own, a row shows its state as the posts list does, by its rule** (`posts_screen/table_component.html.erb`): the status chip unless the record is live, then the unpublished chip when the data's `unpublished` is set, which is `Publishing::State#changes?`, the posts list's `unpublished_changes?` (`PostGraph::Builder` writes it on every page and post node). So a scheduled record can carry both, a live record with a change scheduled carries the unpublished chip only when it is also marked, and a plain live row, a tag, a post type and a pill carry none. The chips stand under the title so that the title keeps the row's whole width, cut only by the row's edge. The rows stand at Pages' x, inside the column's padding with no gutter of their own, and the lines that are no row (the checkbox, a read's heading, the empty and error lines, Back and the last line) at `px-1`, where a row's disclosure column starts; at the column's default width (272px) a page three levels down shows at least twelve characters of its title, a cut title ends in an ellipsis, and the whole title is its title box's tooltip and begins the item's name (`mind_graph_list_test.rb`: "at the default column width a page three levels down shows at least 12 characters of its title, and its whole title is its tooltip and its name"; `tree_item_parity_test.rb` measures each row's left edge from `#tree-column`). The list's Scheduled chip has no tooltip with the time, where the posts list's has one (`at:`): the chips are cloned once from the parts and never changed, so a row cannot carry its own time. Each chip is a part's clone in a wrapper hidden from a screen reader, and **the row's name says the same words**: the title is followed by each chip's own word through `list.stated` ("Beta, Scheduled, Unpublished changes: post, 2 connections. Shows it in the graph."), read from the chips' own keys in `ui.en.yml`, which the view serves in `data-messages` (`mind_graph_card_and_list_modules_test.rb`: "list: a row's name says its state in the chips' own words"). **The Pages group is a tree for a screen reader too**, not an indent alone: a page's row stands in a list INSIDE its parent's item (by the data's `parent`), so the nesting is the document's and is announced as a level; the step is the tree's children list (`--cs-tree-step` a level below Home, as in Pages), a page whose parent has no row here (a local view) stands at the top of the group with no step and no guide, and says where it stands: its ancestors' titles from Home (`ancestors`, which `PostGraph::Builder` writes for such a page and which stops under a page the viewer cannot read) on a `text-small` line under its title joined by " › " and cut at the end, and the whole path in its tooltip and its name (`list.under`: "Print design, under Home › Services"), and each item says its level (`aria-level`) inside its parent's `role="group"`. **One Tab stop.** One item holds `tabindex="0"`, the marked one or else the first, and the stop follows the focus; on the tree Up and Down move to the previous or next item that shows, Right opens a folded parent or goes to its first child, Left folds an open parent or goes to its parent, Home and End go to the first and the last, `*` opens the item's whole branch, and Enter or Space presses the item (`mind_graph_card_and_list_modules_test.rb`: "one Tab stop: the arrows, Home and End move the focus, which skips what is folded or hidden"). There is no type-ahead: the find is the way to a row by its name. A redraw keeps the stop on the same node, else the first, and is told as `tree:changed` on the tree; a find that hides the stop's item moves the stop to the nearest item that shows, and the find's Up on its first match is heard in the capture phase, before the tree's own Up. The first drawing uses up `data-tree-open` (it takes the attribute off the element, as `destroy` does), so a page Turbo restores from its snapshot draws the folds from the cookie as it is now (`mind_graph_worker_and_back_test.rb`: "Back to the graph draws the folds as the open set has them now"). **The folds**: a page's branch folds with the Pages tree's open set (the cookie `cs_tree_open`): the view hands the list `data-tree-open`, the cookie as `TreeOpenSet` read it for the pages the viewer reads (`MindGraphController#show`, `@tree_open`), which the first drawing follows, and every later one reads the cookie; a fold or an open is written through `setOpen` against the node's default (Home, `data-tree-root`, and the selected node's path, `data-tree-on-path`, are open), so a branch folded in Pages is folded here and back (`mind_graph_list_test.rb`: "a branch folded in the Pages tree is folded in the mind graph's list"). A group heading's fold and a hub's are a view state, `data-tree-view-fold`, open at first, never stored and kept while the list is on the screen. Selecting a node opens the path to it (`openPathIn`, which forgets the path's folds). **A row is a treeitem, and it selects**: it does to the graph what a click on the node does, centres the camera on the node, opens its card and moves the focus to the card (which is named by its title, so a screen reader says what opened); closing that card gives the focus back to the row. It never navigates; the card's links do. Its accessible name is one key that says all of it ("Alpha: post, 2 connections. Shows it in the graph."; `list.select_page` / `select_post` / `select_tag` / `select_type`), never strings joined in the script. Hovering or focusing a row gives its node the hovered state, and a node selected on the canvas has its item marked (`aria-selected`), the path to it opened, the tab stop moved to it and its row scrolled into view *inside the column*, by setting the column's `scrollTop` (the list's `scroller`), never `scrollIntoView`, which would scroll the page too; while the whole site's results stand in the tree's place a mark measures and scrolls nothing, and the marked row is there when Back gives the tree back (`mind_graph_card_and_list_modules_test.rb`: "list: a mark while the whole site's results show changes no scroll, and Back shows the marked row"). `node_list.js` rebuilds the list from the same data on every load and every toggle (about 30 ms for 2,202 rows at the measurement, row marks included), with one listener of each kind on the rows' region however many rows there are. It is filled before the layout starts, so it is there when the worker fails. **The focus is not lost when the rows are drawn again**: `render` and `showRows` replace the rows' own region and never the checkbox, and when the focus was in that region it is put on the item of the same node, else on the first item, else on the checkbox. The list prepends what it draws to its region and removes only its own on `destroy`, so the server's last line stays at the region's end. In the document the column, and so the list, comes before `<main>` ("The layout of the screen", above); the notice over the canvas is a polite live region.

**The find field and the last line.** The column's find field is the Pages tree's (`Admin::Ui::TreeFindComponent.new(controls: "mind-graph-tree", noun: :nodes)`, `admin--tree--find`, `tree/find.js`; `docs/reference/design-system.md`, "Tree"): "Find a page, post or tag" over the list's tree, on the client, a title matched anywhere and folding case and accents, what does not match hidden and a match's ancestors kept, a folded branch that holds a match opened without writing the open set, and the count said in its status line ("1 match", "3 matches", "Nothing matches"), whose one line's room is kept from the first key. "/" focuses it, Down goes to the first match and Up from it back to the field, Enter on the first match selects it and opens its card (a `.click()` on its treeitem), and Escape clears it. The find narrows the list and never the canvas (`mind_graph_search_test.rb`: "the find field narrows the list to the matches and their paths, and the graph is left as it is"; `mind_graph_list_test.rb`: "Down Arrow in the find field reaches the first match, and Enter selects it and opens its card"). Whichever tree the list shows carries `id="mind-graph-tree"` and `data-tree-locale`, the groups' tree or a read's (`showRows`), since the region holds one tree at a time: the find narrows the tree in view. The field's keys are the find controller's alone: the find hears Up in the capture phase, and the tree returns on a key whose default was prevented. **The last line** (`[data-mind-graph-search-site]`, the server's, at the end of the list's region and outside `role="tree"`, `mt-2`, one tertiary small button) shows while the field holds text, whether or not there is a tree to narrow: it follows the field's `input` (which `admin--tree--find` also dispatches when it empties the field itself, by Escape, Clear or before the snapshot), never the find's `tree-find:filtered`, which a graph that draws nothing, or whose read failed, never dispatches. It reads "Search the whole site for “…”" (`script.list.search_site`, the field's text in it), and pressing it asks `PostGraph::Search` (the line's `data-url`, through `search/suggestions.js` with the field's text at the press: an answer to text the field has since changed from is dropped), the list `aria-busy` while the request is out. The list draws the answer IN THE TREE'S PLACE (`showResults({ query, rows, failed, back })`): a region beside the rows, the checkbox and the tree hidden and kept underneath, so a redraw while the results show is not lost and `closeResults` gives them back. The results are Back first, "Back to the list" (`script.list.back`), which takes the focus; the heading "Whole site: “…”" (`script.list.search_site_heading`); the groups Pages, Posts and Tags, headed as the tree's groups are but no treeitems, and in them links to each one's local view on the tree row's geometry (`search_results.js`'s `renderResults`: the tree's page glyph, a post's dot or a tag's square, the title, and under a page's title its path in `text-small`, never mono); "No page, post or tag matches." when nothing does; and "Search failed — check your connection." in a `role="alert"` when the request fails. Back and Escape give the tree back with the query kept and the focus on the last line (on the field, when it was emptied meanwhile), and typing in the field while the results show gives the narrowed tree back, so no results stand under other text (`mind_graph_search_test.rb`: "the whole site's search finds a post the graph does not draw and opens its neighbourhood", "the whole site's results group pages, posts and tags, show a page's path, say when nothing matches, and Back and Escape return to the list with the query kept", "a failed whole-site search says so and the graph keeps working", "the results are text: a title with markup is not inserted as markup", "leaving the screen aborts a whole-site search still in flight"). `mind_graph/site_search.js` holds the line and the search (`attachSiteSearch(lastLine, { list, text, findField })`, `findField()` looked up each time), and its `stop()`, which `app.js`'s `destroy()` calls, cancels a search still out, stops listening to the document and hides the line.

**"Not listed on any page"** is a checkbox above the groups (only for a viewer who reads a page). While it is on, the list shows the `unlisted` read's rows IN PLACE OF the groups, under "Posts · N" with the read's whole total, and the canvas is left as it is; the checkbox is the way back. **A row that is not in view**: a row whose post the canvas does not draw says "not in view" where its count would stand, is named as the designer wrote it ("Small sites, long lives, blog post, not in view. Activate to add it to the graph.", with the post TYPE's label as the messages' language lowers it; a post of a retired type says "talk, retired type"), and pressing it ADDS the post (`addPost`): the row is the post's node as the data has it, so nothing is fetched; it gets the line of the hub that brings it in (`via`: a faint line from a type or "All posts", a "Shares a tag" line to a tag; an unlisted row has no hub and floats), the worker is told (`add`), and the post is selected, centred and its card focused. A row whose post IS drawn is the drawn node, with its connections as on the canvas. **Without a graph there is nothing to add to**: the checkbox is there before the graph is, and its read does not wait for it, so its rows can come while the graph is still being read, after that read failed, or while "Show everything" is on its way. Such a row says only that it is not in view (`script.list.absent_post`, the design's sentence without "Activate to add it to the graph."; `aria-disabled`), a press on it does nothing, and `addPost` returns at once when the page holds no data. When the graph arrives the checkbox's rows are kept (`show` lets only a PILL's rows go, which were of the graph before) and are drawn again as rows that add. As first built a press there threw: the post was pushed among the nodes before `countAdded` or the summary read the data that was not there, and the row was dead or read as one in view. **With "Posts" switched off the press switches it back on first**: the row says "Activate to add it to the graph", and as first built the press added a node nobody saw (the legend's count of Posts went up by one). **A read of the list** is a plain `fetch`, ended when the screen is left and given up after the same fifteen seconds as the graph's own load (`AbortSignal.any` with `AbortSignal.timeout`, on both paths and with no fallback: Safari before 17.4 and Firefox before 124 have no `AbortSignal.any` and throw there, so the graph says it could not be loaded and the list that it couldn't be, with a Retry that cannot work); while one is out the rows' region is `aria-busy` (all the pending state there is: nobody gave words for one). An answer that comes after a later ask, or after the groups were asked back, is not drawn (`asked`). **A read that fails says so IN THE LIST**, not in a toast: "The list couldn't be loaded." and a small "Try again", in a `role="alert"`; the checkbox above stays ticked, and the error line stays through a toggle of the legend until the read is asked again or let go (as first built a toggle drew the groups under a ticked checkbox and the error line vanished without a word).

**The pills: one per target, a row per pill, three figures from one source.** In a page's local view each target that brings in more than the twenty drawn has its own "+N more" pill, hanging off its hub or tag. **A press on a pill opens the list** with the posts THAT pill stands for (`listed` for its page and its target), under the heading "More from Blog · 92" (the pill's own count, so it falls with the pill as its posts are added and never says another figure than "+N more" does) with "Open in Posts →" under the rows (only where there are rows: the address is there with none too, and it shows every post of the type or tag, more than the pill counted). **Each pill has a row in the list**, the child row of what it hangs off (which draws a disclosure for it), one step in and flat, in the pill's own words ("+92 more", in the accent) and named whole, beginning with those visible words so that voice control can press it by them ("+92 more: 92 more posts Blog lists, not in view.
