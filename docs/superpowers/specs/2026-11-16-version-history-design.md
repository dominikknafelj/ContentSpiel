# Version history: look back, compare, restore

Design, 2026-11-16. The rest of "Versioning: revisions and restore" on the roadmap (`docs/roadmap.md`), after drafts of live content (2026-10-12) made every publish keep its copy. Built on `docs/reference/publishing.md`; the spec it continues is `2026-10-11-drafts-of-live-content-design.md`.

## 1. What it is for

Every publish of a page or post has kept a whole copy of it since 2026-10-12 (`published_versions`), and nothing in CS Studio shows them. Someone who wants last week's wording back, or wants to know what the publish on Tuesday changed, has to retype it from memory or ask a developer to read a table.

After this, a person opens a record's **History**, sees every version that was published, sees what each one changed (or how it differs from what they are working on now), and **restores** one. A restore never goes public by itself: it brings the old version into the working rows as unpublished changes, and the person publishes it with "Publish changes…", as any other change. The owner's rule from drafts of live content stands: what a person does while working and what the public sees are separate, and the step between them is theirs, on purpose, with a view of what will change.

The same piece settles the open question about **Publish now** on a scheduled record (D9, decided by the owner).

## 2. Decisions

### Decided by the owner

- **D9. Publish now publishes what was scheduled; with changes saved since, it asks.** "Publish now" on a scheduled record still publishes the scheduled version by default. When the record has changes saved after the schedule was set (the mark: `Publishing::State#changes?`), the Publish now dialog offers a choice: **"Publish the scheduled version"** (the default) or **"Publish with the later changes"**, and says plainly which one goes live. Bulk publish in the posts list and MCP's `update_post(published: true)` keep publishing what was scheduled, as today; they show nothing to choose from.

### Rulings (mine, with their cost if wrong)

- **R1. A version is a published copy, and only that.** One row of `published_versions` is one version, numbered as the copy is (`number`). Autosave points and audit snapshots are not versions. *Why:* a copy is whole (the record's fields, every language, every block, the tags) and immutable, and `Snapshot.rebuild` and `DiscardChanges` already read and restore it; an audit snapshot is per subject (a page's blocks are separate subjects, each coalesced over ten minutes) and keeps a translation source's digest, not its text, so a page at an autosave point cannot be rebuilt from it. The roadmap's note "audit snapshots are the version basis" predates the copies and is superseded; the roadmap row says so after this. *Cost if wrong:* a draft that was never published has no history, and a version between two publishes cannot be returned to. The Activity screen still lists every saved change of fields, so what was typed is not lost from view, only from restore. Autosave points can be added later as a second kind of entry without changing what a version is.
- **R2. The history starts at the first copy.** Live and scheduled records got their first copy from the migration of 2026-10-11 (`created_by_id` empty); such a copy is listed as the first version with "Kept when history began" in place of a person. A record that was never published lists no versions and says that a version is kept each time it is published. A record unpublished again keeps its copies and its history. Nothing is backfilled from the audit log. *Cost if wrong:* nothing before 2026-10-11 is reachable, as it is not today.
- **R3. A restore writes the working rows, never a publication.** It is "Discard changes…" with another copy as the target: the rows become the chosen version's content, the site keeps showing the copy in force, and the record carries unpublished changes until someone publishes or discards them. A pending (scheduled) change is left as it is: restore does not withdraw it, and the toast says when one is scheduled. On a draft (no `published_at`) the rows become the version and nothing else changes. *Cost if wrong:* a person who wanted the old version on the site at once presses twice (restore, then publish). The opposite (restore publishes) would put an unreviewed version public with one press, which the owner's rule forbids.
- **R4. Rights.** Seeing a record's history needs `<kind>.edit` on that record (`pages.edit`, `posts.edit`): the plain key, not the owner rule, so an Author sees the history of their live post. Restoring needs what editing the record needs (`AccessControl::OwnerRule`: `edit`, and `publish` once the record has a `published_at`), exactly as a discard does, because a restore writes the rows through the same services. Publishing the result stays `publish`. A record outside the viewer's scope is not found (404), never forbidden. *Cost if wrong:* content removed on purpose from an older version is visible to every editor of the record; viewing could be raised to `publish` with one key.
- **R5. The whole record, every language.** A version is restored whole, as it was published (drafts spec D2: a record is published as a whole). The compare view shows one language at a time and names the languages in which anything differs. *Cost if wrong:* someone who wants only the German text back gets the English of that version too, and must restore and then retype or re-translate the English. Per-language restore needs the save services' per-language write and a per-language overview; it can follow without changing what a version is.
- **R6. Blocks are reconciled by id, as a discard does.** A block the version has and the rows have is rewritten where it differs and keeps its id and notes. A block deleted since comes back as a new block, without the notes it had (the confirmation says so when it applies). A block the version does not have is deleted with its notes (the confirmation names how many blocks go and how many of them carry notes). A block whose type has left the registry cannot be recreated: the restore is refused with the discard's message and nothing changes. A post whose type is switched off cannot be restored (`switched_off_refusal`, first, as for every post write).
- **R7. An image that is gone is left empty, and said.** A version holds library images by id. An image in force or pending is protected (`Media::UsageIndex`, role `published`); an older version's image may since have been moved to the trash or deleted. A restore puts back an image that is still in the library and not in the trash; one that is gone or in the trash is left empty (the hero, an image block's image, an image inside rich text stays a reference that renders nothing, as a rebuilt copy already does), and the confirmation and the toast say how many images could not be brought back. The image foreign keys stay NO ACTION; nothing writes an id that does not exist. Restoring an image needs what choosing one needs (`Media::Choosing`): someone without `media.upload` who would bring back a changed image is refused, as for a discard, with the refusal audited this time. *Cost if wrong:* a restored image block is empty and must be filled by hand; restoring the image from the trash first and then restoring the version brings it back.
- **R8. Tags go back by id; none is made.** As a discard: a post's tags come back as the ids the version holds, under their present names; a tag deleted since stays deleted and the toast says a tag could not be brought back. A list block's filter goes back to the tag the version recorded (`listed_tag_id`).
- **R9. What is not restored** is what a copy does not hold or what is immediate: the slug (the address), the place in the tree, whether and when the record is published, a post's content type and author, translation bookkeeping (whatever the save services leave when text is written back; an outdated mark is what the text now says), a post's related posts (in no copy). The history screen says the first three in one line.
- **R10. One audit event of its own.** `page.version_restored` / `post.version_restored` on the record, never coalesced, `snapshot: nil`, metadata `{ "number" => n, "images_missing" => k, "tags_missing" => t }` (the counts only when not zero). Audit sentence: "restored version 5 of Pricing". The save services it drives record their own `*.updated` and block events as for a discard. It is in `Editing::Signals::MAP` (other open editors refresh) and is a change in the dashboard's feed (it writes content), unlike `scheduled_change_cancelled`.
- **R11. Locks: the settings row, the record, the blocks.** One transaction, `lock_owner` first (`ContentTypes.hold!`, then the record's row), then the block services, which take the page's row again and then block rows; a step that fails rolls everything back. `ContentSpiel::LockOrder` checks it in test as for every write.
- **R12. Live editing.** A restore is refused while another tab holds any part of the record, by the controller (`Admin::EditingClaims`, as `refuse_discard_under_hold`); a call with no tab (the console) is not refused. It is not an autosave and carries no autosave base; the dialog's form carries the version number it showed and the record's `updated_at` as the dialog read it, which the service checks under the lock (a version cannot change, but the rows can: when the record's `updated_at` moved since the dialog read it, the restore is refused with "This page changed since you opened this. Look at the differences again." and nothing is written). Other open editors get the change by live refresh; a form holding unsaved text in another tab gets the stale refusal at its next save, as after a discard.
- **R13. Retention: every version is kept.** No pruning, no setting. Copies go with their record when it is purged from the trash (the dependent delete), as now. *Cost if wrong:* storage grows with every publish (a post's copy holds every language's body). A retention setting ("keep the last N, or N days") can come later; nothing here assumes all copies exist, because the first copies of 2026-10-11 already mean history has a start.
- **R14. Comparing.** Any two versions of one record can be compared, and any version with the **working version** (the rows as saved now, which may equal the copy in force). The comparison is `Publishing::Changes` generalised to two sides in the copy's stored form (the overview: fields, blocks changed / added / removed / moved, languages), plus a word diff per changed text, in one language at a time. Rich text is compared as its Markdown form (`RichText`), so a word diff reads as text; a change of formatting only says "Formatting changed", as the translation pair does.
- **R15. Where it lives: a History screen, not a rail tab.** `/<ADMIN_PATH>/pages/:id/history` and `/posts/:id/history`, under the editor's crumbs, reached from the editor's ⋯ menu ("History", beside "Activity") and from the record's Activity screen. *Why:* comparing needs width (two columns of text, word diffs); the rail is 360px and becomes a slide-over below 1200px of the editor; a separate screen has no live merge to stand inside, no autosave form near it and no hold. *Cost if wrong:* one more navigation away from the editor; a later rail tab can list the same versions and link into this screen.
- **R16. The restore is confirmed in a dialog that shows what will change.** The overview of the publish dialog (`ChangesOverviewComponent`), computed between the working version and the chosen version, plus the lines of R6 to R9 that apply, and one sentence saying it is not published. A restore of the version equal to the working rows says "Nothing to restore" and offers nothing.
- **R17. Not in this version:** autosave points as versions; restoring one language, one block or one field; a rendered preview of an old version (`Snapshot.rebuild` makes it possible: a later "Preview this version"); MCP tools for history; a history of a block alone, of an image, of a form; history in the Mind graph; retention settings. The roadmap's "Remove the rows fallback for a live record with no copy" stays its own piece: a record with no copy simply has no versions here. The two write-queue items left under "Closed by versioning" (a lost answer retried on the old base; an edit typed between a header action's submit and its redirect) are not closed by copies and move to "The write queue and Turbo" in the roadmap.

## 3. What a person sees

The look is the design agent's, from the Design System cards made for this work (§10); this says what each screen holds.

**The editor's ⋯ menu** gains "History" for a viewer who may see it (R4), on a page and a post, draft or not.

**The History screen** (one per record): the record's title and its state chip in a head row, with "Back to editor". Two parts, as the lists' rail (`.admin-list-rail`):

- **The versions**, newest first: at the top **"Working version"** (the rows), with "Unpublished changes" when the mark is set; then each copy: "Version 7", the time it went or goes live (`live_at`), who published it (`created_by`, or "Kept when history began"), and a chip: **"On the site"** for the copy in force of a visible record, **"Scheduled for 12 Oct, 09:00"** for a pending copy, none for older ones. A version that was a scheduled change says so. 50 rows a page with a pager, as every list. Choosing a row shows its comparison.
- **The comparison**: "Version 5 compared with" a select: **the version before it** (the default: what that publish changed), **the working version**, or any other version by number. Under it the language select (the default language first; a language with differences says so) and the overview: the record's changed fields, each with its word diff; then the blocks in reading order, each changed (with its diff), added, removed or moved, by its type and summary, as the publish dialog names them. "No differences" when there are none. One primary action: **"Restore version 5…"**, offered when the viewer may restore (R4) and the version differs from the working version.

A record with no versions: "No versions yet. A version is kept each time this page is published."

**The restore dialog**: "Restore version 5?"; "The working version of Pricing becomes version 5 as it was published on 3 Nov 2026. It is not published: the site keeps showing version 7 until you publish the changes." Then the overview of what changes in the working version, and the lines that apply: blocks that come back without their notes; blocks that go, and how many carry notes; images that are no longer in the library; tags that no longer exist; a change scheduled for a time, which stays scheduled. Footer: Cancel (tertiary), **Restore version 5** (primary). After it: the editor, with the toast "Version 5 restored as unpublished changes. Publish changes… puts it on the site." (or for a draft, "Version 5 restored."), plus the missing-images or tags sentence when it applies.

**Publish now (D9).** "Publish now" on a scheduled record opens a dialog instead of acting at once (one more press on a rare and deliberate act; *my call*, because the button cannot know at page load whether changes will be saved before it is pressed). The dialog asks the server when it opens (through the write queue, so every save of the tab has landed):
- No changes since the schedule: "Publish Pricing now? It goes live now instead of 12 Oct, 09:00, as it was scheduled." Confirm: **Publish now**.
- Changes saved since: a choice of two, the first chosen: **"Publish the scheduled version"**, "What was scheduled goes live now. Your later changes stay unpublished."; **"Publish with the later changes"**, "Everything saved so far goes live now, the later changes included." The confirm button repeats which one ("Publish the scheduled version" / "Publish with the later changes"), so the press says what goes live. The overview of the later changes is behind a "What changed since" disclosure.

## 4. The model

**`Publishing::Changes`** takes two sides, each in the stored form of a copy (`{ "fields", "tags", "blocks" }`): the existing `Changes.of(record)` and `new(record, version)` keep their meaning (rows against a copy), and a new constructor compares two copies, or a copy and the rows, in either order (`Changes.between(record, from:, to:)`, where each side is a `Version` or `:working`). The stand-ins (a block back under a new id is the block it was) and the empty-document equivalence hold for both. A version older than the copy shape it compares with is no case: every copy has one shape (`COPY_MIGRATIONS`).

**`Publishing::TextDiff`** (a value, `app/values/publishing/`): for one changed field or block, in one language, the before and after text to word-diff (plain columns as they are; a rich-text document by its Markdown form; settings by their schema labels and values). `Admin::Ui::WordDiffComponent` draws it.

**`Publishing::History`** (a query, `app/queries/publishing/history.rb`): a record's versions, newest first, paged, with their publisher read in the same statement, and which one is in force and which pending (`Publishing.current`, `Publishing.pending`).

**`Publishing::RestoreVersion`** (a service): R3, R6 to R12. It shares the reconciliation of `DiscardChanges` (the order: what the target lacks first, sections before their blocks, places last; only what differs is written) through one module both include (`Publishing::ReturnTo`), so a discard is "return to the copy in force, and withdraw a pending one" and a restore is "return to version N, and withdraw nothing". The images rule (R7) and the missing-tags count are part of the shared code, so a discard of a copy whose image is gone stops failing too (today "a later Discard changes… of that page can fail on it"). It records R10's event, and leaves the mark as the save services leave it (set when the rows now differ from the copy in force).

**`Publishing::PublishNow`** (a service, D9): under the lock, with `with_changes: false` it is what `Pages::Save` / `Posts::Save` with `published: true` does today; with `with_changes: true` it takes a copy of the rows through `PublishChanges` with the time now and then sets `published_at` to now, in one transaction, so the site never shows the scheduled version first. It checks the schedule shown (`ShownSchedule`) as the publish dialog does. The controllers' `publish` actions call it.

## 5. CS Studio

- `Admin::PageHistoryController` and `Admin::PostHistoryController` (or one `Admin::HistoryController` with a record kind, as `Admin::EditingController` does): `show` (the screen: versions and comparison, `from`, `to` and `locale` in the address, a GET), `restore` (POST, `_top`, behind `admin--saving--queue-hold`; the editor's address afterwards) and the dialog's own read of what a restore changes, a POST through the write queue as the publish dialog's.
- Components in `app/components/admin/history_screen/` (the screen's: the version list, a row, the comparison, a block's diff, the restore dialog). The publish-now dialog joins the editor's dialogs in `app/components/admin/editor_screen/`, drawn once with the page, outside every replaceable part (the publishing rule); its choice is asked when it opens, by `PublishingController#changes` with a part of its own.
- The ⋯ menu's item and the Activity screen's link are drawn from the right of R4.
- Every string is a key (`config/locales/admin/history_screen.en.yml`, the editor's in `editor_screen.en.yml`); the audit sentence in `Audit::Sentence`.

## 6. Rules added

- A version is a published copy; nothing writes `published_versions` for history (no copy is taken by a restore). `test/lib/publishing_guards_test.rb`'s writers stay as they are.
- `RestoreVersion` and `DiscardChanges` share one reconciliation; neither writes a row around the save services.
- A restore never changes `published_at`, the slug, the tree, or a copy.
- The Publish now dialog stands outside the replaced parts, as the other publishing dialogs.

## 11. Settled after the builder check

- **What Publish now writes.** A scheduled record's first copy is in force from the moment it was taken and hidden only by `published_at` (`docs/reference/publishing.md`, "The order of copies"). "Publish the scheduled version" therefore sets `published_at` to now and writes no copy, as today. "Publish with the later changes" takes a new copy of the rows with `live_at` now through `PublishChanges` (which, as always, withdraws a pending change copy that never was public) and then sets `published_at` to now, in one transaction; the scheduled copy stays as an older version that never was on the site, and the History list shows it as any older version. No copy is rewritten.
- **"Changes since the schedule"** is the mark (`Publishing::State#changes?`): on a scheduled record the copy in force is the scheduled one, so the mark already means "saved after the schedule". The dialog's read compares the rows with that copy (`Changes.of`) for its disclosure.
- **The working version** is always the list's first row; when it equals the copy in force it reads "Same as version 7". Restore is offered for a version that differs from the rows, and only then.
- **Translation bookkeeping after a restore** is what the save services leave, as for a discard: writing the default language's text back marks a translation outdated where the original moved. A test pins it, as `discard_changes_test.rb` does.
- **The restore form** is a `_top` form behind `admin--saving--queue-hold`, as the discard's; the dialog's read of what will change is a POST through the write queue, as the publish dialog's.
- **Stand-ins between two copies** need no rows: `Changes.stand_ins` pairs two stored block lists by content and place, so two old versions compare as a version and the rows do.
- **Diffs are computed on the server**, by `Translations::SourceDiff`'s limits (`MAX_WORDS`, `MAX_CELLS`), drawn by `Admin::Ui::WordDiffComponent`; past the limit a field or block says "The text is too long to compare word by word." A Diagram block's drawing and a Code block's source are named as changed with no word diff of the drawing (the source is diffed as text, within the limit).
