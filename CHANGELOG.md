# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Added

- Passkeys for CS Studio. A person signs in with a passkey alone ("Sign in with a passkey" on the sign-in page, no email first), or with their password followed by a passkey or a recovery code. At an IP address (a `PUBLIC_HOST` that is an IP, development at 127.0.0.1) no passkey is offered: a passkey belongs to a domain, and WebAuthn refuses an IP.
- The second step of a sign-in, a page of its own: after a right password for an account that holds a passkey or unused recovery codes, one of its passkeys or one of its codes within five minutes, "Start over" to go back, and from the code view "Use your passkey" back to the passkey. A wrong code and a used one get the same sentence, and every wrong proof counts in the account's sign-in bucket as a wrong password does.
- Recovery codes: ten single-use codes (`XXXX-XXXX`), a backup for passkeys, made with a person's first passkey and with Generate… or Regenerate… on Your account (offered, and allowed, only to a person who holds a passkey), shown once with Copy, Download and "I have saved these codes", stored only as HMACs.
- Your account's Passkeys card (each passkey's name, when it was added and last used, Rename, Remove…, "Add a passkey…") and Recovery codes card (how many are left, when they were made). Removing the last passkey of a person no policy holds to one deletes their recovery codes too, and they sign in with their password again.
- Confirm it's you, a dialog in CS Studio (not the second step): adding or removing a passkey, its registration options and making recovery codes ask for the password or a passkey again when the sign-in, or the last confirmation, is more than ten minutes old. A wrong password counts in the account's bucket.
- Settings → Sign-in, for an owner alone: who must sign in with a passkey, nobody, owners and admins (whoever may manage users, groups or API tokens) or everyone, with how many people it covers who have none yet. Unset, it is owners and admins in production and nobody in development and test.
- Enrolment: a person the policy covers who has no passkey is sent, at their next sign-in, to a page that asks for one before anything else of CS Studio or the preview opens; a session already open carries on.
- The setup wizard's passkey step, after the owner's account (ten steps now): under production's default policy the founding owner adds a passkey before any later step; with `nobody` it can be skipped; at an IP address it says why and goes on.
- The console's way back for a person who lost every passkey and every code: the password command with `remove_passkeys: true` deletes their passkeys and codes, records `passkey.removed` and ends their sessions (`docs/reference/hosting.md`, "The reset mail").
- The mails "A passkey was added to your account on <site>" and "A recovery code was used on <site>" (when, the passkey's name or the address and the codes left, and a button to Your account), sent only while mail works.
- Audit events, all Security events but a rename, under "Sign-in" in the audit log's Action filter: `passkey.added`, `passkey.renamed`, `passkey.removed`, `recovery_codes.generated`, `recovery_code.used`, `session.second_factor_failed` (no actor) and `session.confirmation_failed`.
- A per-address rate limit on the passkey options (sign-in, the second step and Confirm it's you), counted apart from the proofs: 30 a minute, past which the options answer a bare 429 and no passkey is offered until the minute ends.
- Signing out deletes every passkey challenge's cookie (`cs_passkey_<purpose>`), so none is left for the next person on the browser.
- Spans `auth.passkey`, `auth.recovery_code`, `auth.second_factor`, `auth.confirmation` and `auth.account_mail`, and the attributes `auth.passkey.ceremony`, `auth.policy`, `auth.second_factor.required` and `auth.mail.kind` (`docs/reference/hosting.md`, "Telemetry", with a Honeycomb query for the second step's failures).
- `SSO_ISSUER`, `SSO_CLIENT_ID`, `SSO_CLIENT_SECRET` (a secret), `SSO_NAME` and `SSO_REQUIRED` (optional; the first four together make one OpenID Connect provider, some but not all is none and production's boot names the missing ones). `SSO_REQUIRED` without a whole provider, with a value other than `1`, or an `SSO_ISSUER` that is no `https` URL with a host stops the boot in every environment.
- Single sign-on on the sign-in page: with a provider, "Sign in with %{provider}" under the password form, after "or" and before the passkey's button, which takes the person to the provider and back signed in. With `SSO_REQUIRED` the page offers the provider alone, as its one primary, with a small "Owner sign-in" link to the owner path (`?owner=1`): the password form and the passkey for owners, and "Sign in with %{provider} instead". Anyone may open the owner path; a non-owner who signs in there is refused as for a wrong password. A gem's sign-in method (`SignInMethods`) is a button beside the provider's, under `SSO_REQUIRED` too. A sign-in refused or left unfinished at the provider comes back to the page with one sentence for each.
- `ADMIN_HOST`: CS Studio on a host of its own. The reset mail, the lock-out mail's reset request link, the invitation, and the account link of the passkey-added and recovery-code-used mails link to it, the API tokens screen names the MCP address on it, and System status shows a "CS Studio address" row.

### Changed

- On upgrading, owners and admins are asked for a passkey: a production site that has never chosen who must sign in with one asks owners and admins, so at their next sign-in each of them without a passkey is sent to enrolment and cannot open CS Studio until they add one. To choose otherwise, set Settings → Sign-in before then, or from the console (`docs/reference/hosting.md`, "The reset mail": `bin/rails runner 'p Settings::Save.call(SiteSetting.instance, attributes: { passkey_policy: "nobody" }).ok?'`).
- A password alone no longer signs in an account that holds a passkey or an unused recovery code: a right password goes on to the second step, and the known-device cookie `cs_known_device` is written only once a sign-in is whole.
- `session.created` carries `{ "method" }` when a person signed in with a passkey or a recovery code, and reads "signed in with a passkey" or "signed in with a recovery code"; a password's reads "signed in".
- While setup is pending only a page load (a GET or HEAD asking for HTML that is no prefetch) is sent to `/<ADMIN_PATH>/setup`; any other request is answered 404 without loading the session, so a client that does not ask for HTML gets 404 for `/`.
- Settings has eight sections: Sign-in stands after Mail, for an owner alone.
- `filter_parameters` holds `credential` (exactly) and `recovery_code`.

### Breaking

- The MCP endpoint is now `POST /<ADMIN_PATH>/mcp` (`/cs-studio/mcp` by default). `/mcp` is an ordinary public page path.
- The preview is now at `/<ADMIN_PATH>/preview/...`, the setup wizard at `/<ADMIN_PATH>/setup`, and Action Cable at `/<ADMIN_PATH>/cable`. The old addresses are ordinary public page paths, with no redirect. A guard in front of `/<ADMIN_PATH>` (Cloudflare Access, a WAF rule) now covers the whole backend; an MCP client behind Cloudflare Access needs a service token.
- `ADMIN_PATH` may now be `preview`, `setup`, `mcp` or `cable`, and a top-level page with one of those slugs is now reachable.
- `ADMIN_PATH` may no longer be `forms`, the first segment of the public form send.
- The new top-level names `Passkey`, `RecoveryCode`, `Passkeys`, `RecoveryCodes` and `PasskeyChallenges` refuse a gem's `ContentSpiel::Plugins` hook owner of the same name (`passkey`, `recovery_code`, `passkeys`, `recovery_codes`, `passkey_challenges`) at boot. A gem with such an owner renames it.
- A new top-level name, `HostRouteConstraint` (`app/constraints/host_route_constraint.rb`): a gem whose hook owner has that name is refused at boot.
- A new top-level name, `SignInMethods` (`app/models/sign_in_methods.rb`): a gem whose hook owner is `sign_in_methods` is refused at boot.
- A new top-level name, `SignInRules` (`app/models/sign_in_rules.rb`): a gem whose hook owner is `sign_in_rules` is refused at boot.

### Removed

- Action Mailbox and its routes (the ingresses and the conductor under `/rails/conductor/action_mailbox/`).
- Active Storage's direct uploads: `POST /rails/active_storage/direct_uploads` and `PUT /rails/active_storage/disk/:encoded_token` answer 404 to everyone, signed in or not. Every upload goes through CS Studio.
- Turbo's native-app historical-location routes, which the public catch-all already shadowed.
- The concern `ActiveStorageWriteAuthentication`, replaced by `ActiveStorageWriteRefusal`, which answers 404 to everyone.

### Environment

- `ADMIN_HOST` (optional): the address, with its scheme, of a host that serves CS Studio alone, for a backend behind a DMZ. With it, every address under `/<ADMIN_PATH>/` answers only on that host and the public site does not answer on it; `/up` and images answer on both. It needs `PUBLIC_HOST`, another host name and the same scheme, or production does not start (`docs/reference/hosting.md`, "Behind a DMZ"). Passkeys then belong to the admin host's registrable domain, so turning it on or off, or changing it, orphans every passkey already made unless the old and the new host share a registrable domain: people sign in with their password and a recovery code (or are let back in from the console with `remove_passkeys: true`) and add a passkey again.

- `20261112090000_create_passkeys_and_recovery_codes`: the tables `passkeys` (a person's WebAuthn credentials: the credential id, unique, the public key, the sign count, a name, the transports and the last use) and `recovery_codes` (each code's HMAC, unique per account, with when it was made and used), and `users.webauthn_id` (unique, null until the account's first passkey).
- `20261112090100_add_passkey_policy_and_confirmation`: `sessions.confirmed_at` (null for a session open before it, which is asked to confirm once) and `sessions.enrolment_due` (not null, false), and `site_settings.passkey_policy` (null: the environment's default).
- `20261112090200_create_sign_in_spent_challenges`: the table `sign_in_spent_challenges` (the HMAC of each answered passkey challenge, unique, and when it expires, indexed), which the hourly session sweep empties of expired rows.
- `20261113090000_add_sso_link_to_users`: `users.sso_issuer`, `sso_subject` and `sso_linked_at` (null: not linked to a single sign-on provider), a unique index on the issuer and subject together, and a CHECK that the two are both null or both set.
- `20261113090100_add_provider_check_to_sessions`: `sessions.provider_token` (the provider's refresh token, encrypted, null) and `provider_checked_at` (null: no provider to ask again).

### For a gem or an outside caller

- `Session.start(user:, method:, user_agent:, ip_address:, enrolment_due: false, at:)`: a session is confirmed at its start (`confirmed_at`, `Session#confirm!`, `#confirmed_recently?`, `Session::CONFIRM_WINDOW`), and `enrolment_due` holds it at enrolment.
- `SettingsSections.for(owner: false) { |permission| … }`: a section with `owner_only: true` (`SettingsSections::Section#owner_only`, Sign-in) is offered to an owner alone; a caller that does not say `owner:` offers it to nobody.
- `SignIn::OUTCOMES` gains `second_factor_required`, an outcome of `SignIn::PasswordAttempt` with the account, and `SignIn::SECOND_STEP_LIFETIME` is 5 minutes.
- `SignIn::Throttle#reserve_address(address:)` and `#release_address(address:)`: the wide address bucket's half of a reservation, for an attempt that names no account (a passkey sign-in), and the count a verified passkey takes back; `#await_second_proof(reservation, identifier:, address:, device: nil)`, what a right password owing a second proof gives back (the address's counts, and a lock its reservation wrote) while its run's count stands.
- The passkey objects of `SignIn`, which know no session, cookie, route or mail: `SignIn::RelyingParty.for(address:, request_origin:, name:)` (nil at an IP), `SignIn::PasskeyRegistration.new(relying_party:)` with `#options(user_handle:, user_name:, display_name:, exclude: [])` and `#verify(json, challenge:)`, `SignIn::PasskeyCheck.new(relying_party:)` with `#options(allow: [])` and `#verify(json, challenge:, credentials:, ceremony:)`, and `SignIn::SpentChallenge.spend!(digest, expires_at:)`; and `SignIn::Proof.new(account:, identifier:, address:, span:, device: nil)` with `#recovery_code(text)`, `#password(text)` and `#passkey(json, challenge:, check:, ceremony:)`, a second proof counted in the account's bucket.
- `Users::UpdatePassword.call(user, …, remove_passkeys: false)`: `true` from the console alone.
- `Admin::Ui::RecoveryCodesComponent.new(codes:, frame: :dialog | :page, done_href: nil, id: "recovery-codes")` and `Admin::Ui::ConfirmIdentityDialogComponent.new(passkey:, url_options: {})`, shared components with their contracts (`docs/reference/design-system.md`, "Shared components").
- The product layout loads CS Studio's entry point (`admin`) in place of `application` for a page that says `content_for :entry_point, "admin"`, as the setup layout does: the enrolment page.
- Added the hook `ContentSpiel::Plugins.sign_in_methods { |methods| methods.register "owner/name", label:, path:, recheck: nil, passkey_policy: :applies }`, for a gem's way to sign in to CS Studio. It is kept and replayed into `SignInMethods` on every boot and development reload, under the block types' owner rules (a bare name, a second owner in one call, an owner another hook has and an owner named like one of the CMS's top-level constants are refused, naming the place of the call). `label:` is an I18n key of the gem's, `path:` a route helper on `main_app` that the button POSTs to, `recheck:` an optional object answering `call(session)` with `:ok`, `:revoked` or `:unavailable` (a method with none is never asked again and its sessions keep no provider token), and `passkey_policy:` is `:applies` or `:exempt` (the method's provider is the second factor, as SSO's is). The hook registers the method and nothing else: it never signs in an owner account (owners keep their own way in), creates no account (a method finds an invited account), records nothing in the audit log but `session.created`, and is neither hidden nor refused by `SSO_REQUIRED`. `Session::METHODS + SignInMethods.names` are the methods a session may be made with.


## 0.3.0 (2026-11-11)

### Environment

- `TRUSTED_PROXIES` (optional): a CDN's ranges, comma-separated, added to the proxies Rails believes about whose request it is, so that behind Cloudflare each visitor counts as themselves to sign-in's limits, the bot check's trigger and the forms' send limit, and not as one of Cloudflare's few edge addresses. An entry that is not an address or a range stops the boot and names it. Behind Cloudflare a site sets it to Cloudflare's published ranges AND kamal-proxy's `proxy.forward_headers: true` (with `ssl: true` kamal-proxy otherwise replaces `X-Forwarded-For` with the edge's own), which `config/deploy.yml` and a new site's `config/deploy.yml` now carry as a commented line. System status's new "Visitor address" row warns while Cloudflare's `CF-Connecting-IP` differs from the address Rails reads (`docs/reference/hosting.md`, "The visitor's address behind a proxy").
- `TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY` and `TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY` (optional, both or neither; the secret is a secret): Cloudflare Turnstile's keys, which turn on sign-in's bot check. One without the other is no check, and production's boot names the missing one. With both, the sign-in page is always loaded whole (not through Turbo) under the site's policy with `https://challenges.cloudflare.com` added to `frame-src`.
- `LEAKED_PASSWORD_CHECK` (optional): `off` or `on`, the check of a new password against Have I Been Pwned's list of leaked passwords. Unset, it is ON in production and off in development and test, so a production site now calls `https://api.pwnedpasswords.com` when a person sets a password (five characters of the password's SHA-1 leave the server, never the password). A site that cannot reach it accepts the password and logs one line.

### Added

- Sign-in counts the attempts for one email, whether or not an account has it, before the password is checked: the fifth closes the email for 1 minute, each later one doubles it up to 1 hour, a right password clears the run, and a run is forgotten 24 hours after its first attempt. While it is closed the panel says "Try again in a few minutes.", for a known and an unknown email alike.
- A browser that has signed in to an account keeps signing in to it while a bot holds the account's email closed: a right password writes the encrypted cookie `cs_known_device` (HttpOnly, SameSite Lax, Secure over HTTPS, 90 days; a random id and the HMACs of up to five accounts, never an email), and an attempt for an account the cookie names is counted in a bucket of its own, with the same figures, that the account's closing does not touch. The address limits and the bot check apply to it as to anyone. A cookie that does not open, or names another account, is ignored.
- A new password, set through the reset, Your account, the console's password command or the Users editor, opens sign-in for that account at once in every browser: the email's bucket and each known browser's, one closed by the person's own failures included.
- Sign-in closes an address for the rest of the hour after 50 attempts from it in an hour, whatever emails it tried ("Try again later."). An IPv6 address counts by its /64. The store holds neither emails nor addresses, only their HMACs.
- A bot check on sign-in (Cloudflare Turnstile, verified on the server for this site's host and the sign-in action), shown after 3 attempts from the address or with the email in 15 minutes. An attempt without its answer checks no password and counts nothing ("Complete the check, then sign in again."). When Cloudflare cannot be reached, or refuses for a reason of the site's (a wrong secret), the check counts as passed and one line says why.
- A password a person sets (the setup wizard, an invitation, the reset, Your account, the Users editor, the console's `Users::UpdatePassword`) is refused when Have I Been Pwned lists it: "This password is in a list of leaked passwords, so it isn't safe to use. Choose another one." Asked only once the password is otherwise valid; a lookup that cannot be made (5 seconds at most) accepts it.
- The mail "Signing in to <site> is paused" to an account whose email a run of failed sign-ins closed, at most once an hour: how long, when, from which address, and a button to the reset request page (no reset link).
- The audit event `session.locked`, one per bucket a failed sign-in closed (an email's, a known browser's or an address's), with no actor and no subject, its bucket, seconds and back-off level in its metadata, and the email for an email's. A Security event: "Sign-in to one account paused after repeated failures", "Sign-in to one account from one browser paused after repeated failures", "Sign-in from one address paused after repeated failures".
- The CMS's own spans, for sign-in: `auth.sign_in`, `auth.throttle`, `auth.bot_check`, `auth.leaked_password` and `auth.lockout_mail`, each a child of the request's, with fixed words only (`Telemetry::SPANS` and `ATTRIBUTES`; `docs/reference/hosting.md`, "Telemetry", has the Honeycomb queries). The two outside calls are not traced by the HTTP instrumentation, whose span would record the lookup's path.
- Sessions have limits: idle after 12 hours unseen, and 14 days after sign-in whatever is done (`Session::IDLE_TIMEOUT`, `ABSOLUTE_TIMEOUT`). A request or a cable connection that meets an expired session ends it; `Sessions::SweepJob` ends the ones nobody met, hourly. The sign-in page says "Your session ended. Sign in again." to the browser it ended in, and to no other.
- Your account has a Sessions card: every live session with its browser and system (`SessionDevice`, from the new `useragent` gem), address, last activity and how its person signed in, an End on each other one, and "Sign out everywhere else".
- The user editor has a Sessions zone for an owner: "End all sessions" for another user (`Sessions::EndAll`, not offered on the owner's own account).
- `session.ended` in the audit log (Security), one event per act with a count and a reason (`expired_idle`, `expired_absolute`, `ended_by_self`, `ended_by_owner`, `password_changed`), and for an expiry the timeout it ended by (`after_hours`, `after_days`), which its sentence reads; sign-out stays `session.destroyed`.
- Spans `auth.session` (a session a request ended for expiry; a cable connection's expiry is audited only) and `auth.session_sweep`, with `auth.session.reason` and `auth.session.count` (`docs/reference/hosting.md`, "Telemetry").

### Changed

- The `session_id` cookie is no longer permanent: it ends with the session, 14 days after sign-in. A session records when it was last seen, when it ends and how its person signed in (`Session.start`, `Session::METHODS`).
- A sign-in refused for any reason answers the same for an email that has an account and one that does not, in its status, redirect, sentence, cookies, statements and password hashing.
- A reset request looks up nobody in the request: it queues `PasswordResetRequestJob` for every address typed, which finds the account and queues the reset mail, so a known and an unknown address take the same time. The job's arguments (the address typed) are not logged.
- Sign-in's per-address limit (10 attempts in 3 minutes, every attempt counted) counts in the sign-in throttle's store (`SignIn.store`, Solid Cache in production as before, a memory store in tests, where it now trips), under an HMAC of the address and an IPv6 address by its /64, not the address itself, and answers inside an `auth.sign_in` span.
- CS Studio's segmented controls are one component with one look: the posts list's scope, the media library's and the picker's segments, Translations' type, the Trash's types, a form's Form | Submissions, Waiting for you's and the Notes screen's filters, a `control: :segmented` select (the Note block's kind), Settings' mail security and image format, the form builder's "Shown as" and the New form dialog's start. The chosen segment is the accent tint with accent words and a 1px accent edge where it was ink with paper words; the track has no dividers, is 38px tall everywhere (34px outside a filter bar before), wraps whole segments at a narrow width, and a count is always drawn in tabular figures, 0 included. The keyboard is as before: a link is a Tab stop, a radio group one Tab stop the arrows move along (#237).
- The language tabs' chosen language is underlined in the accent, as a rail tab is, where it was ink (#237).
- The channel chip (the audit log's rows, the dashboard's token-made changes) is a badge at the badge's one height, 2px taller than it was (#237).
- A rail tab with a count, and a rail of one tab's heading, is read out as its label and its count apart ("Notes 3", "Notes 2 new"), where a screen reader was handed them run together (#237).
- An outdated language's mark on the language tabs is ↻ in warn ink, as on a translation chip and under a language's progress bar, where it was a solid warn dot; a screen reader still hears "<language>: outdated" (#237).
- A danger notice (a refusal, a broken link, a sign-in page's error) has a 1px danger edge, as a form's error summary has; warn kept its edge, and ok and neutral have none (#237).
- A switch that is on in a form someone else is editing keeps its accent track, where it was drawn in meta as a switch nobody may change (#237).
- A password change's endings of sessions are audited: one `session.ended` (`password_changed`) with the count, when any session ended.

## 0.2.0 (2026-11-10)

### Breaking

- Every `ContentSpiel::Plugins` hook (`block_types`, `form_field_types`, `form_destinations`, `studio_modules`) refuses an owner named like one of the CMS's own top-level constants, so `media/album`, `notes/rating`, `publishing/crm` or `audit/reports` now stops the boot with an `ArgumentError` naming the constant and the place of the hook's call (on a development reload it is the theme's refusal, or for a module hook a log line, as any refused hook is). A gem's code lives in a module named after its owner, which would reopen the CMS's. The names are everything at the top of the application's autoload roots as the autoloader names it (`app/models`, `app/services`, `app/queries`, `app/values`, `app/constraints`, `app/components`, `app/controllers`, `app/helpers`, `app/jobs`, `app/mailers`, `app/channels`, `app/mcp`, `app/sanitizers` and `lib`, so `blocks`, `admin`, `fields`, `public` and `mcp` are refused too); a folder the autoloader ignores (`lib/tasks`) is none, and the owner is compared as the application's inflector camelizes it and plainly. A gem whose owner is refused renames it, and with it its keys, tokens and stored type names, which name the owner. The list grows with the CMS: a later release that adds a top-level name can refuse an installed gem's owner, and names that name under Breaking.

### For a gem or an outside caller

- Added `Blocks::Contract`: a gem's block or form component may include it and declare, with `contract(...)`, what it promises a visitor (accessibility, motion, content edges, widths, its interface, its `public.*` words and its limits). It adds a class method and changes nothing a template can call or a site draws. `bin/rails generate block_type` writes the include and a declaration for the component it generates. Nothing else changes for a site.
- SortableJS is no longer preloaded in CS Studio; a script that imports `sortablejs` fetches it on demand (the editor, the page tree, Settings' language rows and a groups field's rows do).
- For a gem's block type: `t.preview ->(block, locale:) { ContentModel::Preview.new(heading: …, text: …, mono: false) }` declares the heading and the few words CS Studio's tile shows of a closed block, as plain text (the text is cut at 240 characters, the heading at 80). It is optional: a type that declares none is drawn from its `summary`, and a callable that is not a callable taking the block and `locale:` is refused at registration. No public template or theme reads it.
- For a gem's block type: `t.icon "name", family: :text` (one of `text media code form lists structure`) names the hue of the chip CS Studio draws the icon on; a type that names none is drawn on the neutral chip, and any other value is refused at registration. The chip is CS Studio's only: no public page draws it.
- Added two hooks beside `ContentSpiel::Plugins.block_types`: `form_field_types` (a form field type's members, `owner/name`) and `form_destinations` (a destination's label, deliverer and settings block, `owner/name`), with the block types' owner rules and replayed after them (`docs/reference/content-blocks-and-types.md`, "Form field types and destinations from outside"). A destination's deliverer answers `deliver(delivery, final_attempt:)`.
- `StudioModules.register` requires `family:`, one of `text media code form lists structure people`: a CS Studio module's icon stands on that hue's chip in the sidebar. A module that names none raises Ruby's missing keyword at boot, and `:neutral`, `:accent` or any other value is refused at registration. The current module's chip keeps its family; only its row is the accent tint.
- Added the hook `ContentSpiel::Plugins.studio_modules { |modules| modules.register "owner/name", …; modules.badge("owner/name") { |viewer:| … } }`, for a gem's module in CS Studio's sidebar and ⌘K: its block is handed `register` and `badge` with `StudioModules`' own arguments, kept, and replayed after the built-in modules on every boot and development reload. A module a gem registered on `StudioModules` itself was emptied by the application's `reset!` at boot in every environment (from its own `to_prepare`) or at the first development reload (from `after_initialize`); the hook is the way to register one. Its name is `owner/name`, under the block types' owner rules (a bare name is refused), and is the key of its label, `admin.modules.<owner/name>`, which the gem's locale file gives; `badge` sets the count of a module the same hook call registered and refuses any other, a built-in's included; a hook that raises stops the boot naming its place, and on a development reload is logged and leaves the built-in modules registered.

Only one given anew under an unknown name is refused.
